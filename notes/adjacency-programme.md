# Adjacency programme: complete a diagram others already use

## Purpose

The programme does not reframe the project around an adjacent school. It
selects a result an adjacent community already treats as important and adds a
missing causal, BV, charge, or nonlinear layer that can be checked without
accepting the \(D\)-quotient.

Every bridge must have the form

\[
\boxed{
\text{established external structure}
+\text{one certified missing layer from this project}.
}
\]

An external comparison is not complete merely because the notations have been
matched. It must reproduce one external benchmark, state one new theorem or
obstruction in the external variables, and expose a small verifier or fixture
that does not depend on the rest of this repository.

Use [`adjacency-bridge-note-template.md`](adjacency-bridge-note-template.md)
for every outward-facing two-page note.

would compare unlike notions of physical state.

## A1. Cyclic Green-homotopy transfer

### External object

Green-hyperbolic complexes with retarded and advanced Green homotopies, causal
quasi-isomorphisms, and compatible Poisson structures; and conformal
tractor/BGG detour complexes with formal self-adjointness.

### Result to add

Prove, under hypotheses stated independently of conformal gravity, that a
cyclic support-preserving differential contraction from a Green-hyperbolic
parent transfers advanced and retarded Green homotopies to the reduced
complex. Prove the support identities, chain identities, formal adjoints, and
equality up to homotopy of the transferred Green and current pairings.

The theorem must state exactly what changes when the reduction uses curved
homological perturbation, finite-order support-local shears, or a companion
operator rather than a same-bundle Green inverse.

### Paper-shaped output

1. abstract transfer theorem;
2. a toy finite-rank or de Rham-type example;
3. the \(15/60/60/15\to4/9/9/4\) tractor/detour transfer;
4. the Weyl-gravity causal complex as the large application;
5. coefficient tables and the full verifier in appendices or an artifact.

The adjacent consequence is that Lorentzian causal propagation can belong to
the complete detour complex even when the isolated Bach operator has no
canonical scalar-principal same-bundle factorization.

### Extension gate

After the theorem is stable, test one conformal spin-three, conformal
gravitino, or mixed-field detour system on a Bach-flat background. Select one
system with a finite explicit tractor parent; do not begin a higher-spin tower.

## A2. Linearization stability with global charge fibres

### External object

Taub/Fischer--Marsden--Moncrief linearization stability: a linearized solution
on a compact symmetric background may fail to be tangent to an exact solution
because its quadratic source pairs nontrivially with an adjoint cokernel.

### Result to add

Construct a certified bilinear

\[
\mathfrak O:
H^0_{\mathrm{lin}}\times H^0_{\mathrm{lin}}
\longrightarrow \operatorname{coker}L
\]

for the common Einstein--Maxwell/Weyl--Maxwell background. Classify its
frequency, angular-momentum, polarization, and electric/magnetic charge
selection rules. State the result over charge fibres:

\[
\text{fixed global charge: possible obstruction},\qquad
\text{allowed charge variation: specified directions removed}.
\]

The current radion, duality, photon, graviton, and null fixtures are examples
and regression tests, not substitutes for the harmonic theorem.

### Paper-shaped output

The smallest standalone paper needs one background, the full linearized
domain, the obstruction bilinear, an adjoint-cokernel witness, the fixed-charge
no-go, and an explicit extension when the matching charge variation is
allowed. It should explain which part is ordinary linearization instability
and which part arises from embedding a second-order Einstein sector in a
fourth-order theory.

## A3. Coefficient-bearing quantum anomaly bridge

### External object

The type-A/type-B four-dimensional Weyl anomaly, its BRST/descent
classification, heat-kernel or determinant coefficient calculations, and
Riegert/Wess--Zumino trivializations in an extended field space.

### Result to add

Compute the \(a,c\) coefficients by two independent paths:

1. the generated BV/descent quotient and QME ledger;
2. a heat-kernel, determinant, or index calculation with matched zero-mode,
   measure, and normalization conventions.

Then construct the coefficient-bearing map from the local classes
\([\omega C^2]\) and \([\omega E_4]\) to the actual \(D\)-Cartan defect. A
nonzero trace-anomaly coefficient is not by itself a nonzero \(D\)-anomaly.
Track antifield-dependent classes, parity-odd classes, counterterms, boundary
terms, and the lifecycle states `CLASSIFIED`, `COEFFICIENT_COMPUTED`,
`QME_RESTORED`, and `RESIDUAL_TRANSFERRED` separately.

### External payoff

The output should say in familiar anomaly language which coefficients are
universal, which field contents cancel them, whether a Wess--Zumino extension
trivializes the QME or \(D\)-defect, and what new cohomology that extension
introduces. Presentation-independent agreement is the credibility test for
the symbolic infrastructure.

## A4. Lorentzian complement to Einstein from conformal gravity

### External object

Boundary conditions selecting an Einstein branch of conformal gravity in
EAdS/dS, action and curvature decompositions of that relation, and twistor
embeddings used to extract Einstein amplitudes.

### Result to add

Keep three claims separate:

| Boundary-selected question | Causal question added here |
|---|---|
| Is the Einstein branch selected at infinity? | Is the selected initial-data submanifold preserved by real-time evolution? |
| Does the on-shell action reduce to the Einstein value? | Does the covariant symplectic form and charge algebra reduce to Einstein's? |
| Are Einstein external states embedded at tree level? | Are nonlinear sources tangent to the selected branch, or Taub/charge obstructed? |

First generalize the on-shell Einstein--Maxwell inclusion and its
charge-dependent second-order obstructions. Then test a declared Lorentzian
dS/AdS boundary condition for locality, causal preservation, and vanishing
symplectic flux. Do not promote an on-shell inclusion to an off-shell BV chain
map or nonlinear closure.

## B1. Berger relational observables

On the positive Berger clock branch, construct at least one complete observable

\[
\mathcal O_A(\tau)=A\text{ evaluated when the clock phase is }\tau.
\]

Certify its clock chart and multiple-crossing domain, gauge invariance,
support/causal dependence, reduced Poisson brackets, nontrivial
\(\tau\)-evolution, and induced pairing. Reconcile that evolution explicitly
with

\[
\Omega(\delta,\mathcal L_D)=0
\]

on the fixed-coupling constrained tangent space. This is the result that can
connect the Berger calculation to dynamical-reference-frame and
relational-observable work; calling the scalar a clock is not enough.

## B2. Asymptotic Bach/BMS phase space

Choose falloffs by finiteness of the symplectic form, action principle, and
flux, not by analogy alone. Determine the radiative and Coulombic data of the
Bach equation, the extra branch, corner matching, differentiable charges, and
Bondi flux. Keep \(H_{\mathrm{ESU}}\), Minkowski dilation \(D_M\), radial
grading \(D_{\mathrm{rad}}\), and time translation \(P_0\) distinct.

The first paper-sized result may use a restricted polyhomogeneous sector, but
must close that sector under evolution and asymptotic symmetries. It must
report whether the extra branch reaches \(\mathscr I^\pm\), its symplectic
sign, and whether a causal boundary condition removes it.

## B3. Twistor or amplitude fixture

After the nonlinear team has a physical transferred cubic tensor, identify
the Einstein projection in helicity or twistor variables and reproduce one
standard three-point or MHV fixture. Then ask whether the compact
charge-sector obstruction appears as a forbidden external state, a vanishing
projected vertex, a boundary selection rule, or no amplitude effect at all.

This is a dictionary and one calculation, not a twistor reformulation of the
project.

## C1. Common quantization benchmark

Defer the direct Mannheim/PT, Fock-BRST, and causal-BV comparison until all
three sides can be put on a common phase space. When the gate opens, compare:

1. equations and boundary conditions;
2. gauged versus charged residual generators;
3. definition of physical state;
4. Fock, BV, deformation, and asymptotic cohomologies;
5. Dirac, Krein, PT/CPT, and transported cyclic pairings;
6. Jordan/logarithmic modes;
7. BRST descent of any positive metric;
8. compatibility with the first physical interaction vertex;
9. scattering interpretation.

The Pais--Uhlenbeck oscillator is a benchmark, not evidence by itself for the
interacting gravitational gauge complex.

## Shared acceptance criteria

Every bridge deliverable must include:

1. the external theorem or fixture reproduced in matched conventions;
2. an explicit dictionary of fields, gauge groups, couplings, generators,
   charges, boundary conditions, and pairings;
3. the new theorem, obstruction, or calculation in both vocabularies;
4. a minimal independent verifier and content-addressed certificate;
5. exact assumptions, generality level, lifecycle state, and one of the
   dependency tags `LOCAL-ALGEBRAIC`, `EUCLIDEAN-SPECTRAL`, `REDUCED-MODE`, or
   `LORENTZIAN-CAUSAL`;
6. a fail-closed statement of what remains unproved;
7. one precise question an adjacent expert could usefully challenge.
