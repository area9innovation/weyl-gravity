\begin{abstract}
We determine the differential-module and resonance structure of axial
Schwarzschild perturbations in four-dimensional pure Weyl gravity.  The
six-state Bach system has an exact filtration with successive quotients
given by two spin-two Regge--Wheeler modules and one spin-one Maxwell
module.  The repeated spin-two block is a non-split self-extension and is
the first jet of a four-state spin-two/spin-one family.

After Liouville reduction, the extension is represented by a scalar
projective cocycle modulo the symmetric-square operator
\[
 \mathcal K_U=D^3+4UD+2D(U).
\]
For axial \(\ell=2\) we prove the exact normal form
\[
 [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
 =\frac{i\omega}{2}\left[1-\frac2r\right].
\]
For every radiative multipole \(\ell\ge2\), the static class
\([1-2/r]\) is nonzero in the rational cokernel of the corresponding
static symmetric-square operator; the dipole is an explicit exact
exception.  Hence the axial \(\ell=2\) Bach extension has exact threshold
valuation one: its original \(1/\omega\) singularity is exact, but the
surviving order-\(\omega\) class cannot be removed by a gauge holomorphic
at threshold.
The class in brackets is exactly the transverse-traceless critical
spin-two mass jet.  Thus the local parameter relation is
\(m=(i\omega/2)\tau\), modulo rational triangular gauge.  The required
gauge grows linearly at infinity, precisely matching the derivative of the
moving massive Jost phase.  The long-range Coulomb tail cancels the
apparent logarithmic phase derivative, so the gauge preserves the
differentiated Jost classes modulo scalar normalization.

At the covariant parent-operator level, the pure-Weyl metric Green
function is exactly the first mass derivative of the Einstein--Weyl
spin-two resolvent.  This determines both leading Laurent coefficients and
the isolated-resonance time contribution directly from the massive QNM
velocity and projector derivative.  The second mass derivative of the QNM
frequency is likewise determined by the ordinary Regge--Wheeler finite
resolvent part and augmented left/right resonant data.

At a simple spin-two quasinormal frequency the complete connection admits
two possible local Smith types.  A validated projective Evans calculation
selects the defective type \((0,0,2)\) at one damped Schwarzschild mode.
The geometric root vector is Einstein, whereas its generalized root vector
has nonzero projection to the Ricci-carrier quotient.  We further show that
the compact-source, compact-observation outgoing Green operator on the
Schwarzschild exterior has a nonzero rank-one pole of order two at this
frequency.  The same selector is the nonzero critical-mass velocity of the
massive spin-two QNM.  The resulting filtration-protected confluence sits
inside an exact local two-parameter phase diagram.  The physical mass axis
has linear splitting, whereas a transverse filtration-breaking
perturbation has square-root splitting.  The spectral gap controls the
projector norm, positive-metric condition number, resolvent crossover, and
precision required to certify the physical slope.

Promotion to a global physical resolvent and to a full retarded generalized
ringdown expansion requires a separate analytic Fredholm realization,
weighted endpoint domains, and bounded metric reconstruction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}

Pure Weyl gravity has a fourth-order metric equation, but its Schwarzschild
perturbations are not best understood as an unstructured fourth-order
system.  In the axial \(\ell=2\) sector the six-dimensional radial system
has the filtered form
\[
 0\subset M_2\subset E_2\subset M_{\rm Bach},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{gr}M_{\rm Bach}\simeq M_2\oplus M_2\oplus M_1,
\]
where \(M_2\) is the spin-two Regge--Wheeler module and \(M_1\) is the
spin-one Maxwell module.  The middle object \(E_2\) is a self-extension
\[
 0\longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow E_2
 \longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
The decisive point is that this extension is non-split over the rational
differential field.  The two spin-two layers therefore share scalar
characteristics without defining two rationally separable radial
branches.

This paper establishes six central results.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The repeated spin-two block is a non-split Regge--Wheeler
  self-extension, realized exactly as a parameter jet.
\item Its projective extension class has the elementary normal form
  \[
    [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
    =\frac{i\omega}{2}\left[1-\frac2r\right].
  \]
  The static class in brackets is nonzero for every \(\ell\ge2\), with an
  explicit exact dipole control, so the \(\ell=2\) Bach threshold valuation
  is exactly one.
\item The class \([1-2/r]\) is exactly the transverse-traceless critical
  mass jet.  Its linearly growing equivalence gauge is endpoint-compatible:
  the Coulomb tail cancels the apparent logarithmic derivative of the
  massive Jost phase.
\item At a certified damped Schwarzschild quasinormal frequency the
  connection is defective.  The associated generalized root vector has a
  nonzero carrier quotient, and the exterior cut-off outgoing Green
  operator has a genuine rank-one double pole.
\item The selector equals the normalized critical-mass QNM velocity and is
  nonzero.  A two-parameter unfolding proves that the exact Einstein
  filtration protects linear mass splitting, while reverse mixing produces
  generic square-root splitting.  Its discriminant, projectors, positive
  metrics, local contour, and Krein form have exact gap-controlled limits.
\item The covariant pure-Weyl metric Green function is the mass derivative
  of the massive spin-two resolvent.  Its double coefficient is controlled
  by QNM motion, its simple coefficient by projector motion, and its local
  polynomial signal obeys an exact rank-one excitation/observation rule.
  The curvature \(\omega_n''(0)\) is an exact augmented finite-part
  functional of ordinary Regge--Wheeler data.
\end{enumerate}

The fourth result is stronger than a statement about a finite connection
matrix.  It is a meromorphic differential-response theorem for compact
radial sources and local radial observations.  It is deliberately weaker
than a causal spacetime resolvent theorem.  No global retarded
\(t e^{i\omega_nt}\) ringdown assertion is made here; the polynomial term
is proved only for the isolated local resonance contour.

\section{Differential-module conventions}

We work over
\[
 K=\C(r,\omega),\qquad D=f\partial_r,\qquad f=1-\frac2r,
\]
with \(\omega\ne0\) unless a threshold limit is stated explicitly.  All
rational gauges and module morphisms are over the differential field
\((K,D)\).  We use \(D(q)\), \(D(U)\), and \(D^3q\), rather than primes, to
avoid confusing the tortoise derivative with \(\partial_r\).

The axial spin-two and spin-one scalar operators are
\[
 L_2=D^2+U_2,\qquad
 U_2=\omega^2-f\left(\frac6{r^2}-\frac6{r^3}\right),
\]
\[
 L_1=D^2+U_1,\qquad
 U_1=\omega^2-f\frac6{r^2}.
\]
The latter is the \(\ell=2\) Maxwell Regge--Wheeler equation.  A
rank-two scalar module is represented by its companion system on
\((y,Dy)^T\).

The source Einstein layer is the kernel of the linearized Ricci/Schouten
carrier map.  The target spin-two quotient is its physical
Regge--Wheeler carrier.  The third quotient is the Maxwell class of the
target Einstein gauge vector.  Consequently, ``non-Einstein'' below means
nonzero projection to the gauge-invariant carrier quotient, not failure of
a coordinate gauge condition.

\section{Exact axial RW/RW/Maxwell filtration}

In the certified factor gauge the six-state system is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:six-state}
\begin{aligned}
 DX&=AX+EY+CZ,\\
 DY&=AY+DZ,\\
 DZ&=A_1Z,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(X,Y,Z\in K^2\), the companion matrices \(A\) and \(A_1\) represent

\[
 E=USJ,\qquad C=USN.
\]
The rational reconstruction has full row rank on the declared domain.

\begin{theorem}[Filtered axial Bach module]\label{thm:filtration}
For axial \(\ell=2\) Schwarzschild perturbations, the reduced six-state
Bach module has a filtration whose successive simple factors are
\[
 M_2,\qquad M_2,\qquad M_1.
\]
The middle two-spin-two block is an extension \(E_2\) of \(M_2\) by

vector.  The filtration is exact but is not asserted to be a direct sum.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is an exact rational statement.  It is independent of endpoint
normalization, scattering conventions, and the later QNM specialization.

\section{Partial-jet realization}

 \mathcal B(\tau)=

Let its fundamental matrix be
\[
 \Phi_4(\tau)=
 \begin{pmatrix}P(\tau)&Q(\tau)\\0&R\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact partial jet]\label{thm:partial-jet}
The six-state generator in \eqref{eq:six-state} is the first
spin-two-row jet of \(\mathcal B(\tau)\) at \(\tau=0\).  Its fundamental
matrix is
\[
 \Phi_6=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 P&\dot P&\dot Q\\
 0&P&Q\\
 0&0&R
 \end{pmatrix}_{\tau=0},
\qquad
 \det\Phi_6=(\det P)^2\det R.
\]
Consequently any factor-adapted endpoint connection has the triangular
form
\[
 T(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a&b&c\\
 0&a&d\\
 0&0&g
 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 \det T=a^2g.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \(D\Phi_4(\tau)=\mathcal B(\tau)\Phi_4(\tau)\) at
\(\tau=0\).  The equations for \(P,\dot P,Q,\dot Q,R\) reproduce the
three rows of \eqref{eq:six-state} column by column.  The determinant
identity follows from block triangularity.  The same identity applied to
endpoint transition matrices gives the displayed connection form.

\section{Projective gauge and symmetric-square cohomology}

Consider a scalar self-extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-extension}
 Lx=(s_1D+s_0)y,\qquad Ly=0,\qquad L=D^2+U.
\end{equation}
Set
\[
 \mathcal I=s_0-\frac12D(s_1),\qquad
 \mathcal K_U=D^3+4UD+2D(U).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Explicit triangular gauge]\label{lem:gauge}
For \(q\in K\), define
\[
 Q(q)=qD-\frac12D(q).
\]
On every solution \(Ly=0\),
\[
 [L,Q(q)]y=-\frac12\mathcal K_U(q)y.
\]
Thus the change of lift \(x\mapsto x+Q(q)y\) changes the scalar
extension density by
\[
 \mathcal I\longmapsto
 \mathcal I-\frac12\mathcal K_U(q).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand \(LQ(q)-Q(q)L\), use the Leibniz rule for \(D\), and then set
\(D^2y=-Uy\).  The coefficient of \(Dy\) cancels, while the coefficient
of \(y\) is
\(-\frac12[D^3q+4UDq+2D(U)q]\).
\end{proof}

The extension is therefore represented by
\[
 [\mathcal I]\in K/\mathcal K_UK.
\]
Because Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} contains the factor \(1/2\), removal of a
term \(\mathcal K_Uq\) uses the triangular gauge \(Q(2q)\).  This factor
does not alter the cohomology quotient but matters in an explicit frame
calculation.

The equation \(\mathcal K_Uq=0\) is the symmetric-square equation of
\(L\).  If \(y_1,y_2\) span the scalar solution space, then its solution
space is spanned by \(y_1^2,y_1y_2,y_2^2\).  The same operator controls
both the projective obstruction and trace-free horizontal endomorphisms.

\section{Mass-direction normal form of the Bach cocycle}

For the axial \(\ell=2\) Bach extension, rational reduction gives
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}
 =
 \frac{i(r-2)(2r\omega^2+3\omega^2+12)}
 {5r^4\omega}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Mass-direction normal form]\label{thm:mass-normal}
Let
\[
 q(r,\omega)=
 -\frac{i}{120\omega}
 \left(15r+13+\frac{12}{r}+\frac9{r^2}\right).
\]
For \(\omega\ne0\),
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}
 =\mathcal K_{U_2}q+\frac{i\omega}{2}f.
\]
Consequently
\[
 [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
 =\frac{i\omega}{2}[f]
 \quad\hbox{in}\quad K/\mathcal K_{U_2}K.
\]
In particular, \([f]\ne0\) wherever the Bach self-extension is
non-split.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \(D=f\partial_r\) three times to \(q\) and substitute
\[
 U_2=\omega^2-f\left(\frac6{r^2}-\frac6{r^3}\right)
\]
in \(\mathcal K_{U_2}q=D^3q+4U_2Dq+2D(U_2)q\).  Clearing the common
denominator \(120r^4\omega\) reduces the claim to a polynomial identity
in \(r\) and \(\omega\).  Exact coefficient comparison gives the displayed
formula.  The quotient identity follows immediately.  If \([f]=0\), then
\([\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]=0\), which would split the extension.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-radiative-multipole static mass direction]
\label{thm:static-mass-nontrivial}
Let
\[
 \Lambda=\ell(\ell+1),\qquad
 U_{\ell,0}=-f\left(\frac{\Lambda}{r^2}-\frac6{r^3}\right),
 \qquad \ell\ge2.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\;
 [f]\ne0
 \quad\hbox{in}\quad
 \C(r)/\mathcal K_{U_{\ell,0}}\C(r),
 \qquad f=1-\frac2r.
 \;}
\]
Equivalently, no rational function \(p\) satisfies
\(\mathcal K_{U_{\ell,0}}p=f\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A rational solution can have poles only at \(0\), \(2\), and infinity.
At an ordinary finite point, a pole of order \(N\) would produce an
uncancelled pole of order \(N+3\), because the coefficient of
\(\partial_r^3\) is nonzero there.

Put \(s=r-2\).  Since
\[
 D=\frac{s}{2+s}\partial_s
   =\frac{s}{2}\partial_s+O(s^2\partial_s),
 \qquad U_{\ell,0}=O(s),
\]
a putative leading term \(p\sim s^{-N}\), \(N>0\), gives
\[
 D^3p=-\frac{N^3}{8}s^{-N}+O(s^{1-N}),
\]
whereas the potential terms are \(O(s^{1-N})\) and \(f=O(s)\).
Thus \(p\) has no pole at \(r=2\).

At \(r=0\), the indicial leading term is
\[
 \mathcal K_{U_{\ell,0}}(r^k)
 =
 -8(k-6)(k-2)(k+2)r^{k-6}+\cdots.
\]
For \(k<0\), only \(k=-2\) is exceptional.  Write
\[
 p=a_{-2}r^{-2}+a_{-1}r^{-1}+a_0+a_1r+\cdots.
\]
The first three Laurent equations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_{-1}&=\frac{\Lambda-2}{3}a_{-2},\\
 a_0&=\frac{(\Lambda-2)(5\Lambda-4)}{72}a_{-2},\\
 a_1&=\frac{\Lambda(\Lambda-2)(\Lambda-1)}{72}a_{-2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first resonant compatibility condition is
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \frac{\Lambda^2(\Lambda-2)^2}{9}a_{-2}=0.
 \;}
\]
For \(\ell\ge2\), \(\Lambda\ge6\), so
\(a_{-2}=a_{-1}=a_0=a_1=0\).
In particular, the exceptional exponent does not produce an admissible
pole.  Hence a rational solution would be a polynomial.

If \(p\sim a_Mr^M\) at infinity, then
\[
 \mathcal K_{U_{\ell,0}}p
 =(M-1)(M-2\ell-2)(M+2\ell)a_Mr^{M-3}+\cdots.
\]
The static Regge--Wheeler equation has a polynomial solution
\[
 y_\ell(r)=\sum_{k=3}^{\ell+1}c_kr^k,
 \qquad
 c_k=
 \frac{(k-1)(k-2)-\Lambda}{2(k-3)(k+1)}c_{k-1}
 \quad(4\le k\le\ell+1).
\]
Direct coefficient substitution proves
\((D^2+U_{\ell,0})y_\ell=0\), so the symmetric-square identity gives
\[
 \mathcal K_{U_{\ell,0}}(y_\ell^2)=0.
\]
For \(M>3\), the leading coefficient above can vanish only at
\(M=2\ell+2=\deg y_\ell^2\).  Subtracting the appropriate multiple of
\(y_\ell^2\) therefore leaves a polynomial of degree at most three:
\[
 p=a_0+a_1r+a_2r^2+a_3r^3.
\]
The preceding Laurent recurrence gives \(a_0=a_1=0\).  Exact substitution
then yields
\[
 (\Lambda+4)a_2=15a_3,
 \qquad
 (3\Lambda+4)a_2=(6\Lambda+33)a_3.
\]
The inhomogeneous equation forces \(a_3\ne0\).  Eliminating \(a_2\)
would require
\[
 15(3\Lambda+4)=(\Lambda+4)(6\Lambda+33),
 \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
 \Lambda^2+2\Lambda+12=0.
\]
This has no real root and hence no value
\(\Lambda=\ell(\ell+1)\), \(\ell\ge2\).
\end{proof}

\section{Period formula for the connection shear}

Let \(y_1,y_2\) be a scalar basis and set
\[
 Y=
 \begin{pmatrix}y_1&y_2\\Dy_1&Dy_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 W=y_1Dy_2-y_2Dy_1.
\]
Because the companion matrix has zero trace, \(D(W)=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Symmetric-square period matrix]\label{thm:period}
For the family
\[
 (D^2+U+\tau V)y=0,
\]
the interaction-picture variation satisfies
\[
 Y^{-1}\partial_\tau A\,Y
 =
 \frac{V}{W}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 y_1y_2&y_2^2\\
 -y_1^2&-y_1y_2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently the spin-two connection shear is determined by the three
periods
\[
 \int\frac{V}{W}y_1^2\,dr_*,
 \qquad
 \int\frac{V}{W}y_1y_2\,dr_*,
 \qquad
 \int\frac{V}{W}y_2^2\,dr_*.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the basis \((y,Dy)^T\),
\[
 A(\tau)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-(U+\tau V)&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 \partial_\tau A=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\-V&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Insert
\[
 Y^{-1}=\frac1W
 \begin{pmatrix}Dy_2&-y_2\\-Dy_1&y_1\end{pmatrix}
\]
and multiply.  Integration of the interaction-picture equation gives the
three entries.
\end{proof}

A coboundary \(\mathcal K_Uq\) changes these periods by endpoint terms.
This is the connection-level form of Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}: rational
triangular gauges alter Jost frames but not the intrinsic divisor or Smith
selector once the endpoint units are tracked.

\section{Nonsplitting, endomorphisms, and Fitting ideals}

\begin{theorem}[Non-split physical-axis extension]\label{thm:nonsplit}
For every real \(\omega>0\), the axial \(\ell=2\) spin-two block
\[
 0\longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow E_2
 \longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow0
\]
is non-split over \(\C(r)[D]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Certificate-supported exact proof]
The rational splitting equation reduces, after exhaustive pole and degree
bounds, to a finite coefficient system.  Fixed minors of its coefficient
and augmented matrices are
\[

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local commutant]\label{thm:commutant}

 \End_{\C(r)[D]}(E_2)
 \simeq\C[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Every endomorphism is \(aI+bN\), where \(N^2=0\).  Hence the only
idempotents are \(0,I\), the only involutions are \(\pm I\), and every
diagonalizable local endomorphism is scalar.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The submodule \(M_2\) is the unique simple submodule: a second simple
submodule mapping nontrivially to the quotient would split the extension.
An endomorphism therefore induces scalars \(a,d\) on the submodule and
quotient.  Naturality of the nonzero extension class gives \(a=d\).
Subtracting \(aI\) leaves a map that vanishes on the submodule and quotient,
so it factors through the quotient back into the submodule and is a scalar
multiple of the canonical nilpotent \(N\).  The corollaries follow from
\((aI+bN)^k=a^kI+ka^{k-1}bN\).
\end{proof}

exponents are \((1,1)\).  Appending the spin-one unit adds a zero exponent.

\section{Resonant evaluation of the self-extension}
\label{sec:resonant-evaluation}

The projective selector, adjoint overlap, root-chain coefficient, and
Green-pole residue are different representations of one resonant
functional.  This is the finite-interval Evans/Melnikov mechanism; compare
the general relation between Evans derivatives and adjoint generalized
eigenfunctions in \cite{kapitula1999}.

Let
\[
 L(\omega):X\longrightarrow Y
\]
denote the analytic finite-interval spin-two boundary pencil, with the
endpoint conditions included either in its domain or as finite-dimensional
components of an augmented range.  At a simple resonance choose right and
left states
\[
 L_nu_n=0,\qquad L_n^*\widetilde u_n=0,
\qquad L_n=L(\omega_n),
\]
and define, using the bilinear duality of the augmented boundary problem,
\[
 \alpha_n=
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,L'(\omega_n)u_n\right\rangle,
 \qquad \alpha_n\ne0.
\]
Let the repeated block be
\[
 \mathbb L(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}L(\omega)&K(\omega)\\0&L(\omega)\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 \beta_n=
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,K(\omega_n)u_n\right\rangle.
\]
All endpoint components of the augmented pencil and its duality are
included in these pairings.

\begin{theorem}[Resonant evaluation theorem]
\label{thm:resonant-evaluation}
In endpoint frames compatible with the partial jet,
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \kappa_n:=
 \frac{b(\omega_n)}{a'(\omega_n)}
 =
 \frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n}
 =
 -\omega_n'(0).
 \;}
\]
Here \(\omega_n(\tau)\) is the resonance branch of
\[
 L(\omega,\tau)=L(\omega)+\tau K(\omega)
\]
through \(\omega_n\).  The equality is invariant under a common analytic
nonzero Evans unit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate
\[
 L(\omega_n(\tau),\tau)u(\tau)=0
\]
at \(\tau=0\):
\[
 L_n\dot u+\omega_n'(0)L_n'u_n+K_nu_n=0.
\]
Pairing with \(\widetilde u_n\) removes the first term and gives
\[
 \omega_n'(0)\alpha_n+\beta_n=0.
\]
On the connection side, differentiation of
\[
 a(\omega_n(\tau),\tau)=0
\]
gives
\[
 a'(\omega_n)\omega_n'(0)
 +\partial_\tau a(\omega_n,0)=0.
\]
The exact partial-jet construction identifies
\(\partial_\tau a(\omega_n,0)=b(\omega_n)\).  Multiplication of the Evans
coefficient by an analytic unit \(u(\omega,\tau)\) multiplies both
\(a'\) and \(b\) at the root by \(u(\omega_n,0)\), so the ratio is
unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Resonant functional on the extension class]
\label{prop:resonant-functional}
The assignment
\[
 \mathfrak M_n([K])
 :=
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,K(\omega_n)u_n\right\rangle
\]
is a well-defined linear functional on the local self-extension class.
Thus nonsplitting is the statement \([K]\ne0\), while defectiveness at
\(\omega_n\) is the sharper statement
\(\mathfrak M_n([K])\ne0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under a triangular change of splitting,
\[
 K\longmapsto K+LQ-QL.
\]
At the resonance,
\[
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,(L_nQ-QL_n)u_n\right\rangle
 =
 \left\langle L_n^*\widetilde u_n,Qu_n\right\rangle
 -
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,QL_nu_n\right\rangle
 =0.
\]
When endpoint conditions are encoded in an augmented pencil, the same
calculation includes the corresponding boundary terms by definition.
\end{proof}

The theorem and proposition separate a global module property from its
evaluation at one resonance.  A nonzero extension class need not be
defective at every QNM: the linear functional \(\mathfrak M_n\) may vanish
at isolated modes.

\section{The generalized root vector is non-Einstein}

\begin{proposition}[Nonzero carrier in the generalized root]
\label{prop:carrier-root}
Assume

 a(\omega_n)=0,\qquad a'(\omega_n)\ne0,\qquad
 b(\omega_n)\ne0.

The geometric root vector belongs to the source Einstein submodule, while

 -\frac{a'(\omega_n)}{b(\omega_n)}
 =
 -\frac{\alpha_n}{\beta_n}
 =

\]
Thus the defective doubled resonance is not two copies of an Einstein
quasinormal mode: its generalized member is intrinsically non-Einstein.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

 a=a_1z+O(z^2),\qquad b=b_0+O(z),
\qquad a_1b_0\ne0.

For

 c(z)=c_0+zc_1+O(z^2),\qquad T_2(z)c(z)=O(z^2),

the order-zero equation gives \(c_0=e_X\), the source Einstein vector.

 a_1+b_0y=0,

so \(y=-a_1/b_0\ne0\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration}, \(e_Y\) projects

\section{Exterior cut-off outgoing Green double pole}

Fix ordinary radii \(2<r_H<r_I<\infty\) inside the certified analytic
continuation domains.  Let
\[
 H(\omega):\C^3\longrightarrow
 C^\infty([r_H,r_I];\C^6)
\]
be the analytic Poisson operator for horizon-selected homogeneous data,
let \(\Gamma_-(\omega)\) be the unwanted incoming trace at \(r_I\), and
set
\[
 T(\omega)=\Gamma_-(\omega)H(\omega).
\]
For a compactly supported source \(F\), let \(P_H(\omega)F\) denote the
inhomogeneous solution with zero horizon data.  The outgoing solution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:green}
 R_{\rm out}(\omega)F
 =
 P_H(\omega)F-
 H(\omega)T(\omega)^{-1}
 \Gamma_-(\omega)P_H(\omega)F.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-interval outgoing Green pole]\label{thm:green-pole}
On a neighborhood of the certified frequency \(\omega_n\), the operator
\[
 R_{\rm out}(\omega):
 C_c^\infty((r_H,r_I);\C^6)
 \longrightarrow
 C^\infty_{\rm loc}((r_H,r_I);\C^6)
\]
is meromorphic and has a nonzero rank-one pole of order two.  Up to the
endpoint trace sign convention, its principal coefficient is
\[
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{(\omega-\omega_n)^{-2}}R_{\rm out}
 =
 \frac{b(\omega_n)}{a'(\omega_n)^2}
 H(\omega_n)e_X
 \otimes
 \left[e_Y^*\Gamma_-(\omega_n)P_H(\omega_n)\right].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All factors in \eqref{eq:green} except \(T^{-1}\) are analytic.  After
analytic elimination of the spin-one unit, the singular block is
\[
 T_2^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a^{-1}&-ba^{-2}\\
 0&a^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{z^{-2}}T_2^{-1}
 =-\frac{b(\omega_n)}{a'(\omega_n)^2}
 e_X\otimes e_Y^*.
\]
The minus sign in \eqref{eq:green} gives the displayed convention.

The range vector is nonzero because the homogeneous Poisson map is
injective, so \(H(\omega_n)e_X\ne0\).  The covector is nonzero by a cutoff
Jost construction.  Given any incoming coefficient \(q\), choose the
corresponding incoming Jost solution \(J_-q\) near \(r_I\), multiply it by
a smooth cutoff \(\chi\) that vanishes near \(r_H\) and equals one near
\(r_I\), and set
\[
 F=(D-\mathbb A(\omega_n))(\chi J_-q).
\]
Then \(F\) is compactly supported, \(P_H(\omega_n)F=\chi J_-q\), and
\(\Gamma_-P_H(\omega_n)F=q\).  Hence the incoming trace map from compact
sources is onto.  Taking \(q=e_Y\) shows that the covector does not vanish.
The tensor product is therefore nonzero and rank one.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact transparent cuts}

Put \(x=r_*\).  On the certified QNM neighborhood, let
\[
 u_H(x,\omega),\qquad u_\infty(x,\omega)
\]
be analytic spin-two Jost solutions satisfying the future-horizon and
outgoing-infinity conditions.  The scalar Evans coefficient is, up to an
analytic unit,
\[
 a(\omega)=W(u_H,u_\infty).
\]
Let \(\chi_{\rm s}\) and \(\chi_{\rm o}\) be nontrivial smooth source and
observation cutoffs with compact support.  Choose finite cuts
\[
 x_-<
 \operatorname{supp}\chi_{\rm s}\cup
 \operatorname{supp}\chi_{\rm o}
 <x_+.
\]
At the two cuts impose the exact transparent boundary conditions
\[
 W(u,u_H)|_{x_-}=0,\qquad
 W(u,u_\infty)|_{x_+}=0.
\]
Their coefficients are analytic in \(\omega\).  Uniqueness of the exterior
Jost problems shows that the resulting finite-interval inverse agrees
exactly, between the cutoffs, with the meromorphically continued exterior
outgoing inverse.

\begin{theorem}[Exterior cut-off Green pole]
\label{thm:exterior-cutoff-pole}
For any nontrivial compactly supported source and observation cutoffs,
\[
 \chi_{\rm o}R_{\rm ext}(\omega)\chi_{\rm s}
\]
has a genuine nonzero rank-one pole of order two at the certified
Schwarzschild QNM.  In the augmented Fredholm normalization its principal
coefficient is
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{(\omega-\omega_n)^{-2}}
 \bigl(\chi_{\rm o}R_{\rm ext}\chi_{\rm s}\bigr)
 =
 -\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}
 (\chi_{\rm o}u_n)
 \otimes
 (\widetilde u_n\chi_{\rm s}).
 \;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The transparent conditions select exactly the restrictions of the
exterior horizon and infinity Jost solutions.  Hence
\[
 \chi_{\rm o}R_{\rm ext}(\omega)\chi_{\rm s}
 =
 \chi_{\rm o}R_{[x_-,x_+]}(\omega)\chi_{\rm s}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:green-pole} and
\eqref{eq:invariant-double-pole} give the displayed principal
coefficient.  It is nonzero: \(\beta_n\ne0\), while a nonzero solution of
a second-order ODE cannot vanish on a nonempty open interval.  Thus
\(\chi_{\rm o}u_n\) and
\(\widetilde u_n\chi_{\rm s}\) are nonzero for every nontrivial smooth
cutoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Non-annihilation by certified reconstruction]
The certified rational reconstruction into the axial metric/curvature
variables does not annihilate the range
\(H(\omega_n)e_X\).  Hence at least one reconstructed local observable has
the same order-two radial response pole.
\end{corollary}

In the finite-interval Fredholm normalization of
Section~\ref{sec:resonant-evaluation}, the scalar resolvent has
\[
 L(\omega)^{-1}
 =
 \frac{u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n}
 {\alpha_n(\omega-\omega_n)}
 +O(1).
\]
The upper-right block of \(\mathbb L^{-1}\) is
\(-L^{-1}KL^{-1}\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:resonant-evaluation} therefore
gives the invariant principal coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant-double-pole}
 \boxed{\;
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{(\omega-\omega_n)^{-2}}
 (\mathbb L^{-1})_{12}
 =
 -\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}
 u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n
 =
 -\frac{\kappa_n}{\alpha_n}
 u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n.
 \;}
\end{equation}
The Poisson/trace coefficient in Theorem~\ref{thm:green-pole} and the
exterior coefficient in Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-cutoff-pole} are the same
rank-one residue expressed in endpoint and augmented Fredholm coordinates.

\section{Conditional global Fredholm promotion}
\label{sec:fredholm}

Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-cutoff-pole} is an exterior differential

A global physical resolvent without cutoffs requires a separate

 \mathbb L(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 L(\omega)&K(\omega)\\
 0&L(\omega)
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(L(\omega):D\subset X\to Y\) is an analytic Fredholm pencil of

\[
 u_n\in\ker L(\omega_n),\qquad
 \widetilde u_n\in\ker L(\omega_n)^*,
\]
and define using the \(Y^*\)-\(Y\) bilinear duality
\[
 \alpha_n=
 \langle\widetilde u_n,L'(\omega_n)u_n\rangle,
\qquad
 \beta_n=

\]

If \(L^{-1}\) is meromorphic near \(\omega_n\), its zero is simple,

 \mathbb L^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}

 \end{pmatrix}

 -\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}

\section{Critical Einstein--Weyl mass comparison}
\label{sec:mass}

On the transverse-traceless spin-two sector, the quadratic
Einstein--Weyl parent equation is
\[
 (\mathcal E+m\mathcal A)\phi=0,
\]
where the parameter \(m\) has the dimensions of squared mass.  Its
Schwarzschild axial scalar reduction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:massive-rw}
 L_m(\omega)y=
 \left[D^2+\omega^2-V_2-mf\right]y=0,
 \qquad f=1-\frac2r.
\end{equation}
For a differentiable family \(y(m)\), differentiation at \(m=0\) gives
\[
 L_2\,\partial_my=f\,y.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact local critical-mass-jet identification]
\label{thm:mass-jet}
The projective cocycle of the transverse-traceless critical mass jet is
\[
 [\mathcal I_{\rm mass}]=[f].
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\;
 [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
 =\frac{i\omega}{2}[\mathcal I_{\rm mass}].
 \;}
\]
With the convention \(L_m=L_2-mf\), the local first-order parameter
relation is
\[
 \boxed{\;m=\frac{i\omega}{2}\tau\;}
\]
modulo rational triangular gauge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:massive-rw} gives the extension equation
\(L_2\partial_my=fy\), so its scalar projective density is \(f\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:mass-normal} gives
\([\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]=(i\omega/2)[f]\).  Comparing the two tangent
equations yields the parameter relation.
\end{proof}

This theorem closes the local transverse-traceless differential-module
comparison.  We next show that the same gauge matches the differentiated
physical Jost classes.

\subsection{Endpoint-compatible mass gauge}

The rational representative in Theorem~\ref{thm:mass-normal} satisfies
\[
 q(r,\omega)=-\frac{i}{8\omega}r+O(1),
 \qquad r\to\infty.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Forced moving-phase gauge]
\label{prop:unbounded-gauge}
Every rational \(q\) satisfying
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}
 =\mathcal K_{U_2}q+\frac{i\omega}{2}f
\]
has the forced leading behavior
\[
 q(r,\omega)=-\frac{i}{8\omega}r+O(1).
\]
Thus the equivalence gauge is necessarily unbounded in a fixed massless
phase frame.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At infinity,
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}-\frac{i\omega}{2}f
 \longrightarrow-\frac{i\omega}{2},
\qquad
 U_2\longrightarrow\omega^2.
\]
A rational \(q=o(r)\) is \(O(1)\) and gives
\(\mathcal K_{U_2}q=o(1)\), so it cannot produce the nonzero constant.
If \(q\sim cr\), then \(Dq\to c\), while \(D^3q\to0\) and
\(D(U_2)q\to0\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal K_{U_2}q\longrightarrow4\omega^2c.
\]
Matching the constant gives \(c=-i/(8\omega)\), uniquely.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Factor normalization.}
The normalization in Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} must be retained.  Define the
field-redefinition operator
\[
 \boxed{\;
 Q_q=2qD-D(q).
 \;}
\]
Then
\[
 [L_2,Q_q]y=-\mathcal K_{U_2}(q)y,
 \qquad L_2y=0.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 L_2x=\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}y,
\qquad
 \widehat x=x+Q_qy,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-gauged-extension}
 \boxed{\;
 L_2\widehat x=\frac{i\omega}{2}fy.
 \;}
\end{equation}
The factor \(2\) in \(Q_q\) is essential.

\begin{theorem}[Coulomb cancellation and differentiated Jost classes]
\label{thm:jost-mass-jet}
Let \(y_\sigma(m,r)\), \(\sigma=\pm1\), be a massive spin-two Jost germ at
infinity, with \(k=(\omega^2-m)^{1/2}\) on the branch through
\(k(0)=\omega\).  It has the form
\[
 y_\sigma(m,r)
 =e^{\sigma ikr}r^{\rho_\sigma(m)}
 \left(1+O(r^{-1})\right),
\qquad
 \rho_\sigma(m)=
 \sigma i\left(2k+\frac{m}{k}\right).
\]
In particular,
\[
 \rho_\sigma'(0)=0,
\qquad
 \frac{\partial_my_\sigma(0,r)}{y_\sigma(0,r)}
 =-\frac{\sigma i}{2\omega}r+O(1).
\]
Writing \(c(\omega)=i\omega/2\), the rational gauge satisfies
\[
 \frac{Q_qy_\sigma}{y_\sigma}
 =\frac{\sigma r}{4}+O(1)
 =c(\omega)
 \frac{\partial_my_\sigma(0,r)}{y_\sigma(0,r)}+O(1).
\]
At the horizon the mass perturbation is multiplied by \(f=O(r-2)\);
the indicial exponent is unchanged, \(q\) is regular, and \(Q_q\)
preserves the phase-factored analytic ingoing germ.  Hence, at either
endpoint \(e\in\{\mathcal H^+,\mathcal I^+\}\),
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \widehat x_e-c(\omega)\partial_my_e(0)
 =\lambda_e(\omega)y_e
 \;}
\]
for an analytic scalar \(\lambda_e\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At infinity the massive equation is
\[
 D^2y+
 \left(k^2+\frac{2m}{r}+O(r^{-2})\right)y=0.
\]
Substitution of the displayed ansatz at order \(r^{-1}\) gives
\[
 2\sigma ik\rho_\sigma+4k^2+2m=0,
\]
and therefore the stated \(\rho_\sigma\).  Since
\[
 k'(0)=-\frac1{2\omega},
\qquad
 \left.\frac{d}{dm}\left(2k+\frac{m}{k}\right)\right|_{m=0}
 =-\frac1\omega+\frac1\omega=0,
\]
the logarithmic derivative cancels.  The remaining phase derivative gives
the linear term in \(\partial_my_\sigma/y_\sigma\).  Using
\(q=-ir/(8\omega)+O(1)\) and
\(Dy_\sigma/y_\sigma=\sigma i\omega+O(r^{-1})\) gives
\[
 2q\frac{Dy_\sigma}{y_\sigma}-D(q)
 =\frac{\sigma r}{4}+O(1).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:mass-gauged-extension} shows that
\(\widehat x-c\,\partial_my\) is homogeneous.  The asymptotic calculation
places it in the endpoint Jost line, proving the infinity statement.
Regular Frobenius dependence and the unchanged indicial exponent prove
the horizon statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Critical-mass Evans derivative and QNM velocity]
\label{thm:mass-qnm-slope}
Let \(a(\omega,m)\) be the massive spin-two Evans coefficient in analytic
horizon and infinity Jost normalizations, and let \(b_{\rm B}(\omega)\)
be the Bach connection shear.  There is an analytic scalar \(h(\omega)\)

\begin{theorem}[Universal critical-resonance criterion]
\label{thm:universal-critical-resonance}
Let \(L(\omega):D\to Y\) be an analytic Fredholm pencil with fixed domain,
and suppose \(\omega_n\) is a simple resonance.  Let
\[
 L_nu_n=0,\qquad L_n^*\widetilde u_n=0,
\qquad
 \alpha_n=\langle\widetilde u_n,L_n'u_n\rangle\ne0.
\]
For an analytic deformation \(L_m=L+mA\), put
\[
 \beta_n=\langle\widetilde u_n,A(\omega_n)u_n\rangle
\]
in the augmented endpoint pairing.  If \(R=L^{-1}\), then
\[
 C(\omega)=R(\omega)A(\omega)R(\omega)
\]
has expansion
\[
 \boxed{\;
 C(\omega)=
 \frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}
 \frac{u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n}{(\omega-\omega_n)^2}
 +\frac{C_{-1}}{\omega-\omega_n}
 +C_{\rm hol}(\omega).
 \;}
\]
Consequently \(C\) has an exact pole of order two if and only if
\(\beta_n\ne0\); at a simple resonance it cannot have a pole of order
three.  If \(\omega_n(m)\) is the deformed resonance branch, then
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \nu_n:=\omega_n'(0)=-\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n},
 \qquad
 C_{-2}=-\nu_nP_n,
 \qquad
 P_n=\frac{u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n}{\alpha_n}.
 \;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 R(\omega)=\frac{P_n}{\omega-\omega_n}+H_n+O(\omega-\omega_n).
\]
The only second-order term in \(RAR\) is
\[
 P_nA_nP_n=
 \frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n.
\]
Differentiating
\(L_m(\omega_n(m))u_n(m)=0\) and pairing with
\(\widetilde u_n\) gives
\(\beta_n+\nu_n\alpha_n=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical simple pole and tangent state]
\label{prop:canonical-simple-pole}
Let \(z=\omega-\omega_n\) and expand
\[
 R(\omega)=\frac{P_n}{z}+H_n+O(z),
 \qquad
 A(\omega)=A_n+zA_n'+O(z^2).
\]
Then the critical response \(C=RAR=-\partial_mR_m|_{m=0}\) has
\[
 \boxed{\;
 C_{-2}=P_nA_nP_n=-\nu_nP_n
 \;}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{\;
 C_{-1}
 =P_nA_nH_n+H_nA_nP_n+P_nA_n'P_n
 =-\dot P_n.
 \;}
\]
The term \(P_nA_n'P_n\) is absent only when the deformation operator is
frequency independent in the fixed-domain pencil.

Let
\[
 g_n=(A_n+\nu_nL_n')u_n.
\]
Then the right and left tangent classes are
\[
 \boxed{\;
 [\dot u_n]
 =
 [-H_ng_n]\in D/\C u_n,
 \qquad
 [\dot{\widetilde u}_n]
 =
 [-\widetilde u_n(A_n+\nu_nL_n')H_n]
 \bmod\C\widetilde u_n.
 \;}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct multiplication of the two displayed expansions gives both Laurent
coefficients, including the \(A_n'\) term.  Differentiating the moving
simple pole gives \(C_{-1}=-\dot P_n\).

The differentiated QNM equation is
\[
 L_n\dot u_n+g_n=0,
 \qquad
 \langle\widetilde u_n,g_n\rangle
 =\beta_n+\nu_n\alpha_n=0.
\]
The Laurent identities imply
\[
 L_nH_n=I-L_n'P_n.
\]
Since \(P_ng_n=0\), the vector \(-H_ng_n\) solves the equation; the
remaining freedom is a multiple of \(u_n\).  The left formula follows
from the corresponding right Laurent identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Second-order massive-QNM curvature]
\label{thm:qnm-curvature}
Let
\[
 L_m(\omega)=L(\omega)+mA(\omega),
 \qquad
 \omega_n(m)=\omega_n+\nu_nm+\frac12\xi_nm^2+O(m^3),
\]
be a fixed-domain analytic Fredholm realization of the massive QNM
branch, with all endpoint-map derivatives included in the augmented
operator.  Put
\[
 B_n=A_n+\nu_nL_n',
 \qquad
 \alpha_n=\langle\widetilde u_n,L_n'u_n\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \xi_n=\omega_n''(0)=
 \frac{
 2\langle\widetilde u_n,B_nH_nB_nu_n\rangle
 -\nu_n^2\langle\widetilde u_n,L_n''u_n\rangle
 -2\nu_n\langle\widetilde u_n,A_n'u_n\rangle
 }{\alpha_n}.
 \;}
\]
The formula is independent of the analytic normalization chosen for
\(u_n(m)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first differentiated equation and the finite-part reconstruction are
\[
 L_n\dot u_n+B_nu_n=0,
 \qquad
 \dot u_n=-H_nB_nu_n+c\,u_n.
 \]

 L_n\ddot u_n+2B_n\dot u_n+
 \left(\xi_nL_n'+\nu_n^2L_n''+2\nu_nA_n'\right)u_n=0.

Pairing with \(\widetilde u_n\), substituting the finite-part formula, and
using
\[
 \langle\widetilde u_n,B_nu_n\rangle
 =\beta_n+\nu_n\alpha_n=0
\]
eliminates both \(L_n\ddot u_n\) and the normalization term \(c\,u_n\).
Solving the remaining scalar equation for \(\xi_n\) gives the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Augmented QNM Hellmann--Feynman formula]
\label{prop:qnm-hellmann-feynman}

 \boxed{\;
 \partial_pa
 =

 y_-(x)\,\partial_pQ(x,\omega,m)\,y_+(x)\,dx+B_p,
 \;}

endpoint-normalization term) in the augmented pairing.  At a simple QNM,

 \boxed{\;

 =-\frac{\partial_ma}{\partial_\omega a}
 =-\frac{
 \langle\widetilde u_n,A_nu_n\rangle_{\rm aug}}
 {\langle\widetilde u_n,L_n'u_n\rangle_{\rm aug}}.
 \;}

For \(Q=\omega^2-V_2-mf\), this is
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \nu_n
 =-
 \frac{-\displaystyle\int f\,u_n^2\,dx+B_m}
 {2\omega_n\displaystyle\int u_n^2\,dx+B_\omega}.
 \;}
\]
The integrals are bilinear QNM integrals, not positive \(L^2\) norms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the two scalar equations, apply the Lagrange identity, and
integrate between the endpoint cuts.  The bulk term is the displayed
integral; differentiating the analytic transparent maps supplies \(B_p\).
At a QNM the two Jost solutions are proportional, and the proportionality
cancels from \(-a_m/a_\omega\).  The augmented formula is the same
identity written invariantly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic versus filtration-preserving splitting]
\label{prop:generic-filtered-splitting}
For a general first-order perturbation of

\begin{theorem}[Invariant two-parameter unfolding]
\label{thm:two-parameter-unfolding}
Let \(F(\omega,m,\epsilon)\) be the determinant of the reduced
two-spin-two connection block, or the complete determinant divided by the
local spin-one unit, and put \(z=\omega-\omega_n\).  At the critical point,
\[
 F=F_\omega=0,\qquad F_{\omega\omega}\ne0.
\]
The exact Einstein branch gives \(F(\omega_n,m,0)=0\), hence \(F_m=0\)
at the origin.  Define
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \nu_n=-\frac{2F_{\omega m}}{F_{\omega\omega}},
 \qquad
 c_n=\frac{2F_\epsilon}{F_{\omega\omega}}.
 \;}
\]
For a declared filtration-breaking direction with \(F_\epsilon\ne0\),
the Weierstrass-reduced characteristic equation is
\[
 \boxed{\;
 z^2-\nu_nmz+c_n\epsilon
 +O(z^3,z^2m,zm^2,z\epsilon,m\epsilon,m^3,\epsilon^2)=0.
 \;}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{\;
 z_\pm=
 \frac{\nu_nm}{2}
 \pm\frac12\sqrt{\nu_n^2m^2-4c_n\epsilon}
 +\cdots,
 \qquad
 \Delta^2=\nu_n^2m^2-4c_n\epsilon+\cdots.
 \;}
\]
The pair \((\nu_n,c_n)\) is invariant under multiplication
\(F\mapsto uF\) by an analytic nonzero unit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taylor expansion gives
\[
 F=
 \frac12F_{\omega\omega}z^2+F_{\omega m}zm
 +F_\epsilon\epsilon+\text{higher terms}.
\]
Division by \(F_{\omega\omega}/2\) gives the displayed characteristic
equation.  If \(\widetilde F=uF\), the identities
\(F=F_\omega=F_m=0\) imply
\[
 \widetilde F_{\omega\omega}=uF_{\omega\omega},
 \qquad
 \widetilde F_{\omega m}=uF_{\omega m},
 \qquad
 \widetilde F_\epsilon=uF_\epsilon
\]
at the critical point, so both ratios are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lidskii reverse-coupling coefficient]
\label{thm:lidskii-coefficient}

 \boxed{\;

 \;}

\(V_1\mapsto V_1+\lambda V_0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use \(z=O(\epsilon^{1/2})\) and
\(V=V_0+zV_1+z^2V_2+\cdots\).  At order \(z^2\sim\epsilon\), pairing the
expanded equation with \(W_0\) gives

 d_nz^2+\epsilon\langle W_0,BV_0\rangle=0.
\]
Thus \(z^2=-c_n\epsilon\), agreeing with
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-parameter-unfolding} at \(m=0\).  The shift of
\(V_1\) changes \(d_n\) by
\(\lambda\langle W_0,\mathbb L_n'V_0\rangle=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exceptional parabola and branch monodromy]
\label{cor:exceptional-parabola}
To leading order, the defective locus is

\begin{theorem}[Gap-controlled projectors, nilpotent, and metrics]
\label{thm:gap-controlled-confluence}

\[
 v_\pm=\begin{pmatrix}1\\z_\pm\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 w_\pm^{\mathsf T}=

Then
\[
 w_\pm^{\mathsf T}v_\pm
 =2z_\pm-\nu_nm=\pm\Delta
\]
and the spectral projectors are
\[
 \boxed{\;
 P_\pm=\frac{v_\pm w_\pm^{\mathsf T}}{\pm\Delta},
 \qquad
 \|P_\pm\|\asymp|\Delta|^{-1}.
 \;}
\]
In the normalized critical basis,
\[
 \boxed{\;
 N=
 \lim_{(m,\epsilon)\to(0,0)}
 \frac{\Delta}{2}(P_+-P_-)
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \;}
\]
This limit is independent of the approach path through points with
\(\Delta\ne0\), provided no additional spectral degeneracy is introduced.

Let
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\z_+&z_-\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(\det S=-\Delta\), one has
\[
 \operatorname{cond}S\asymp|\Delta|^{-1}.
\]
For any uniformly bounded and boundedly invertible positive diagonal
branch metric \(H_{\rm br}\), its pullback
\[
 H=(S^{-1})^\dagger H_{\rm br}S^{-1}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \operatorname{cond}H\asymp|\Delta|^{-2}.
 \;}
\]
Thus the physical mass axis gives projector growth \(O(|m|^{-1})\) and
metric conditioning \(O(|m|^{-2})\), whereas the transverse mixing axis
gives \(O(|\epsilon|^{-1/2})\) and \(O(|\epsilon|^{-1})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct multiplication gives
\(T(z_\pm;m,\epsilon)v_\pm=0\) and
\(w_\pm^{\mathsf T}T(z_\pm;m,\epsilon)=0\) to leading order.  The
biorthogonal projector formula gives \(P_\pm\).  Moreover,
\[
 \frac{\Delta}{2}(P_+-P_-)
 =\frac12
 \left(v_+w_+^{\mathsf T}+v_-w_-^{\mathsf T}\right)
 \longrightarrow N.
\]
The smallest singular value of \(S\) is comparable to \(|\Delta|\), while
the largest remains bounded above and below.  Pullback of a uniformly
positive branch metric squares this singular-value ratio.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Root-space polarization and nilpotent pole]
\label{thm:root-polarization}
Let
\[
 \mathbb L(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}L(\omega)&K(\omega)\\0&L(\omega)\end{pmatrix},
\]
with
\[
 L_nu_n=0,\qquad L_n^*\widetilde u_n=0,
\qquad
 \alpha_n=\langle\widetilde u_n,L_n'u_n\rangle\ne0,
\qquad
 \beta_n=\langle\widetilde u_n,K_nu_n\rangle\ne0.
\]
The right and left geometric root vectors are
\[
 \boxed{\;
 V_0=(u_n,0),\qquad
 W_0=(0,\widetilde u_n).
 \;}
\]
They are self-orthogonal in the full-pencil duality:
\[
 \langle W_0,V_0\rangle=0,
\qquad
 \langle W_0,\mathbb L_n'V_0\rangle=0.
\]
The first generalized right vector has carrier component
\[
 -\frac{\alpha_n}{\beta_n}u_n=-\frac1{\kappa_n}u_n.
\]
A left generalized vector can be chosen with Einstein-dual component
\(-(\alpha_n/\beta_n)\widetilde u_n\); both chains cross the filtration at
first generalized order.
The second-order Laurent coefficient is
\[
 \boxed{\;
 R_{-2}=
 -\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}V_0\otimes W_0,
 \qquad
 R_{-2}^2=0.
 \;}
\]
Its range is the Einstein resonant line and that line lies in its kernel.
Consequently \(R_{-2}\) is, up to its nonzero scalar normalization, the
nilpotent generator \(N\) of the dual-number commutant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \((x,cu_n)\in\ker\mathbb L_n\), then
\(L_nx+cK_nu_n=0\).  Pairing with \(\widetilde u_n\) gives
\(c\beta_n=0\), hence \(c=0\).  The adjoint argument gives the stated left
root.  For a right chain
\(\mathbb L_nV_1+\mathbb L_n'V_0=0\), write the carrier component of
\(V_1\) as \(\gamma u_n\).  The scalar solvability condition is
\(\gamma\beta_n+\alpha_n=0\).  The Laurent coefficient follows from the
block inverse.  Its square vanishes because
\((V_0\otimes W_0)^2=\langle W_0,V_0\rangle V_0\otimes W_0=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Confluent projectors and local contour]
\label{thm:confluent-projectors}
Normalize the massive vector by
\[

\]
and use the confluent basis \((u_0,v)\).  The branch-to-confluent matrix and

\]
\[

 \begin{pmatrix}1&-1/m\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 P_m(m)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1/m\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Hence
\[
 P_0+P_m=I,\qquad
 mP_0\longrightarrow-N,\qquad
 mP_m\longrightarrow N,
 \qquad
 N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and \(\|P_0\|,\|P_m\|\asymp |m|^{-1}\).
Equivalently, after normalizing the confluent coordinate by the spectral
separation \(\delta_m=\nu_nm+O(m^2)\), the projector scale is
\(O(|\delta_m|^{-1})=O(|\nu_nm|^{-1})\).

For a small contour enclosing only the two resonances, the local
meromorphic evolution contribution satisfies

 \boxed{\;
 \mathcal U_m(t)
 \longrightarrow
 e^{i\omega_nt}\left(I+i\nu_ntN\right).
 \;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The projector formulas are
\[
 P_0=S_m\diag(1,0)S_m^{-1},
 \qquad
 P_m=S_m\diag(0,1)S_m^{-1}.

Residue calculus on the local contour gives
\[
 \mathcal U_m(t)
 =e^{i\omega_nt}P_0(m)+e^{i\omega_m t}P_m(m)
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 e^{i\omega_nt}&
 \dfrac{e^{i\omega_mt}-e^{i\omega_nt}}{m}\\[3mm]

 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(\omega_m-\omega_n=\nu_nm+O(m^2)\), the off-diagonal entry tends to
\(i\nu_nt e^{i\omega_nt}\).
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:difference-quotient-poles} is the divided difference of

for analytic \(F\).  Taking \(F(\omega)=e^{i\omega t}\) gives the local

\begin{theorem}[Critical singularity of positive branch metrics]
\label{thm:critical-positive-metric}
For real signed \(m\ne0\), suppose a positive branch metric is
\[
 H_{\rm br}(m)=\diag(h_0(m),h_1(m)),
 \qquad
 0<h_*\le h_0,h_1\le h^*.
\]
In the confluent basis,
\[
 H_m=(S_m^{-1})^\dagger H_{\rm br}S_m^{-1}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 h_0&-h_0/m\\
 -h_0/m&(h_0+h_1)/m^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its condition number satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\;\operatorname{cond}(H_m)\asymp |m|^{-2}.\;}
\]
In the spectral-separation normalization this is
\(O(|\nu_nm|^{-2})\).
It is the physical-axis specialization of the universal gap law
\(\operatorname{cond}H\asymp|\Delta|^{-2}\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:gap-controlled-confluence}.
No scalar rescaling of \(H_m\) has a bounded, positive-definite,
nondegenerate critical limit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
One has
\[
 \det H_m=\frac{h_0h_1}{m^2},
\qquad
 \lambda_{\max}(H_m)=\frac{h_0+h_1}{m^2}+O(1),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(H_m)\longrightarrow
 \frac{h_0h_1}{h_0+h_1}>0.
\]
Scaling slower than \(m^2\) leaves the largest eigenvalue unbounded;
scaling like \(m^2\) gives a rank-one limit; and scaling faster gives zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The axial Schwarzschild Bach equation contains a repeated
Regge--Wheeler factor, but the repetition is not a rational direct sum.  It
is a non-split self-extension whose projective class is represented by the
elementary direction \((i\omega/2)(1-2/r)\).  That direction is exactly
the transverse-traceless critical mass jet.  The static mass-direction
class is nonzero for every radiative \(\ell\ge2\), while the dipole is
exact.  For the explicitly reduced \(\ell=2\) Bach cocycle this proves
exact threshold valuation one: the singular leading frame term is exact,
but the surviving linear class is not.  The equivalence gauge has
forced linear growth at infinity, but this is the derivative of the moving
massive phase rather than an obstruction: the Coulomb tail cancels its
apparent logarithmic shear.  At the certified damped mode, the scalar zero
is simple while the extension selector is nonzero.  The resulting defect
has concrete differential content: the generalized root has a nonzero
Ricci-carrier quotient, the compactly cut-off exterior outgoing Green
operator has a genuine rank-one double pole, its full two-layer coefficient
is nilpotent, and the massive spin-two QNM moves with nonzero critical
slope.
At the covariant level, the metric response is the mass derivative of the
massive spin-two resolvent: QNM motion controls its double coefficient,
while projector motion controls its accompanying simple coefficient.  The
second derivative of the massive QNM is fixed by the ordinary finite
Regge--Wheeler resolvent and the augmented resonant pairing, furnishing the
first finite correction to the confluent projector and contour limits.

The physical mass deformation is filtration-preserving rather than a
generic square-root unfolding.  In the two-parameter phase diagram, the
protected Einstein branch stays fixed and the mass axis splits linearly,
whereas reverse Einstein-to-carrier mixing produces square-root branching
and resonance exchange.  The actual spectral gap controls projector
growth and positive-metric conditioning, and recovering the physical slope
requires filtration-breaking error \(o(m^2)\).  The gap-renormalized
projector difference converges path-independently to the unique nilpotent
commutant generator.  Along the physical axis, the local two-pole contour
converges to the Jordan polynomial and the renormalized opposite-sign
branch form becomes the pure-Weyl hyperbolic plane.  A global causal
time-domain interpretation still requires the separate retarded contour
theory stated above.
