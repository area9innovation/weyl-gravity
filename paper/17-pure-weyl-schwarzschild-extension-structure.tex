\begin{abstract}
We determine the differential-module and resonance structure of axial
Schwarzschild perturbations in four-dimensional pure Weyl gravity.  The
six-state Bach system has an exact filtration with successive quotients
given by two spin-two Regge--Wheeler modules and one spin-one Maxwell
module.  The repeated spin-two block is a non-split self-extension and is
the first jet of a four-state spin-two/spin-one family.

After Liouville reduction, the extension is represented by a scalar
projective cocycle modulo the symmetric-square operator
\[
 \mathcal K_U=D^3+4UD+2D(U).
\]
For axial \(\ell=2\) we prove the exact normal form
\[
 [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
 =\frac{i\omega}{2}\left[1-\frac2r\right].
\]
The class in brackets is exactly the transverse-traceless critical
spin-two mass jet.  Thus the local parameter relation is
\(m=(i\omega/2)\tau\), modulo rational triangular gauge.  The required
gauge grows linearly at infinity; an explicit boundary transgression
therefore remains before the globally normalized Jost slopes can be
identified.

At a simple spin-two quasinormal frequency the complete connection admits
two possible local Smith types.  A validated projective Evans calculation
selects the defective type \((0,0,2)\) at one damped Schwarzschild mode.
The geometric root vector is Einstein, whereas its generalized root vector
has nonzero projection to the Ricci-carrier quotient.  We further show that
the compact-source, compact-observation outgoing Green operator on the
Schwarzschild exterior has a nonzero rank-one pole of order two at this
frequency.

Promotion to a global physical resolvent and to a time-domain generalized
ringdown term requires a separate analytic Fredholm realization, weighted
endpoint domains, and bounded metric reconstruction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}

Pure Weyl gravity has a fourth-order metric equation, but its Schwarzschild
perturbations are not best understood as an unstructured fourth-order
system.  In the axial \(\ell=2\) sector the six-dimensional radial system
has the filtered form
\[
 0\subset M_2\subset E_2\subset M_{\rm Bach},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{gr}M_{\rm Bach}\simeq M_2\oplus M_2\oplus M_1,
\]
where \(M_2\) is the spin-two Regge--Wheeler module and \(M_1\) is the
spin-one Maxwell module.  The middle object \(E_2\) is a self-extension
\[
 0\longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow E_2
 \longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
The decisive point is that this extension is non-split over the rational
differential field.  The two spin-two layers therefore share scalar
characteristics without defining two rationally separable radial
branches.

This paper establishes four central results.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The repeated spin-two block is a non-split Regge--Wheeler
  self-extension, realized exactly as a parameter jet.
\item Its projective extension class has the elementary normal form
  \[
    [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
    =\frac{i\omega}{2}\left[1-\frac2r\right].
  \]
\item The class \([1-2/r]\) is exactly the transverse-traceless critical
  mass jet.  The local equivalence gauge has forced linear growth at
  infinity, and its failure to preserve Jost frames is an explicit
  boundary Wronskian.
\item At a certified damped Schwarzschild quasinormal frequency the
  connection is defective.  The associated generalized root vector has a
  nonzero carrier quotient, and the exterior cut-off outgoing Green
  operator has a genuine rank-one double pole.
\end{enumerate}

The third result is stronger than a statement about a finite connection
matrix.  It is a meromorphic differential-response theorem for compact
radial sources and local radial observations.  It is deliberately weaker
than a causal spacetime resolvent theorem: no inverse-Laplace deformation,
late-time expansion, or \(t e^{i\omega_nt}\) assertion is made here.

\section{Differential-module conventions}

We work over
\[
 K=\C(r,\omega),\qquad D=f\partial_r,\qquad f=1-\frac2r,
\]
with \(\omega\ne0\) unless a threshold limit is stated explicitly.  All
rational gauges and module morphisms are over the differential field
\((K,D)\).  We use \(D(q)\), \(D(U)\), and \(D^3q\), rather than primes, to
avoid confusing the tortoise derivative with \(\partial_r\).

The axial spin-two and spin-one scalar operators are
\[
 L_2=D^2+U_2,\qquad
 U_2=\omega^2-f\left(\frac6{r^2}-\frac6{r^3}\right),
\]
\[
 L_1=D^2+U_1,\qquad
 U_1=\omega^2-f\frac6{r^2}.
\]
The latter is the \(\ell=2\) Maxwell Regge--Wheeler equation.  A
rank-two scalar module is represented by its companion system on
\((y,Dy)^T\).

The source Einstein layer is the kernel of the linearized Ricci/Schouten
carrier map.  The target spin-two quotient is its physical
Regge--Wheeler carrier.  The third quotient is the Maxwell class of the
target Einstein gauge vector.  Consequently, ``non-Einstein'' below means
nonzero projection to the gauge-invariant carrier quotient, not failure of
a coordinate gauge condition.

\section{Exact axial RW/RW/Maxwell filtration}

In the certified factor gauge the six-state system is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:six-state}
\begin{aligned}
 DX&=AX+EY+CZ,\\
 DY&=AY+DZ,\\
 DZ&=A_1Z,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(X,Y,Z\in K^2\), the companion matrices \(A\) and \(A_1\) represent
\(L_2\) and \(L_1\), and the forcing blocks factor through a single scalar
source:
\[
 E=USJ,\qquad C=USN.
\]
The rational reconstruction has full row rank on the declared domain.

\begin{theorem}[Filtered axial Bach module]\label{thm:filtration}
For axial \(\ell=2\) Schwarzschild perturbations, the reduced six-state
Bach module has a filtration whose successive simple factors are
\[
 M_2,\qquad M_2,\qquad M_1.
\]
The middle two-spin-two block is an extension \(E_2\) of \(M_2\) by
\(M_2\).  The quotient \(M_1\) is the Maxwell module of the carrier gauge
vector.  The filtration is exact but is not asserted to be a direct sum.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is an exact rational statement.  It is independent of endpoint
normalization, scattering conventions, and the later QNM specialization.

\section{Partial-jet realization}

Introduce the four-state family
\[
 \mathcal B(\tau)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A+\tau E&D+\tau C\\
 0&A_1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let its fundamental matrix be
\[
 \Phi_4(\tau)=
 \begin{pmatrix}P(\tau)&Q(\tau)\\0&R\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact partial jet]\label{thm:partial-jet}
The six-state generator in \eqref{eq:six-state} is the first
spin-two-row jet of \(\mathcal B(\tau)\) at \(\tau=0\).  Its fundamental
matrix is
\[
 \Phi_6=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 P&\dot P&\dot Q\\
 0&P&Q\\
 0&0&R
 \end{pmatrix}_{\tau=0},
\qquad
 \det\Phi_6=(\det P)^2\det R.
\]
Consequently any factor-adapted endpoint connection has the triangular
form
\[
 T(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a&b&c\\
 0&a&d\\
 0&0&g
 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 \det T=a^2g.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \(D\Phi_4(\tau)=\mathcal B(\tau)\Phi_4(\tau)\) at
\(\tau=0\).  The equations for \(P,\dot P,Q,\dot Q,R\) reproduce the
three rows of \eqref{eq:six-state} column by column.  The determinant
identity follows from block triangularity.  The same identity applied to
endpoint transition matrices gives the displayed connection form.
\end{proof}

The parameter \(\tau\) is intrinsic at this stage.  Identifying it with a
physical Fierz--Pauli mass requires an additional finite-mass reduction and
compatible endpoint germs; Section~\ref{sec:mass} records the exact
remaining gate.

\section{Projective gauge and symmetric-square cohomology}

Consider a scalar self-extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-extension}
 Lx=(s_1D+s_0)y,\qquad Ly=0,\qquad L=D^2+U.
\end{equation}
Set
\[
 \mathcal I=s_0-\frac12D(s_1),\qquad
 \mathcal K_U=D^3+4UD+2D(U).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Explicit triangular gauge]\label{lem:gauge}
For \(q\in K\), define
\[
 Q(q)=qD-\frac12D(q).
\]
On every solution \(Ly=0\),
\[
 [L,Q(q)]y=-\frac12\mathcal K_U(q)y.
\]
Thus the change of lift \(x\mapsto x+Q(q)y\) changes the scalar
extension density by
\[
 \mathcal I\longmapsto
 \mathcal I-\frac12\mathcal K_U(q).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand \(LQ(q)-Q(q)L\), use the Leibniz rule for \(D\), and then set
\(D^2y=-Uy\).  The coefficient of \(Dy\) cancels, while the coefficient
of \(y\) is
\(-\frac12[D^3q+4UDq+2D(U)q]\).
\end{proof}

The extension is therefore represented by
\[
 [\mathcal I]\in K/\mathcal K_UK.
\]
Because Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} contains the factor \(1/2\), removal of a
term \(\mathcal K_Uq\) uses the triangular gauge \(Q(2q)\).  This factor
does not alter the cohomology quotient but matters in an explicit frame
calculation.

The equation \(\mathcal K_Uq=0\) is the symmetric-square equation of
\(L\).  If \(y_1,y_2\) span the scalar solution space, then its solution
space is spanned by \(y_1^2,y_1y_2,y_2^2\).  The same operator controls
both the projective obstruction and trace-free horizontal endomorphisms.

\section{Mass-direction normal form of the Bach cocycle}

For the axial \(\ell=2\) Bach extension, rational reduction gives
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}
 =
 \frac{i(r-2)(2r\omega^2+3\omega^2+12)}
 {5r^4\omega}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Mass-direction normal form]\label{thm:mass-normal}
Let
\[
 q(r,\omega)=
 -\frac{i}{120\omega}
 \left(15r+13+\frac{12}{r}+\frac9{r^2}\right).
\]
For \(\omega\ne0\),
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}
 =\mathcal K_{U_2}q+\frac{i\omega}{2}f.
\]
Consequently
\[
 [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
 =\frac{i\omega}{2}[f]
 \quad\hbox{in}\quad K/\mathcal K_{U_2}K.
\]
In particular, \([f]\ne0\) wherever the Bach self-extension is
non-split.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \(D=f\partial_r\) three times to \(q\) and substitute
\[
 U_2=\omega^2-f\left(\frac6{r^2}-\frac6{r^3}\right)
\]
in \(\mathcal K_{U_2}q=D^3q+4U_2Dq+2D(U_2)q\).  Clearing the common
denominator \(120r^4\omega\) reduces the claim to a polynomial identity
in \(r\) and \(\omega\).  Exact coefficient comparison gives the displayed
formula.  The quotient identity follows immediately.  If \([f]=0\), then
\([\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]=0\), which would split the extension.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Threshold scope]
The displayed \(1/\omega\) singularity of
\(\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}\) is cohomologically removable: its intrinsic
class has the representative \((i\omega/2)f\).  The removing gauge
\(Q(2q)\), however, is itself \(O(\omega^{-1})\).  The theorem therefore
does not construct a threshold-uniform splitting or justify commuting the
limits \(\omega\to0\) and \(\tau\to0\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Mass-shaped is not yet physical mass]
The representative \(f=1-2/r\) is the radial direction expected from a
mass term after a helicity-two reduction.  The theorem proves a local
cohomology normal form, not equality with a particular Einstein--Weyl mass
parameter.  If a finite-mass reduction gives
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm mass}=c(\omega)f+\mathcal K_{U_2}p,
\]
then the two extensions are locally equivalent precisely when
\(c(\omega)\ne0\); their parameter normalization is
\(2c(\omega)/(i\omega)\).
\end{remark}

\section{Period formula for the connection shear}

Let \(y_1,y_2\) be a scalar basis and set
\[
 Y=
 \begin{pmatrix}y_1&y_2\\Dy_1&Dy_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 W=y_1Dy_2-y_2Dy_1.
\]
Because the companion matrix has zero trace, \(D(W)=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Symmetric-square period matrix]\label{thm:period}
For the family
\[
 (D^2+U+\tau V)y=0,
\]
the interaction-picture variation satisfies
\[
 Y^{-1}\partial_\tau A\,Y
 =
 \frac{V}{W}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 y_1y_2&y_2^2\\
 -y_1^2&-y_1y_2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently the spin-two connection shear is determined by the three
periods
\[
 \int\frac{V}{W}y_1^2\,dr_*,
 \qquad
 \int\frac{V}{W}y_1y_2\,dr_*,
 \qquad
 \int\frac{V}{W}y_2^2\,dr_*.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the basis \((y,Dy)^T\),
\[
 A(\tau)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-(U+\tau V)&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 \partial_\tau A=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\-V&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Insert
\[
 Y^{-1}=\frac1W
 \begin{pmatrix}Dy_2&-y_2\\-Dy_1&y_1\end{pmatrix}
\]
and multiply.  Integration of the interaction-picture equation gives the
three entries.
\end{proof}

A coboundary \(\mathcal K_Uq\) changes these periods by endpoint terms.
This is the connection-level form of Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}: rational
triangular gauges alter Jost frames but not the intrinsic divisor or Smith
selector once the endpoint units are tracked.

\section{Nonsplitting, endomorphisms, and Fitting ideals}

\begin{theorem}[Non-split physical-axis extension]\label{thm:nonsplit}
For every real \(\omega>0\), the axial \(\ell=2\) spin-two block
\[
 0\longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow E_2
 \longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow0
\]
is non-split over \(\C(r)[D]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Certificate-supported exact proof]
The rational splitting equation reduces, after exhaustive pole and degree
bounds, to a finite coefficient system.  Fixed minors of its coefficient
and augmented matrices are
\[
 \det M_{[0,1,2]}=3456\,\omega^{10},
\qquad
 \det[M\mid{\rm rhs}]_{[0,1,2,5]}
 =-645120\,i\,\omega^9.
\]
They are simultaneously nonzero for every real \(\omega>0\), so the
inhomogeneous coboundary equation is inconsistent.  The fixed-minor
argument also covers the former frame event \(\omega^2=3\).
\end{proof}

The scalar Regge--Wheeler module is simple on the positive real axis and
has scalar rational endomorphism ring.  The only nonzero complex
reducibility points allowed by the terminal Riccati ansatz with matching
horizon and infinity signs are the algebraically special frequencies
\[
 \omega=\pm i\frac{(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{12}.
\]
For \(\ell=2\), these are \(\omega=\pm2i\), away from the physical axis.

\begin{theorem}[Local commutant]\label{thm:commutant}
For real \(\omega>0\),
\[
 \End_{\C(r)[D]}(E_2)
 \simeq\C[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Every endomorphism is \(aI+bN\), where \(N^2=0\).  Hence the only
idempotents are \(0,I\), the only involutions are \(\pm I\), and every
diagonalizable local endomorphism is scalar.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The submodule \(M_2\) is the unique simple submodule: a second simple
submodule mapping nontrivially to the quotient would split the extension.
An endomorphism therefore induces scalars \(a,d\) on the submodule and
quotient.  Naturality of the nonzero extension class gives \(a=d\).
Subtracting \(aI\) leaves a map that vanishes on the submodule and quotient,
so it factors through the quotient back into the submodule and is a scalar
multiple of the canonical nilpotent \(N\).  The corollaries follow from
\((aI+bN)^k=a^kI+ka^{k-1}bN\).
\end{proof}

For the triangular connection
\[
 T=
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b&c\\0&a&d\\0&0&g\end{pmatrix},
\]
the Fitting ideals are
\[
 I_1=(a,b,c,d,g),\qquad
 I_2=(a^2,ad,bd-ac,ag,bg),\qquad
 I_3=(a^2g).
\]
They give a normalization-independent local classification of all
specializations.

\section{Analytic Jost connections and Smith dichotomy}

The moving Frobenius/Jost phases define analytic endpoint-selected planes
on the certified spectral domains.  Transport to a common ordinary radius
and change to a factor-adapted frame.  At a simple spin-two QNM
\(\omega_n\), assume the spin-one coefficient \(g(\omega_n)\ne0\).  Analytic
row and column operations remove the spin-one row and column, leaving
\[
 T_2(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}a(\omega)&b(\omega)\\0&a(\omega)\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 a(\omega_n)=0,\quad a'(\omega_n)\ne0.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Local Smith dichotomy]\label{prop:smith}
At \(\omega_n\),
\[
 \Smith(T)=
 \begin{cases}
 (0,0,2),&b(\omega_n)\ne0,\\
 (0,1,1),&b(\omega_n)=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The first case has geometric multiplicity one and one length-two root
chain; the second has two independent length-one roots.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(z=\omega-\omega_n\).  Since \(a=a_1z+O(z^2)\) with
\(a_1\ne0\), the determinant valuation of the two-spin-two block is two.
If \(b(0)\ne0\), its first Fitting ideal is the unit ideal, so its exponents
are \((0,2)\).  If \(b(0)=0\), all entries vanish to first order and the
exponents are \((1,1)\).  Appending the spin-one unit adds a zero exponent.
\end{proof}

Projectively, write the selected endpoint lines in a common chart as
\[
 v_H(\omega,\tau),\qquad v_\infty(\omega,\tau),
\qquad
 \delta=v_\infty-v_H.
\]
At a simple root,
\[
 \frac{b(\omega_n)}{a'(\omega_n)}
 =
 \frac{\delta_\tau(\omega_n,0)}
 {\delta_\omega(\omega_n,0)}.
\]
This ratio is invariant under analytic nonzero endpoint units and under
moving the matching section.

\section{Resonant evaluation of the self-extension}
\label{sec:resonant-evaluation}

The projective selector, adjoint overlap, root-chain coefficient, and
Green-pole residue are different representations of one resonant
functional.  This is the finite-interval Evans/Melnikov mechanism; compare
the general relation between Evans derivatives and adjoint generalized
eigenfunctions in \cite{kapitula1999}.

Let
\[
 L(\omega):X\longrightarrow Y
\]
denote the analytic finite-interval spin-two boundary pencil, with the
endpoint conditions included either in its domain or as finite-dimensional
components of an augmented range.  At a simple resonance choose right and
left states
\[
 L_nu_n=0,\qquad L_n^*\widetilde u_n=0,
\qquad L_n=L(\omega_n),
\]
and define, using the bilinear duality of the augmented boundary problem,
\[
 \alpha_n=
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,L'(\omega_n)u_n\right\rangle,
 \qquad \alpha_n\ne0.
\]
Let the repeated block be
\[
 \mathbb L(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}L(\omega)&K(\omega)\\0&L(\omega)\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 \beta_n=
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,K(\omega_n)u_n\right\rangle.
\]
All endpoint components of the augmented pencil and its duality are
included in these pairings.

\begin{theorem}[Resonant evaluation theorem]
\label{thm:resonant-evaluation}
In endpoint frames compatible with the partial jet,
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \kappa_n:=
 \frac{b(\omega_n)}{a'(\omega_n)}
 =
 \frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n}
 =
 -\omega_n'(0).
 \;}
\]
Here \(\omega_n(\tau)\) is the resonance branch of
\[
 L(\omega,\tau)=L(\omega)+\tau K(\omega)
\]
through \(\omega_n\).  The equality is invariant under a common analytic
nonzero Evans unit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate
\[
 L(\omega_n(\tau),\tau)u(\tau)=0
\]
at \(\tau=0\):
\[
 L_n\dot u+\omega_n'(0)L_n'u_n+K_nu_n=0.
\]
Pairing with \(\widetilde u_n\) removes the first term and gives
\[
 \omega_n'(0)\alpha_n+\beta_n=0.
\]
On the connection side, differentiation of
\[
 a(\omega_n(\tau),\tau)=0
\]
gives
\[
 a'(\omega_n)\omega_n'(0)
 +\partial_\tau a(\omega_n,0)=0.
\]
The exact partial-jet construction identifies
\(\partial_\tau a(\omega_n,0)=b(\omega_n)\).  Multiplication of the Evans
coefficient by an analytic unit \(u(\omega,\tau)\) multiplies both
\(a'\) and \(b\) at the root by \(u(\omega_n,0)\), so the ratio is
unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Resonant functional on the extension class]
\label{prop:resonant-functional}
The assignment
\[
 \mathfrak M_n([K])
 :=
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,K(\omega_n)u_n\right\rangle
\]
is a well-defined linear functional on the local self-extension class.
Thus nonsplitting is the statement \([K]\ne0\), while defectiveness at
\(\omega_n\) is the sharper statement
\(\mathfrak M_n([K])\ne0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under a triangular change of splitting,
\[
 K\longmapsto K+LQ-QL.
\]
At the resonance,
\[
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,(L_nQ-QL_n)u_n\right\rangle
 =
 \left\langle L_n^*\widetilde u_n,Qu_n\right\rangle
 -
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,QL_nu_n\right\rangle
 =0.
\]
When endpoint conditions are encoded in an augmented pencil, the same
calculation includes the corresponding boundary terms by definition.
\end{proof}

The theorem and proposition separate a global module property from its
evaluation at one resonance.  A nonzero extension class need not be
defective at every QNM: the linear functional \(\mathfrak M_n\) may vanish
at isolated modes.

\section{The generalized root vector is non-Einstein}

\begin{proposition}[Nonzero carrier in the generalized root]
\label{prop:carrier-root}
Assume
\[
 a(\omega_n)=0,\qquad a'(\omega_n)\ne0,\qquad
 b(\omega_n)\ne0.
\]
The geometric root vector belongs to the source Einstein submodule, while
every length-two root polynomial has first generalized coefficient with
nonzero projection to the carrier quotient.  In the normalized triangular
frame that projection is
\[
 -\frac{a'(\omega_n)}{b(\omega_n)}
 =
 -\frac{\alpha_n}{\beta_n}
 =
 -\frac1{\kappa_n}.
\]
Thus the defective doubled resonance is not two copies of an Einstein
quasinormal mode: its generalized member is intrinsically non-Einstein.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(z=\omega-\omega_n\) and write
\[
 a=a_1z+O(z^2),\qquad b=b_0+O(z),
\qquad a_1b_0\ne0.
\]
For
\[
 c(z)=c_0+zc_1+O(z^2),\qquad T_2(z)c(z)=O(z^2),
\]
the order-zero equation gives \(c_0=e_X\), the source Einstein vector.
Write \(c_1=xe_X+ye_Y\).  The \(e_X\) component at order \(z\) is
\[
 a_1+b_0y=0,
\]
so \(y=-a_1/b_0\ne0\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration}, \(e_Y\) projects
nontrivially to the Ricci-carrier quotient.  Adding a multiple of \(e_X\)
changes \(x\), but cannot remove \(y\).
\end{proof}

\section{Exterior cut-off outgoing Green double pole}

Fix ordinary radii \(2<r_H<r_I<\infty\) inside the certified analytic
continuation domains.  Let
\[
 H(\omega):\C^3\longrightarrow
 C^\infty([r_H,r_I];\C^6)
\]
be the analytic Poisson operator for horizon-selected homogeneous data,
let \(\Gamma_-(\omega)\) be the unwanted incoming trace at \(r_I\), and
set
\[
 T(\omega)=\Gamma_-(\omega)H(\omega).
\]
For a compactly supported source \(F\), let \(P_H(\omega)F\) denote the
inhomogeneous solution with zero horizon data.  The outgoing solution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:green}
 R_{\rm out}(\omega)F
 =
 P_H(\omega)F-
 H(\omega)T(\omega)^{-1}
 \Gamma_-(\omega)P_H(\omega)F.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-interval outgoing Green pole]\label{thm:green-pole}
On a neighborhood of the certified frequency \(\omega_n\), the operator
\[
 R_{\rm out}(\omega):
 C_c^\infty((r_H,r_I);\C^6)
 \longrightarrow
 C^\infty_{\rm loc}((r_H,r_I);\C^6)
\]
is meromorphic and has a nonzero rank-one pole of order two.  Up to the
endpoint trace sign convention, its principal coefficient is
\[
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{(\omega-\omega_n)^{-2}}R_{\rm out}
 =
 \frac{b(\omega_n)}{a'(\omega_n)^2}
 H(\omega_n)e_X
 \otimes
 \left[e_Y^*\Gamma_-(\omega_n)P_H(\omega_n)\right].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All factors in \eqref{eq:green} except \(T^{-1}\) are analytic.  After
analytic elimination of the spin-one unit, the singular block is
\[
 T_2^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a^{-1}&-ba^{-2}\\
 0&a^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{z^{-2}}T_2^{-1}
 =-\frac{b(\omega_n)}{a'(\omega_n)^2}
 e_X\otimes e_Y^*.
\]
The minus sign in \eqref{eq:green} gives the displayed convention.

The range vector is nonzero because the homogeneous Poisson map is
injective, so \(H(\omega_n)e_X\ne0\).  The covector is nonzero by a cutoff
Jost construction.  Given any incoming coefficient \(q\), choose the
corresponding incoming Jost solution \(J_-q\) near \(r_I\), multiply it by
a smooth cutoff \(\chi\) that vanishes near \(r_H\) and equals one near
\(r_I\), and set
\[
 F=(D-\mathbb A(\omega_n))(\chi J_-q).
\]
Then \(F\) is compactly supported, \(P_H(\omega_n)F=\chi J_-q\), and
\(\Gamma_-P_H(\omega_n)F=q\).  Hence the incoming trace map from compact
sources is onto.  Taking \(q=e_Y\) shows that the covector does not vanish.
The tensor product is therefore nonzero and rank one.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact transparent cuts}

Put \(x=r_*\).  On the certified QNM neighborhood, let
\[
 u_H(x,\omega),\qquad u_\infty(x,\omega)
\]
be analytic spin-two Jost solutions satisfying the future-horizon and
outgoing-infinity conditions.  The scalar Evans coefficient is, up to an
analytic unit,
\[
 a(\omega)=W(u_H,u_\infty).
\]
Let \(\chi_{\rm s}\) and \(\chi_{\rm o}\) be nontrivial smooth source and
observation cutoffs with compact support.  Choose finite cuts
\[
 x_-<
 \operatorname{supp}\chi_{\rm s}\cup
 \operatorname{supp}\chi_{\rm o}
 <x_+.
\]
At the two cuts impose the exact transparent boundary conditions
\[
 W(u,u_H)|_{x_-}=0,\qquad
 W(u,u_\infty)|_{x_+}=0.
\]
Their coefficients are analytic in \(\omega\).  Uniqueness of the exterior
Jost problems shows that the resulting finite-interval inverse agrees
exactly, between the cutoffs, with the meromorphically continued exterior
outgoing inverse.

\begin{theorem}[Exterior cut-off Green pole]
\label{thm:exterior-cutoff-pole}
For any nontrivial compactly supported source and observation cutoffs,
\[
 \chi_{\rm o}R_{\rm ext}(\omega)\chi_{\rm s}
\]
has a genuine nonzero rank-one pole of order two at the certified
Schwarzschild QNM.  In the augmented Fredholm normalization its principal
coefficient is
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{(\omega-\omega_n)^{-2}}
 \bigl(\chi_{\rm o}R_{\rm ext}\chi_{\rm s}\bigr)
 =
 -\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}
 (\chi_{\rm o}u_n)
 \otimes
 (\widetilde u_n\chi_{\rm s}).
 \;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The transparent conditions select exactly the restrictions of the
exterior horizon and infinity Jost solutions.  Hence
\[
 \chi_{\rm o}R_{\rm ext}(\omega)\chi_{\rm s}
 =
 \chi_{\rm o}R_{[x_-,x_+]}(\omega)\chi_{\rm s}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:green-pole} and
\eqref{eq:invariant-double-pole} give the displayed principal
coefficient.  It is nonzero: \(\beta_n\ne0\), while a nonzero solution of
a second-order ODE cannot vanish on a nonempty open interval.  Thus
\(\chi_{\rm o}u_n\) and
\(\widetilde u_n\chi_{\rm s}\) are nonzero for every nontrivial smooth
cutoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Non-annihilation by certified reconstruction]
The certified rational reconstruction into the axial metric/curvature
variables does not annihilate the range
\(H(\omega_n)e_X\).  Hence at least one reconstructed local observable has
the same order-two radial response pole.
\end{corollary}

In the finite-interval Fredholm normalization of
Section~\ref{sec:resonant-evaluation}, the scalar resolvent has
\[
 L(\omega)^{-1}
 =
 \frac{u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n}
 {\alpha_n(\omega-\omega_n)}
 +O(1).
\]
The upper-right block of \(\mathbb L^{-1}\) is
\(-L^{-1}KL^{-1}\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:resonant-evaluation} therefore
gives the invariant principal coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant-double-pole}
 \boxed{\;
 \operatorname{Coeff}_{(\omega-\omega_n)^{-2}}
 (\mathbb L^{-1})_{12}
 =
 -\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}
 u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n
 =
 -\frac{\kappa_n}{\alpha_n}
 u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n.
 \;}
\end{equation}
The Poisson/trace coefficient in Theorem~\ref{thm:green-pole} and the
exterior coefficient in Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-cutoff-pole} are the same
rank-one residue expressed in endpoint and augmented Fredholm coordinates.

\section{Conditional global Fredholm promotion}
\label{sec:fredholm}

Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-cutoff-pole} is an exterior differential
Green-operator theorem after compact source and observation localization.
A global physical resolvent without cutoffs requires a separate
closed-operator realization.  The correct abstract block notation is
\[
 \mathbb L(\omega)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 L(\omega)&K(\omega)\\
 0&L(\omega)
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(L(\omega):D\subset X\to Y\) is an analytic Fredholm pencil of
index zero and \(K(\omega):D\to Y\) is graph-norm bounded.  Suppose
\[
 u_n\in\ker L(\omega_n),\qquad
 \widetilde u_n\in\ker L(\omega_n)^*,
\]
and define using the \(Y^*\)-\(Y\) bilinear duality
\[
 \alpha_n=
 \langle\widetilde u_n,L'(\omega_n)u_n\rangle,
\qquad
 \beta_n=
 \langle\widetilde u_n,K(\omega_n)u_n\rangle.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Conditional global pole formula]
If \(L^{-1}\) is meromorphic near \(\omega_n\), its zero is simple,
\(\alpha_n\ne0\), and \(\beta_n\ne0\), then
\[
 \mathbb L^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 L^{-1}&-L^{-1}KL^{-1}\\
 0&L^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
has an order-two pole with principal coefficient
\[
 -\frac{\beta_n}{\alpha_n^2}
 u_n\otimes\widetilde u_n
\]
in the upper-right block.
\end{proposition}

This proposition is standard block algebra once the Fredholm spaces and
pairing exist.  What remains open is not the exterior cut-off pole proved
above, but the construction of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item closed weighted horizon and null-infinity domains;
\item a physical gauge quotient and bounded metric reconstruction;
\item a causal exterior resolvent on a Laplace contour;
\item a justified contour deformation and control of the non-pole part.
\end{enumerate}
Only after these steps could the double pole be promoted to a rigorous
\(t e^{i\omega_nt}\) contribution to retarded ringdown.

\section{Critical Einstein--Weyl mass comparison}
\label{sec:mass}

On the transverse-traceless spin-two sector, the quadratic
Einstein--Weyl parent equation is
\[
 (\mathcal E+m\mathcal A)\phi=0,
\]
where the parameter \(m\) has the dimensions of squared mass.  Its
Schwarzschild axial scalar reduction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:massive-rw}
 L_m(\omega)y=
 \left[D^2+\omega^2-V_2-mf\right]y=0,
 \qquad f=1-\frac2r.
\end{equation}
For a differentiable family \(y(m)\), differentiation at \(m=0\) gives
\[
 L_2\,\partial_my=f\,y.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact local critical-mass-jet identification]
\label{thm:mass-jet}
The projective cocycle of the transverse-traceless critical mass jet is
\[
 [\mathcal I_{\rm mass}]=[f].
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\;
 [\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]
 =\frac{i\omega}{2}[\mathcal I_{\rm mass}].
 \;}
\]
With the convention \(L_m=L_2-mf\), the local first-order parameter
relation is
\[
 \boxed{\;m=\frac{i\omega}{2}\tau\;}
\]
modulo rational triangular gauge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:massive-rw} gives the extension equation
\(L_2\partial_my=fy\), so its scalar projective density is \(f\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:mass-normal} gives
\([\mathcal I_{\rm Bach}]=(i\omega/2)[f]\).  Comparing the two tangent
equations yields the parameter relation.
\end{proof}

This theorem closes the local transverse-traceless differential-module
comparison.  It does not identify independently normalized global Jost
connections, because the equivalence gauge is not bounded at infinity.

\subsection{Forced asymptotics of the equivalence gauge}

The rational representative in Theorem~\ref{thm:mass-normal} satisfies
\[
 q(r,\omega)=-\frac{i}{8\omega}r+O(1),
 \qquad r\to\infty.
\]

\begin{proposition}[No bounded rational mass-equivalence gauge]
\label{prop:unbounded-gauge}
Every rational \(q\) satisfying
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}
 =\mathcal K_{U_2}q+\frac{i\omega}{2}f
\]
has the forced leading behavior
\[
 q(r,\omega)=-\frac{i}{8\omega}r+O(1).
\]
In particular, no bounded rational triangular gauge identifies the Bach
jet with the critical mass jet at infinity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At infinity,
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm Bach}-\frac{i\omega}{2}f
 \longrightarrow-\frac{i\omega}{2},
\qquad
 U_2\longrightarrow\omega^2.
\]
A rational \(q=o(r)\) is \(O(1)\) and gives
\(\mathcal K_{U_2}q=o(1)\), so it cannot produce the nonzero constant.
If \(q\sim cr\), then \(Dq\to c\), while \(D^3q\to0\) and
\(D(U_2)q\to0\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal K_{U_2}q\longrightarrow4\omega^2c.
\]
Matching the constant gives \(c=-i/(8\omega)\), uniquely.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Boundary transgression}

The normalization in Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} must be retained here.  Put

 Q(q)=qD-\frac12D(q),
\qquad

 K_{\rm Bach}-\frac{i\omega}{2}K_{\rm mass}
 =[L_2,\widehat Q]
\end{equation}
in the scalarized companion convention.  The factor \(-2\) is essential:
using \(Q(q)\) itself would be inconsistent with the cocycle normalization
of Theorem~\ref{thm:mass-normal}.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-cut boundary transgression]
\label{thm:boundary-transgression}
Let \(u_n,\widetilde u_n\) be resonant right and left states, with the
finite-cut pairing defined by Green's formula.  Then

 \right]_{x_-}^{x_+}.
 \;}
\]
In an augmented boundary pencil, the same term is absorbed into the
derivatives of the horizon and infinity boundary maps, restoring
splitting-gauge invariance of the complete overlap.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On finite cuts, use \eqref{eq:bulk-commutator}, \(L_2u_n=0\), and
\(L_2^\dagger\widetilde u_n=0\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,
 \left(K_{\rm Bach}-\frac{i\omega_n}{2}K_{\rm mass}\right)
 u_n\right\rangle
 &=
 \left\langle\widetilde u_n,[L_2,\widehat Q]u_n\right\rangle\\
 &=
 \left[W(\widetilde u_n,\widehat Q u_n)\right]_{x_-}^{x_+}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the finite-cut identity.  Equivalently, include the boundary
functionals in the augmented operator and its duality before
differentiation.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The axial Schwarzschild Bach equation contains a repeated
Regge--Wheeler factor, but the repetition is not a rational direct sum.  It
is a non-split self-extension whose projective class is represented by the
elementary direction \((i\omega/2)(1-2/r)\).  That direction is exactly
the transverse-traceless critical mass jet.  The equivalence gauge has
forced linear growth at infinity, so the remaining global normalization
question is one explicit endpoint Wronskian.  At the certified damped mode,
the scalar zero is simple while the extension selector is nonzero.  The
resulting defect has concrete differential content: the generalized root
has a nonzero Ricci-carrier quotient, and the compactly cut-off exterior
outgoing Green operator has a genuine rank-one double pole.

This separates three notions that should not be conflated.  A repeated
scalar divisor fixes algebraic multiplicity; the extension selector decides
whether that multiplicity is semisimple or defective; and a causal
time-domain interpretation requires a further global Fredholm theory.  The
first two questions are settled here at one physical Schwarzschild
resonance.  The third remains a sharply delimited next problem.
