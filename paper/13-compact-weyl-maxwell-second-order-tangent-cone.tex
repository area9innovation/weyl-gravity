\begin{abstract}
Linearized solutions of a nonlinear gauge theory need not be tangent to exact
solutions.  We classify this failure at second order for finite harmonic
configurations of Weyl--Maxwell gravity on the compact magnetically supported
product
\(
 \R_t\times S^1\times S^2.
\)
The certified linear inventory contains the ordinary Einstein--Maxwell
branches, two additional fourth-order branches in each generic parity,
exceptional dipoles, a homogeneous generalized block, and axial twist modes.

For corrections that are smooth, spatially periodic, and finite
exponential-polynomials in time, the complete second-order obstruction space
is five-dimensional.  Its covectors are precisely the Taub pairings with
time translation, circle translation, and the three sphere rotations.  Thus
the complete finite-support smooth tangent cone is
\[
 \cZ^{\rm smooth}_2
 =\{u:\mu_H(u)=\mu_{P_x}(u)=\mu_{J_1}(u)=
              \mu_{J_2}(u)=\mu_{J_3}(u)=0\}.
\]
This is a necessary-and-sufficient theorem in the declared correction class,
not merely a list of examples.

Bounded corrections obey additional conditions.  Polynomial-growth source
coefficients and nonzero-shell adjoint pairings survive independently of the
five moment maps.  We classify the complete standard generalized-zero
subcone, prove that electric-charge and flat-Wilson oscillator columns are
bounded-removable, and determine the full circumference column: a nonzero
circle-radius tangent is compatible with a finite oscillator precisely when
that oscillator has zero circle momentum.  A repaired full-time
circumference-velocity fixture also exposes a nonzero polar polynomial
obstruction that was invisible at \(t=0\).  The remaining bounded oscillator
columns are stated as open gates.  Consequently smooth second-order
extendibility is a moment-map theorem, whereas bounded extendibility is a
strict resonance problem.

All results are \textsc{local-algebraic} and \textsc{reduced-mode}.  They do
not establish an all-orders family, a causal retarded correction, a final
residual quotient, or a quantum state space.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, answer, and claim boundary}

Let \(\Phi(s)\) be a putative curve of solutions of the Weyl--Maxwell
equations \(\cE(\Phi)=0\), expanded as
\[
 \Phi(s)=\bar\Phi+s u+\frac{s^2}{2}v+O(s^3).
\]
The first two equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-order-equation}
 \cL u=0,
 \qquad
 \cL v=-\cE^{(2)}_{\bar\Phi}(u,u),
 \qquad
 \cL:=D\cE_{\bar\Phi}.
\end{equation}
The first equation describes the linear solution space.  The second asks
whether the quadratic source lies in the image of the linearized operator in
a specified function class.  Changing that function class changes the
answer: a resonant source may have a smooth secular primitive but no bounded
primitive.

The paper answers the following finite-harmonic question.

\begin{quote}
For the complete certified linear inventory on the compact product, which
real finite-support tangents admit a spatially periodic second-order
correction, and which additional conditions appear when that correction is
required to remain bounded in time?
\end{quote}

The answer has two layers:
\begin{align}
 u\in\cZ^{\rm smooth}_2
 &\Longleftrightarrow \mu_H=\mu_{P_x}=\mu_{J_1}=\mu_{J_2}=\mu_{J_3}=0,
 \label{eq:smooth-summary}\\
 u\in\cZ^{\rm bounded}_2
 &\Longrightarrow u\in\cZ^{\rm smooth}_2,
 \quad \cP_{j,r}(u)=0,
 \quad \cR_{j,a}(u)=0 .
 \label{eq:bounded-summary}
\end{align}
Here \(\cP_{j,r}\) are positive polynomial-growth coefficients and
\(\cR_{j,a}\) are reduced adjoint pairings on nonzero characteristic shells.
The first line is complete in the declared carrier.  The paper gives several
complete columns of the second line but does not claim the complete common
zero locus of all bounded obstructions.

\section{Theory and common background}

We use the compactified Pleba\'nski--Hacyan product
\begin{equation}
 M=\R_t\times S^1_L\times S^2,
\end{equation}
with unit sphere curvature and a parallel unit magnetic flux on a fixed
compact \(U(1)\) bundle.  With the conventions of the companion linear
phase-space paper, the background solves both Einstein--Maxwell and
Weyl--Maxwell theory.  The latter has local gauge group
\[
 \Diff(M)\times\mathrm{Weyl}(M)\times U(1).
\]

The spatial slice \(\Sigma=S^1\times S^2\) is compact.  Its background
stabilizer algebra contains
\[
 H=\partial_t,qquad P_x=\partial_x,qquad J_1,J_2,J_3\in\mathfrak{so}(3).
\]
For a linear tangent \(u\), the corresponding covariant phase-space moment
maps are quadratic forms
\[
 \mu_X(u)=\frac12\Omega(u,\mathcal L_Xu),
 \qquad X\in\{H,P_x,J_1,J_2,J_3\}.
\]
On a closed slice, the covariant Taub bridge identifies these with the
pairings of the quadratic field-equation source against the stabilizer
cokernel covectors.

\section{The complete finite harmonic carrier}

The real finite-support carrier \(V_{\rm fin}\) is the direct sum of the
following certified linear blocks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item generic Einstein and extra primaries for every \(\ell\ge2\), parity,
      magnetic label, and compact momentum;
\item standard and extra exceptional dipoles for every compact momentum;
\item the axial twist Jordan block;
\item the six-coordinate homogeneous generalized block; and
\item the electric and Wilson global directions on the fixed bundle.
\end{enumerate}
Reality pairs opposite frequencies and momenta.  A finite input therefore
produces a finite quadratic source.  Its complete output decomposition is
indexed by
\[
 (L,M,K,\Omega,\text{parity}).
\]
Angular Clebsch--Gordan selection gives finitely many \(L\), while temporal
and circle products give finite sums of input frequencies and momenta.

\begin{definition}[Correction classes]
Let \(\mathscr X_{\rm smooth}\) be the spatially periodic fields whose time
coefficients are finite sums of \(t^p e^{-i\Omega t}\).  Let
\(\mathscr X_{\rm bounded}\subset\mathscr X_{\rm smooth}\) be the subclass
with \(p=0\).  Define
\[
 \cZ_2^{\mathscr X}
 =\{u\in V_{\rm fin}:\exists v\in\mathscr X,
             \ \cL v=-\cE^{(2)}(u,u)\}.
\]
\end{definition}

The distinction is physical as well as analytic.  A secular coefficient is
smooth for every finite time but prevents a uniformly bounded perturbative
expansion.  It may signal a nearby family with shifted frequency, a required
renormalization of parameters, or genuine resonant growth.  The certificate
records only the exact second-order disposition.

\section{Smooth tangent-cone theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Complete finite-support smooth tangent cone]
\label{thm:smooth-cone}
For the complete carrier \(V_{\rm fin}\) above,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \cZ^{\rm smooth}_2
 =\{u\in V_{\rm fin}:\mu_H(u)=\mu_{P_x}(u)=
    \mu_{J_1}(u)=\mu_{J_2}(u)=\mu_{J_3}(u)=0\}.}
\end{equation}
Every point of this cone admits a spatially periodic second-order correction
whose time dependence is a finite exponential-polynomial.  Conversely, each
of the five moment maps must vanish for such a correction to exist.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Proof architecture}

The proof is blockwise but exhaustive.

\paragraph{Nonzero output blocks.}
Direct exceptional Fourier complexes and generic Smith modules remove every
off-shell output algebraically.  On a characteristic shell, the scalar
factor admits a finite secular primitive.  Thus resonance does not create a
new cokernel in \(\mathscr X_{\rm smooth}\).

\paragraph{Homogeneous zero block.}
The only potentially non-surjective rows reduce to ordinary polynomial
operators.  The operators \(D_t^4\) and \(D_t^2\) are surjective on the
finite source polynomials generated at quadratic order, whose degree is at
most six.

\paragraph{Axial dipole zero block.}
After gauge and Noether reduction, the only surviving covectors are the
three lifted rotations.  The homogeneous zero block supplies the time and
circle translations.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \coker\cL_{\rm smooth}
 =\operatorname{span}\{\zeta_H,\zeta_{P_x},
       \zeta_{J_1},\zeta_{J_2},\zeta_{J_3}\}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Covariant identification.}
The exact Taub bridge identifies
\(\langle\zeta_X,\cE^{(2)}(u,u)\rangle\) with \(\mu_X(u)\).
This proves both necessity and sufficiency.

\begin{remark}
The theorem is stronger than checking a chosen set of modes.  It includes
arbitrary finite sums across the certified generic, exceptional, homogeneous,
and twist blocks.  It is weaker than an infinite-mode Sobolev theorem and
weaker than causal solvability.
\end{remark}

\section{What changes in the bounded category}

On \(\mathscr X_{\rm bounded}\), differentiation cannot invert a positive
power of \(t\), and a resonant exponential has no bounded primitive.  The
obstruction ledger consequently enlarges to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-cokernel}
 \{\mu_H,\mu_{P_x},\mu_{J_i}\}
 \cup\{\cP_{j,r}\}
 \cup\{\cR_{j,a}\}.
\end{equation}
The certificates contain fixtures in which the last two families are
independent of all five moment maps.  Hence the smooth cone is strictly
larger than the bounded cone.

\begin{theorem}[Standard generalized-zero bounded cone]
On the complete standard generalized-zero carrier with homogeneous
coordinates \((a,b,c,d,Q_e,W_x)\) and axial twist position/velocity
\((A,B)\), bounded second-order extension holds exactly on
\begin{equation}
 a=b=Q_e=0,qquad B=0,qquad
 c,d,W_x\ \text{arbitrary},qquad A\in\R^3\ \text{arbitrary}.
\end{equation}
The surviving source has an explicit bounded correction.
\end{theorem}

The triangular positive-degree ideal supplies the proof.  Its leading rows
contain
\[
 \frac{15}{2}b^2t^2,qquad
 \operatorname{STF}(B\otimes B)t^2,qquad
 Q_e a\,t.
\]
They force \(b=B=0\) and \(Q_ea=0\).  Intersecting with the five moment maps
then forces \(a=Q_e=0\) on this carrier.  The remaining homogeneous source
vanishes, while constant twist position is tangent to an exact lifted
mapping-torus family and has a constant polar correction.

\begin{corollary}[Universal polynomial elimination]
For every point of the complete finite-support bounded cone,
\[
 b=0,qquad B=0,qquad Q_ea=0.
\]
Oscillatory products cannot cancel these zero-frequency polynomial rows.
\end{corollary}

\section{Natural transport columns}

Several apparently dangerous global--oscillator products are derivatives of
exact families and can therefore be classified without solving each harmonic
source independently.

\begin{proposition}[Electric and Wilson columns]
For every certified nonzero-frequency standard or extra primary, the
electric-charge cross source has a bounded correction obtained by
differentiating electromagnetic duality,
\[
 F_\theta=\cos\theta F+\sin\theta\,{\star_g F}.
\]
At the background,
\[
 h_{\rm cross}=0,qquad
 f_{\rm cross}=\star_{\bar g}f+(D_g\star)[h]F_{\rm bg}.
\]
Its sphere period vanishes for \(\ell\ge1\), so it lifts globally on the
fixed bundle.  Every Wilson--oscillator source vanishes because
\(\delta A=W_xdx\) has \(\delta F=0\) and the local action depends on the
connection through \(F\).
\end{proposition}

This removes the complete \(Q_e\) and \(W_x\) oscillator columns from the
bounded frontier.  It does not remove the separate homogeneous condition
\(Q_ea=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Complete circumference column]
Let \(c\) vary the circle radius and let a certified oscillator have compact
momentum \(k\).  The \(c\)-times-oscillator source admits a bounded correction
if and only if
\[
 c=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad k=0.
\]
For \(k\ne0\), the radius family gives a smooth secular correction but no
bounded one.
\end{theorem}

Indeed, writing \(R^2=1+\eta c\),
\[
 \omega_R^2=\frac{k^2}{R^2}+m_{\rm branch}^2,
 \qquad
 \left.\partial_\eta\omega_R\right|_0=-\frac{ck^2}{2\omega}.
\]
Differentiating the exact family produces
\[
 \left.\partial_\eta e^{-i\omega_Rt}\right|_0
 =\frac{i ck^2}{2\omega}t e^{-i\omega t}.
\]
The action-derived current is nonradical on every standard branch and extra
multiplicity block, so the associated shell pairing cannot vanish when
\(ck\ne0\).  This is a genuine \(\cR_{j,a}\) obstruction, not a polynomial
\(\cP_{j,r}\) obstruction.

\section{Exceptional resonance as an independent obstruction}

Moment-map cancellation does not guarantee bounded or even nonsecular
second-order closure in a restricted exceptional carrier.  For the
axisymmetric exceptional \(\ell=1,k=0\) axial and polar amplitudes
\((a_x,a_p)\), a twist velocity can cancel the time-translation moment map.
The two parity self-sources can also be tuned to cancel their even
\(L=2\) resonance.  But the required interior point has
\(a_xa_p\ne0\), and the mixed axial \(L=2\) source has exact nonzero adjoint
pairing
\[
 -\frac{8\sqrt3}{9}.
\]
The boundary axes retain their individual self-obstructions.  Thus the only
axisymmetric two-polarization exceptional solution of the complete
second-order equation is \(a_x=a_p=0\).  The example proves that resonant
cokernel equations contain information independent of global charges.

\section{Current bounded frontier}

After the certified eliminations above, the full bounded problem is sharply
localized.  The still-open rows are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the homogeneous \(a\)-column against all oscillators;
\item the homogeneous \(d\)-column against nonzero-momentum oscillators;
\item shell resonances among bounded wave products and the remaining
      exceptional/global blocks; and
\item the common zero locus of these functionals for arbitrary finite sums.
\end{enumerate}
The electric, Wilson, and circumference columns are no longer open.  The
first full-time velocity fixture also sharpens the remaining \(d\)-column.
Locality gives
\[
 \cE^{(2)}[dt,u](t)=t\,\cE^{(2)}[c,u](t)
                    +\cE^{(2)}[dt,u](0).
\]
For the two axial extra representatives and the first polar representative,
the coefficient of \(t\) vanishes.  For the second polar representative it
is the nonzero eight-row vector
\[
 (-36,0,164,-8\sqrt3 i,0,-100,-24,-20).
\]
Thus the isolated fixture has polynomial zero locus
\(d z_{\mathrm{polar},2}=0\).  The previously computed constant-shell
adjoint matrix remains valid, but cannot by itself establish boundedness.
Joint cancellation with the \(a\)-column and same-channel sources remains
open.  Any future completion must preserve the separation between
positive-degree obstructions \(\cP\) and shell pairings \(\cR\).

\section{Interpretation}

The solution set near a symmetric compact background is not generally a
linear space.  Its second-order approximation is a cone cut out first by a
moment map and then, if uniform boundedness is demanded, by resonance
conditions.  Three points are important.

First, a linearly nonradical extra Weyl mode may fail to be tangent to the
nonlinear fixed-charge solution space.  This is nonlinear selection, not
linear gauge removal.  Second, mixing Einstein and extra directions can
cancel the moment maps, so no conclusion follows from the sign of a pure
branch alone.  Third, even complete moment-map cancellation leaves bounded
resonances.  Smooth formal continuation and bounded perturbative stability
are therefore different claims.

The result supplies the middle map in the programme's residual atlas:
\[
 X_\alpha
 \longmapsto \Omega(X_\alpha,\overline X_\alpha)
 \longmapsto \mu(X_\alpha)
 \longmapsto D^2\cE[X_\alpha,X_\alpha].
\]
Interaction sourcing, detector response, and BRST status are separate later
maps.

\section{What is not proved}

The following remain outside the theorem:
\begin{itemize}
\item infinite harmonic sums and Sobolev completion;
\item retarded or advanced solvability and nonlinear causal dependence;
\item integration of a second-order jet to an exact or all-orders family;
\item the final residual/background-stabilizer quotient;
\item asymptotically flat, de Sitter, anti-de Sitter, or black-hole boundary
      conditions;
\item observer coupling, particles, quantum BRST cohomology, and unitarity.
\end{itemize}
These exclusions are not cosmetic.  In particular, Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth-cone}
does not establish nonlinear stability.

\section{Outlook}

The immediate mathematical target is the full bounded finite-support cone.
The immediate physical target is to compare its strata with the transferred
Einstein/extra/Maxwell interaction tensor and with observer response.  A
later causal theorem would replace the finite secular right inverses by
support-controlled Green operators or prove that this cannot be done.

The intended final synthesis is not that every fourth-order mode disappears.
It is that each mode receives an exact disposition:
\[
 \boxed{\text{linear mode}\;\to\;\text{moment map}\;\to\;
 \text{resonance}\;\to\;\text{interaction and observation}.}
\]
