## The computation

Take the general exchange-covariant two-field quadratic Lagrangian — the shape
any O(1,1)-style embedding must reduce to:

```
L = ∂Ω·∂Υ + a Ω² + b Υ²
```

The exchange `Ω ↔ Υ` is a symmetry **iff `a = b`**. The quadratic form and
propagator are exactly

```
M = [[2a, −k²], [−k², 2b]]     det M = 4ab − k⁴     ⟨ΩΩ⟩ = 2b/(4ab − k⁴)
```

| (a, b) | exchange-symmetric | ⟨ΩΩ⟩ | poles k² | coincident double pole |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (0, 0) | **yes** | `0` | 0 | no |
| (0, 1) | no | `−2/k⁴` | 0 | **yes** |
| (1, 1) | **yes** | `−2/(k⁴−4)` | ±2 | no — **split** |
| (3/2, 3/2) | **yes** | `−3/(k⁴−9)` | ±3 | no — **split** |
| (0, −5/7) | no | `10/(7k⁴)` | 0 | **yes** |

## The theorem

> **Manifest exchange symmetry ⟹ no coincident double pole.**
> Either the two-point function vanishes identically (`a = b = 0`), or the poles
> separate to `k² = ±2a`.

Equivalently: the coincident double pole **requires** `a ≠ b`, which breaks the
exchange.

## This explains Bateman–Turok rather than refuting them

Their action *is* exchange-symmetric — `∂Ω·∂Υ` and `Ω²Υ²` both are, and there is
no lone `Ω²` or `Υ²` term. The asymmetric quadratic term that produces the double
pole is generated by the **vacuum**: expanding `Ω = λ⁻¹ + ω` about `⟨Ω⟩ = λ⁻¹`
turns the symmetric quartic `(λ²/2)Ω²Υ²` into `(1/2)Υ² + …` — exactly the
`a = 0, b ≠ 0` row. Integrating out `Υ` returns `−(1/2)(□ω)²`.

So **ghost parity is spontaneously broken by the vacuum that reproduces the
fourth-order theory**: `⟨Ω⟩ = λ⁻¹` while `⟨Υ⟩ = 0`, and the exchange maps that
vacuum to a different one. That is presumably what *"hidden"* means in their
title.

Two independent things corroborate it:

- Their own caveat that the `φ` and `(Ω,Υ)` path integrals are inequivalent —
  *"the former integrates over Ω > 0 whereas the latter integrates over all Ω"* —
  which is a statement about the **vacuum**, not the action.
- Paper 05's regulated masses `m±² = μ² ± √(εg)`: **split** exactly when the
  symmetry-breaking parameter `ε` is nonzero.

## Why this changes the tensor test — the point of the exercise

The queued check, *"does κ commute with s"*, is too weak. What a gravitational
lift actually has to supply is **both** of:

1. a two-field form of the linearized Weyl theory whose **action** is
   exchange-symmetric; **and**
2. a vacuum that is **BRST-invariant** while simultaneously **breaking the
   exchange** in the pattern `a = 0, b ≠ 0`.

The second is the hard one. In a gauge theory the vacuum is already constrained
by BRST invariance, and here it is being asked to break a discrete symmetry in a
specific direction at the same time. That is a much sharper target than a
commutator check, and it is the honest statement of what the lift requires.

## What this does not establish

- **Nothing about the gravitational case directly.** No tensor field, no gauge
  symmetry, no BRST differential appears in the computation. It constrains any
  two-field embedding without deciding the gravitational one.
- **No refutation of Bateman–Turok.** Their construction is consistent with this
  and is the reason the mechanism is *hidden*.
- **Not** that a suitable BRST-invariant, parity-breaking vacuum fails to exist
  for linearized Weyl gravity. That is now the open question, and it is a better
  posed one than the test it replaces.
- Nothing `LORENTZIAN-CAUSAL`, nothing about `g−2`.
