## Result

The flat Schrödinger-potential representation has an exact
determinant--resolvent Ward identity, but every identity generated by a fixed
log-field direction collapses to the already-known BT action Ward identity.
It supplies no new uncancelled determinant term and therefore does not close
the volume-uniform moment.

Let

\[
 u(\psi)=r(\psi)-\frac1N\sum_xr_x(\psi)\mathbf1,
 \qquad
 L_\psi=D_\psi u:\mathbf1^\perp\longrightarrow\mathbf1^\perp.
\]

The preceding flat-potential theorem implies

\[
                 |\det_{\mathbf1^\perp}L_\psi|=\det{}'K(u).
\]

For a fixed mean-zero log-field direction \(h\), push that constant vector
forward to the potential carrier:

\[
                         X_h(u)=L_\psi h.
\]

Piola's identity gives

\[
 \operatorname{div}_uX_h
 =D_h\log|\det L_\psi|
 =D_h\log\det{}'K.
\]

The flat negative-log density is

\[
 V(u)=G(u)+\log\det{}'K(u),\qquad
 G(u)=\frac{\|u\|_2^2+N\ell_0(u)^2}{2\lambda^2}
     =\frac{\|r(\psi)\|_2^2}{2\lambda^2}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 X_h\mathbin{\cdot}\nabla_uV-\operatorname{div}_uX_h=D_hG.}
\]

The determinant derivative cancels pointwise.  Integration by parts therefore
gives

\[
 \mathbb E_\nu[X_h\mathbin{\cdot}\nabla_uf]
 =\mathbb E_\nu[fD_hG].
\]

For \(F_h(u)=\langle h,\psi(u)\rangle\),
\(X_h\cdot\nabla F_h=\|h\|_2^2\), so

\[
                  \mathbb E[F_hD_hG]=\|h\|_2^2.
\]

Taking \(f=D_hG\) similarly gives

\[
                  \mathbb E[(D_hG)^2]=\mathbb E[D_h^2G].
\]

These are precisely the original Schwinger--Dyson and score/Hessian
identities in different coordinates.  The inverse determinant has not
created a stronger Ward estimate.

## The genuinely new resolvent object

The flat coordinates nevertheless expose the derivative that a different
proof must control.  Let \(K(u)\Omega=0\), let \(K^+\) be its symmetric
pseudoinverse, and take \(h\perp\mathbf1\).  Differentiating the ground-state
equation in a potential direction \(k\) gives

\[
 D_k\Omega
 =-K^+\bigl[\operatorname{diag}(k)-D_k\ell_0 I\bigr]\Omega
   +\alpha(k)\Omega.
\]

The gauge term disappears from the centered logarithm.  Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \nabla_uF_h
 =-\operatorname{diag}(\Omega)K^+
   \operatorname{diag}(\Omega)^{-1}h
 =L_\psi^{-T}h.}
\]

The displayed vector is automatically mean-zero.  It is the actual
ground-state resolvent interface.  A successor must use a vector field adapted
to this resolvent and retain its divergence together with the
log-pseudodeterminant score.  Reusing \(X_h=L_\psi h\) only invokes the
cancellation above.

## Consequence for the continuum gate

This result closes one narrowly tempting route: changing to flat potential
coordinates and then integrating along a fixed physical Fourier direction
does not improve the old Ward identity.  The determinant is exactly the
Jacobian of that change of variables, so Piola divergence removes it.

It does not show that every nonconvex determinant--resolvent method fails.
The surviving calculation is the divergence and effective-potential pairing
of a resolvent-adapted field such as
\(\operatorname{diag}(\Omega)K^+\operatorname{diag}(\Omega)^{-1}h\).
That calculation must either yield a volume-uniform lowest-mode estimate or
produce a controlled bad-volume sequence.  Only after a positive one-mode
result should the programme sum dyadic Fourier shells.
