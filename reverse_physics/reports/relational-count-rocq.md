## The question

The refutation killed the **absolute** count and left the obvious question
unanswered: if *"how many degrees of freedom are in this region"* is not well
posed, what does the DOF-independence assumption become?

Something survives, and it is pinned down completely.

## The move, and why it survives

Replace `μ(U)` by a **relative** count

```
ν(U, V)  =  degrees of freedom in U relative to V
```

Dilation invariance now acts on **both** arguments: `ν(dil_c U, dil_c V) = ν(U,V)`.
Applied to balls that says `ν(B_r, B_s)` depends only on the **ratio** `r/s` —
which is not degenerate at all. It is the familiar content of conformal
invariance: *there is no length, only ratios of lengths.*

The dilation argument that killed the absolute count doesn't kill this one; it
says exactly what conformal invariance always says.

## The scaling law

Add the one property any relative count must have — the chain rule
`ν(U,W) = ν(U,V)·ν(V,W)` — and write `f(t) = ν(B_t, B_1)`. Chaining through
`B_b` and using dilation invariance with `c = b`:

```
f(ab) = f(a) · f(b)          hence     f(2ⁿ) = f(2)ⁿ
```

**The entire family is generated by one number.**

So what survives of "how many degrees of freedom" in a conformally invariant
theory is **not a function on regions**. It is a single scaling exponent.

## The two results are one phenomenon

`a_reference_region_forces_a_trivial_exponent`: if some region could serve as an
absolute reference — if `ν(−, B_1)` were itself dilation invariant, which is
exactly what having an absolute count would mean — then `f(2) = 1` and the
exponent collapses, and every dyadic scale ties.

**The exponent is nonzero precisely because there is no conformally invariant
reference.** The refutation and this certificate are the same fact seen from two
sides.

The gate carries a control asserting the relative count is trivial *without* the
reference assumption; it is rejected. So the relational notion is genuinely
non-degenerate, not a second collapse.

## What their assumption becomes

`#states = ∏ #confDOF` is a **product of counts**. Products of ratios are sums of
exponents, so DOF-independence survives as **additivity of the exponent** over
independent subsystems — not as a product of counts.

That reading is prose. Only the scaling law is proved.

## What this does not establish

- **The exponent is not shown positive, nor equal to a dimension.** Monotonicity
  is *not* assumed, so the power-law reading is interpretation.
- **Dyadic scales only.** `f(2ⁿ) = f(2)ⁿ` is proved; extending to all rationals
  needs a regularity argument not given here.
- **The additivity reading is not proved.** No product structure on regions is
  formalised.
- Geometric inputs are explicit hypotheses, as in the companion files.
- Nothing about curved configurations, non-constant conformal factors, or extra
  structure.
- No claim about GR or its dynamics; not a reproduction or refutation of
  Carcassi–Aidala's derivation.
