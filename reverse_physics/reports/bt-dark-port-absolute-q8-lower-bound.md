## The q6-to-q8 inequality

Let

\[
 \bar q_4=q_4/\lambda^4=\|x_2\|^2>0.
\]

For each angle cell, the complete certified coefficient satisfies

\[
 2\operatorname{Re}\langle x_2,X_4(c_i)\rangle
 =\bar q_4R_6(c_i).
\]

Writing `Delta R6=R6(c2)-R6(c1)` and subtracting the two identities gives

\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle x_2,X_4(c_2)-X_4(c_1)\rangle
 ={\bar q_4\Delta R_6\over2}.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields

\[
 \|X_4(c_2)-X_4(c_1)\|^2
 \geq {\bar q_4(\Delta R_6)^2\over4},
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{{Q_{8,-}\over\bar q_4}\geq{(\Delta R_6)^2\over8}.}
\]

Thus a nonzero complete `q6` contrast forces a positive absolute dark-port
`q8` coefficient without knowing the components of `X4` orthogonal to `x2`
and without computing `X6`.

the dark port, while the unequal complete next-order corrections do not.
The first dark click therefore detects the angle dependence generated by the
complete tree-plus-loop correction.  The strict lower bound is independent
of finite positive box volume because its displayed part comes from the
active-loop contrast.

The theorem is confined to two orthogonal common-lattice modes, sharp finite
duration, the declared normal-ordered massless unit-residue auxiliary
scheme, and the finite-volume spectator normalization.  It does not provide
