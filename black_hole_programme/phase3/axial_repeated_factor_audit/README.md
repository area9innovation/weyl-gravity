# Axial repeated-factor shortcut audit

The complete Fourier-reduced axial Bach module on the declared Schwarzschild
pilot interval is **not** a scalar Regge--Wheeler square.

The certified structure is instead the typed exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow {\cal E}_{\rm Einstein}^{\,2}
\longrightarrow {\cal E}_{\rm Bach}^{\,6}
\xrightarrow{\ \delta{\rm Ric}\ }
{\cal E}_{\rm carrier}^{\,4}
\longrightarrow0
\]

together with the covariant composition

\[
\delta B[h]
=
\left(\tfrac12\Box+C\right)\delta{\rm Ric}[h].
\]

The two factors act on different modules.  A scalar Regge--Wheeler equation
has two local radial solutions and its square has four, not six.  The horizon
data also show that the carrier is not a naïve duplicated-root copy of the
Regge--Wheeler module.  The scalar exponents are
\(\{0,-4i\omega\}\), whereas the four-dimensional Ricci carrier has

\[
\{0,0,-4i\omega,-2-4i\omega\}.
\]

This exponent comparison is diagnostic, not a general matrix-square no-go:
composition of singular differential expressions can shift indicial roots.
The invariant scalar-square obstruction is the six-versus-four solution
dimension.

The carrier does have two columns at each of the two asymptotic exponential
rates, but their distinct tensorial powers and log-free recurrences do not
turn them into canonical spectral derivatives of one scalar mode.

Frequency sensitivities used by validated radial transport are also not
physical generalized modes.  They solve an inhomogeneous variational
equation.  A genuine time-translation Jordan vector appears only after
differentiating the complete Fourier factor, which adds
\(iv\,e^{i\omega v}u\).  Fixed-frequency radial polynomial or logarithmic
behaviour does not by itself create a time Jordan chain.

Finally, the older \(H_1=\mathrm{constant}\), \(H_0\sim-i\omega r\) column is
not a counterexample.  It belonged to an incomplete two-row reconstruction
and fails the subsequently restored \(v\phi\) Ricci row.

This audit does not exclude every local weighted matrix square, arbitrary
singular or nonlocal equivalences, and does not construct a canonical
spectral-derivative submodule.
