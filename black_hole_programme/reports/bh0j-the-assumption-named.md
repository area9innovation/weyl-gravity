## 1. The pair is a solution, not a relabelling

`BH0H` left this open on purpose: the profile realising its frame change was obtained from a
transformation law, and a transformation law does not by itself say the result solves anything.

In the frame where the scalar is constant, the conformally coupled action collapses to
Einstein-Hilbert plus a cosmological term, so its stress tensor is a combination of `G_ab` and
`g_ab`. The gravitational equation is `alpha W_ab = (1/2) T_ab`, and that frame's metric is a
Bach **vacuum**. So consistency forces `T_ab = 0` - which for a constant scalar is exactly the
**Einstein condition.**

Computed: `1 - u/r - hat_k r^2` has trace-free Ricci vanishing identically, and

```
R = 12 hat_k        and BH0F requires R = -24 lambda S_0^2        so  hat_k = -2 lambda S_0^2
```

**The de Sitter term is the scalar potential.** With `hat_k` fixed that way, Bach-flatness and
the Einstein condition are then verified together on the same metric. The exterior pair solves
the coupled system with nothing left over.

A control keeps that from being empty: `lambda = 0` must degenerate to `R = 0`, i.e.
Schwarzschild. Otherwise "the metric is some Einstein space" would carry no information about
the scalar.

## 2. `u` is a conformal invariant. `gamma` is not.

`BH0H`'s action reads `u -> u`, and `gamma -> gamma - 2wC - 3uC^2`. So the mass coefficient is
an invariant of the solution and the linear coefficient is a coordinate on the conformal class.

Two frames with the **same** `u` and **different** `gamma` are exhibited. Therefore

> **`gamma` is not a function of the mass.**

It needs a second, independent datum - and `BH0H` already identified it: `C`, the scalar's
fractional gradient. For any target `gamma` and any `u` there is a `C` attaining it, and the
required `C` varies with the target, so `gamma` is a free datum rather than a derived one. That
is checked, not assumed; a `C` independent of the target would have meant `gamma` was derived
after all.

## 3. What that does to the question

`BH0C` requires `gamma` to be one constant across galaxies whose masses differ by orders of
magnitude. That had the look of a coincidence in need of explanation.

It is neither a coincidence nor a tension. `gamma` **cannot** depend on mass, because mass is a
conformal invariant and `gamma` is not. So the universality requirement is exactly:

> one boundary datum on the symmetry-breaking sector is common to every galaxy, while the mass
> varies freely.

That is consistent. It is not delivered by the gravitational theory. And it is the whole of what
a conformal-gravity rotation-curve fit assumes.

**Both readings of that are available and this prefers neither.** Favourably: the universality
`BH0C` demands is structurally natural rather than a fine-tuning of gravity. Unfavourably: the
explanation of rotation curves is imported from the symmetry-breaking sector rather than
produced by gravity.

## 4. What is not established

- **The interior**, and the matching that would fix `C` from a given configuration. That needs a
  matter model this repository does not have, and it is what remains of "forced versus sourced".
- That a cosmological boundary condition **can** deliver a universal `C`. The assumption is
  named here, not discharged.
- Which frame matter follows. `BH0I` computes both branches; neither is selected.
- The Yukawa form, the scalar action, the conformal weights. All premises, as in `BH0F` and
  `BH0I`.
- Anything numerical: no galaxy, no fit, no `a0`.
- Anything outside the exterior Bach vacuum or the gauge `b = 1/B`.

## Correction

A literature search that should have preceded this work was run afterwards, and it changes what
may be claimed. See the `correction` field of the certificate for the full text. In short:

- **Hobson & Lasenby**, *Conformal gravity does not predict flat galaxy rotation curves*,
  Phys. Rev. D **104** (2021) 064014, arXiv:2103.13451, already establish that timelike
  geodesics are not conformally invariant, that a scalar generating particle mass puts massive
  particles on geodesics of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter metric, and that the resulting curves
  have no flat region. That is a **stronger** conclusion than exhibiting both branches and
  selecting neither.
- **Mannheim & Kazanas**, Gen. Rel. Grav. **26** (1994) 337, already give the moment structure,
  which is the substance of the standing objection to the Newtonian limit.
- **Mannheim & O'Brien**, arXiv:1211.0188, already use the decomposition
  `gamma = gamma_0 + gamma_*(M)` as the fitting parametrisation, retaining both terms.

The computations and their controls stand. The novelty implied by their framing does not, and
`BH0K`'s inequality conclusion is withdrawn outright.
