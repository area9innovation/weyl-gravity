## 1. The fork `BH0D` left

`BH0D` reduced the vacuum equation to `nabla^4 B = 0` and listed among its non-results:

> any Green's function. None is constructed and no multipole integral is evaluated.

and posed the question sharply:

> does the fourth-order source integrate to something proportional to baryonic mass? If yes,
> `gamma` is proportional to `M` and `BH0C` says the Tully-Fisher slope is wrong. If no, the
> specific conformal matter coupling that avoids it is the physical content. **Either branch is
> a result.**

## 2. The Green's function, constructed

From `nabla^2 r = 2/r` and `nabla^2(1/r) = 0` away from the origin, `r` is the biharmonic kernel
with a delta at the origin. The four-dimensional kernel `{1/r, 1, r, r^2}` is exhibited rather
than quoted - each verified biharmonic, and `r^3` verified **not** to be.

The angular average of `|x - x'|` over a shell is `[(r+r')^3 - |r-r'|^3]/(6rr')`, which reduces
to `r + r'^2/(3r)` outside and `r' + r^2/(3r')` inside. Both branches are computed.

## 3. The moments

For a compact spherically symmetric source, outside it:

```
gamma  =  -(1/2) int f(r') r'^2 dr'        the ZEROTH moment
beta   =  -(1/6) int f(r') r'^4 dr'        the SECOND moment
```

The constructed exterior field is then verified to satisfy `nabla^4 B = 0`, which is `BH0B`'s
condition reached by a different route than the integrals.

**So with `f` tracking the density:**

- `gamma` is **proportional to the total mass**.
- The Newtonian coefficient tracks **`M<r^2>`, not `M`**.

For a uniform ball, in closed form: `gamma = -M/(8 pi)` exactly, and `beta/gamma = R^2/5` - its
mean square radius. That the ratio is a *length squared* is what makes "beta is not a mass" a
computation rather than a reading of the integrand.

A shell and a ball of equal mass give the **same** `gamma` and **different** `beta`. Without
that check, "different moments" would be a statement about the formulae rather than about the
physics.

## 4. What it does to the line

`BH0J` showed `gamma` cannot depend on mass, because mass is a conformal invariant of the
solution and `gamma` is a coordinate on the conformal class. This shows the **sourced** piece of
`gamma` is proportional to mass. Both hold, and they are not in conflict - they decompose it:

```
gamma  =  gamma_0  +  gamma_*(M)
```

with `gamma_0` the boundary datum `BH0J` named and `gamma_*` the piece computed here. `BH0C`
requires the total to be universal across galaxies of widely differing mass. So the requirement
is neither automatic nor contradictory. It is

> **`gamma_*(M) << gamma_0` across the sample.**

That is the sharpest form the assumption has taken on this line, and unlike everything before it
the statement is *checkable against data* - data this repository does not hold.

Separately: the second-moment result for the Newtonian coefficient is the shape of Flanagan's
objection, reached from the source side rather than the field side. It is exhibited, not used to
decide anything.

## 5. Premises, declared

- `f` proportional to `(T^0_0 - T^r_r)` is the combination `BH0E` already uses when it requires
  radiation to still source the equation. The overall constant is Mannheim-Kazanas normalisation.
  **Both entered, not derived** - and nothing here depends on the constant, since every statement
  is about which *moment* a coefficient is.
- A perfect-fluid description. `BH0I` takes a mass generated by a Yukawa coupling, `m = hS`;
  reconciling the two is a matter-sector question and is not addressed.

## 6. What is not established

- The interior solution, or the scalar's profile inside the source. Only the exterior
  coefficients as functionals of the source.
- Any numerical comparison. Whether `gamma_*` is in fact subdominant to `gamma_0` for real
  galaxies is observational and not answered here.
- The resolution of Flanagan-Mannheim.
- That the fluid and Yukawa descriptions are compatible.

## 7. Where the line ends

Both sides are now computed. The vacuum side: the term is forced, biharmonic in origin, a
coordinate on the conformal class, and the exterior pair is an exact solution. The source side:
`gamma` is the zeroth moment and the Newtonian coefficient is the second.

What remains is **observational rather than algebraic** - the inequality
`gamma_*(M) << gamma_0` against a rotation-curve sample, and the second-moment Newtonian
coefficient against solar-system and laboratory tests. Neither is this repository's measurement,
and that is the right place for a reverse-physics line to stop.

## Correction

A literature search that should have preceded this work was run afterwards, and it changes what
may be claimed. See the `correction` field of the certificate for the full text. In short:

- **Hobson & Lasenby**, *Conformal gravity does not predict flat galaxy rotation curves*,
  Phys. Rev. D **104** (2021) 064014, arXiv:2103.13451, already establish that timelike
  geodesics are not conformally invariant, that a scalar generating particle mass puts massive
  particles on geodesics of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter metric, and that the resulting curves
  have no flat region. That is a **stronger** conclusion than exhibiting both branches and
  selecting neither.
- **Mannheim & Kazanas**, Gen. Rel. Grav. **26** (1994) 337, already give the moment structure,
  which is the substance of the standing objection to the Newtonian limit.
- **Mannheim & O'Brien**, arXiv:1211.0188, already use the decomposition
  `gamma = gamma_0 + gamma_*(M)` as the fitting parametrisation, retaining both terms.

The computations and their controls stand. The novelty implied by their framing does not, and
`BH0K`'s inequality conclusion is withdrawn outright.
