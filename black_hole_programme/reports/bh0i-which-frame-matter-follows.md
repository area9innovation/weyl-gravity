## 1. The premise is a variational identity

A point particle of mass `m` extremises `-int m ds`. The symmetry-breaking scalar exists in
order to generate a mass scale, and the way it does that is a Yukawa coupling, `m = h S`. Then

```
-int h S ds_g  =  -int h sqrt( -S^2 g_ab dx^a dx^b )  =  -int h ds_{S^2 g}
```

identically. So the particle is a geodesic of `S^2 g` and **not** of `g`.

**The mass scale is the conformal factor.** That is the whole content, and it is an identity
rather than an interpretation - it is checked here as an equality of Lagrangians in an
unspecified metric function and an unspecified profile, not on a chosen solution.

`BH0H` showed `S^2 g` is exactly the frame in which `S` is constant, up to the constant
`S_0^2`. `BH0F` puts that frame at `gamma = 0`.

## 2. Both branches, computed

Take `BH0H`'s pair: a hatted frame with `gamma = 0`, and its image under the Moebius map
carrying `gamma = -2C - 3uC^2`. Both are Bach vacua - confirmed through the curvature engine,
so this is a fork between two admissible solutions of the same theory, not between a solution
and an artefact.

| branch | trajectory follows | linear coefficient of `v^2` |
|---|---|---|
| `m` constant, independent of `S` | geodesics of `g` | `gamma/2` |
| `m = h S`, generated by `S` | geodesics of `S^2 g` | **`0`** |

The first entry is `BH0C`'s weak-field circular-orbit formula, reached here by a different
route and verified against it. The second is zero: a particle whose mass tracks the scalar sees
no linearly rising term at all.

The disagreement is not an artefact of the weak-field expansion. The **exact** circular-orbit
velocities differ too, and that is checked separately.

## 3. What this settles and what it does not

It settles that the frame question cannot be left implicit. Two branches of the same theory, on
the same Bach vacuum, give different rotation curves; a construction has to say which it takes.
A rotation-curve fit needs the first branch. The second is what the symmetry breaking was
introduced to provide.

It does **not** adjudicate. Whether a matter sector can generate a mass scale by breaking
conformal invariance and still leave particle masses independent of the breaking field is a
question about that sector. Nothing here answers it, and nothing here refutes conformal gravity
or MOND.

## 4. What this does not establish

- Which branch a real matter sector realises. That is the open premise, left open.
- The Yukawa form `m = h S`. Standard, and a premise here exactly as `BH0F` takes the scalar
  field equation as one.
- Anything about the interior of the source or the matching that fixes the profile. The
  computation is in the exterior Bach vacuum, which is where rotation curves are measured.
- That the profile solves the coupled system with matter present - `BH0H`'s open direction (a),
  and the other half of "forced versus sourced".
- Anything numerical: no galaxy, no fit, no `a0`.

## 5. Controls

- **The identity is compared as functions**, in an unspecified `a(r)` and an unspecified
  `S(r)`, so it is not checked on a convenient solution.
- **`S` is declared positive, and that is recorded rather than absorbed.** `sqrt(S^2 X) = S
  sqrt(X)` needs it, and the first run **failed** without it. A vev passing through zero has
  vanishing generated mass and a degenerate conformal factor, so the identity is exactly as
  valid as `BH0H`'s frame change and no more.
- **Non-vacuity, two-sided.** With `S` constant the two Lagrangians must coincide; with `S`
  non-constant they must differ. Without the second, "the frames disagree" would be compatible
  with there being no fork at all.
- **Not an expansion artefact.** The exact velocities are required to differ, not only their
  series.
- **An independent rail.** Both frames are confirmed Bach vacua through the curvature engine.
- **Consistency with `BH0C`.** The constant-mass branch reproduces `gamma/2`.
