# Core dictionary: the first 67 concepts

The public dictionary now contains the complete first writing batch from the
150-concept proposal: 60 new entries and expanded accounts for all seven existing
entries. Each of the 67 has General, Physics, Mathematics and Specialist prose,
an expanded application or example, related links and pinned source provenance.
The full text contains approximately 18,500 words across all four perspectives.
The remaining 83 proposed concepts are a backlog, not empty public entries.

## Reader experience

The default A–Z list contains explained concepts only. Search accepts canonical
labels, explicit aliases and acronym expansions. Every entry links to its fuller
account and related concepts; the existing audience menu selects or compares the
four perspectives. The list and general explanations work without JavaScript.

The larger extracted-phrase queue is inside a closed Editorial inventory
section. Its compressed data is fetched only on first opening. Existing source
filters, drafting briefs and legacy links remain available there. Frequency and
canonicalization filters apply to that queue, not to admission of public definitions.

Bare ACA and RCA no longer automatically alias ACA₀ and RCA₀. Acronyms in the
core entries are expanded, including Quantum field theory (QFT) and the wave
reconstruction notation WR/WRμ. Ambiguous terms such as field, support, mode,
energy and particle are searchable with explicit related links but do not trigger
automatic annotation in arbitrary prose. This avoids silently selecting a sense.

## Editorial review

The General accounts introduce the idea and a concrete comparison or consequence.
Physics accounts identify the modeled quantity and operational or dynamical role.
Mathematics accounts state structures, conditions and coding distinctions.
Specialist accounts identify the exact project use and the boundary that must not
be crossed. The original seven entries retain their worked examples and gain
additional distinctions and crosslinks; they were not shortened to fit a template.

The AI editorial pass checked these distinctions throughout the batch:

- existence versus an algorithm or a supplied approximation schedule;
- finite calculations versus completed or uniform constructions;
- positive energy versus a positive quantum state;
- physical fields versus scalar fields in algebra;
- gauge ghosts versus problematic norm/energy modes;
- residual deformation/vertex classes versus one-particle states;
- chain-complex consistency versus causal or probabilistic construction;
- local/reduced/Euclidean results versus full Lorentzian certification;
- a scoped failed architecture versus refutation of an entire theory;
- certificate reproduction versus independent verification and empirical validity.

Current introduction, wave reversal, reduced positivity and trace-repair reports
are pinned as appropriate. General mathematical explanations are editorial prose,
not newly proved theorems. Source hashes identify the reviewed material; they do
not certify every explanatory sentence. No independent expert approval or peer
review is claimed, and no scientific lifecycle state is promoted.

## Validation and limits

The scoped checks cover all 67 proposal IDs, all 268 perspective summaries and
expanded accounts, distinct audience prose, known crosslinks, source freshness,
canonical-system boundaries, public search, lazy inventory loading, all existing
editorial controls, popup interaction, perspective changes and mobile layout.
The initial reading-site check was run before rebuilding and correctly detected
the old generated dictionary. A later check exposed a verifier assumption about
the retired index heading; it now verifies the exact explained-entry links instead.
Both checks were rerun against the final site.

The extraction and proposal evidence are refreshed after the dictionary change.
Exact commands, elapsed times, input/output hashes and test-tier decisions are in
[the receipt](../receipts/core-dictionary-v1.json). Tier 3 is unnecessary: no
mathematical inputs, algebraic kernels or scientific theorem statuses changed.

The 83 remaining concepts still need their own accounts. Crosslinks to this core
help with their prerequisites, but do not constitute explanations for every
technical expression in specialist papers. The editorial inventory intentionally
retains unreviewed candidate senses for future work.
