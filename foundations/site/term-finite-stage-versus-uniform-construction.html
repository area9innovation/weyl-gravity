Finite-stage versus uniform construction

How to read the programme’s claims and evidence.

General

Solving every finite version of a problem is different from finding one solution that works for the whole unlimited problem. The separate finite solutions may disagree or become less controlled as the size grows.

Example and distinction

For each short list of modes, a correction might make a positivity test pass. That does not show that one smooth correction works for all modes together. The project’s cutoff example makes this distinction concrete.

Physics

The distinction is between a family of finite solutions and compatible full data meeting uniform regularity or bounds. A cutoff-dependent choice may become singular or fail consistency as the cutoff grows.

Use in the model

The reduced tensor result permits each finite positive repair but excludes one smooth repair preserving the declared commutator over the full family. It does not negate every possible choice of full carrier or physical construction.

Mathematics

The quantifier pattern “for each N there exists x_N” does not imply “there exists x working for every N.” Even compatible approximants may need compactness or uniform estimates to yield an admissible limit.

Conditions and consequences

State the topology and limiting class explicitly. Finite exact arithmetic proves the tested identities, while an infinite obstruction or completion theorem requires its own argument.

Specialist

The wave and cutoff examples expose different missing resources: convergence information for named reconstruction, and full regularity/compatibility for positivity repair. They should not be combined into one theorem.

Project role and claim boundary

The reduced obstruction remains conditional on its reference and commutator constraints. Finite checks support the witnesses but do not certify all infinite claims, full metric positivity or causal products.
