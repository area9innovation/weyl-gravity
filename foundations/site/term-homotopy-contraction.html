Homotopy contraction

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

A homotopy contraction relates a large complex to a smaller one while accounting for the information removed as trivial. It uses maps in both directions and an explicit correction describing their difference from doing nothing.

Example and distinction

It is more than keeping a convenient subset of components. The maps must satisfy identities showing that the reduction respects the original consistency structure.

Physics

A contraction packages projection, inclusion and a homotopy connecting their composite to the identity. It can organize transfer from a full gauge complex to residual data.

Use in the model

The algebraic identities do not by themselves preserve positivity, causal support or analytic domains. Those requirements must accompany any physical interpretation of the reduced model.

Mathematics

In a common convention, maps p and i satisfy pi=1 and 1−ip=dh+hd, together with chain-map conditions and any stated side conditions. Sign conventions depend on grading.

Conditions and consequences

A homotopy equivalence supports a cohomology comparison when the complexes are valid. Checking selected contraction blocks does not establish every differential-square or pairing identity required for the full object.

Specialist

Use pi_cl for the homological projection in the programme’s notation. Keep the imported operators and exact domains fixed when verifying contraction data.

Project role and claim boundary

The trace/ghost candidate’s repaired contraction identities do not certify full nilpotency, chain maps or cyclicity. The classical import gate remains closed until all required data pass their own checks.
