Existence, uniqueness and well-posedness

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

Existence asks whether a solution can be found. Uniqueness asks whether the permitted data select only one. Well-posedness also asks whether small changes in the data cause suitably small changes in the solution.

Example and distinction

These questions depend on which solutions are allowed. A formula that produces one example does not show that every admissible input has a unique, stable solution.

Physics

A well-posed initial-value problem provides existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence in specified data and solution spaces. Gauge theories may formulate uniqueness only after accounting for redundancy.

Use in the model

Hyperbolicity helps organize such results but is not the whole theorem. Constraints, boundary conditions and global geometry still matter, and a successful scalar formulation does not automatically establish the full metric gauge problem.

Mathematics

Well-posedness is a property of a specified input-output problem, not merely its differential expression. The time interval, topology and equivalence notion for solutions are essential.

Conditions and consequences

A weak existence theorem may lack uniqueness, while continuous dependence can fail in a chosen stronger topology. Logical strength may also change when the requested solution is represented with additional quantitative information.

Specialist

Separate principal-operator criteria, complete existence estimates and the encoded complex’s consistency. The tested normally- or strongly-hyperbolic architectures are scoped witnesses, not an exhaustive classification of every formulation.

Project role and claim boundary

The full-export square-zero failure blocks its dependent construction regardless of success on a reduced scalar problem. Conversely, excluding one witness family does not refute all possible well-posedness routes.
