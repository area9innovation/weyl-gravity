/topics/introduction/versions/general/0/paragraphs/1

This programme began with an unusual theory of gravity called Weyl gravity. It grew into a broader investigation: start with the jobs a physical theory must do, then identify which physical ideas and mathematical assumptions make each job possible. We call this reverse foundations of physics. The aim is a clearer route from an interesting idea to a testable claim.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/1/paragraphs/0

A theory begins with physical ideas: how systems affect one another, which changes leave the laws unchanged, and what can count as a measurement. It also chooses a mathematical setting. A finite table of numbers and a space containing every possible wave are different settings, even if one approximates the other.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/2/paragraphs/1

A cell might ask whether states can be defined in a particular setting, or whether a limiting prediction can be reconstructed under particular assumptions. Its color reports the kind of evidence recorded there. Even several successful cells do not make a complete theory: the pieces must fit together, and the atlas records missing connections as well as results.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/3/paragraphs/0

The gravity studies ask several different questions about extra solutions: do they exist mathematically, survive the rules that remove redundant descriptions, remain possible when interactions are included, and support sensible probabilities? The word ghost is often used around these questions, but one answer cannot settle all of them.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/3/paragraphs/1

Other examples test different connections. Mannheim’s proposal uses conformal gravity to explain how galaxies rotate; comparing its predictions with data requires stating how measurement errors are weighted. A curve can do better by one score and worse by another. A stability shortcut for Bateman and Turok’s proposal for higher-derivative physics can fail without disproving their entire theory. Each case needs a precise question and a conclusion with explicit limits.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/4/paragraphs/0

Imagine receiving increasingly detailed descriptions of a wave. One supplier gives an error schedule telling you when an approximation is accurate enough. Another only promises that the descriptions eventually agree. Those are different amounts of information, even though the wave and the detector are the same.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/5/paragraphs/0

The programme has not produced a complete theory of the universe. A mathematical construction must still connect to meaningful states, predictions, and observations. Failing a proposed route may teach us something important without proving that every route must fail.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/6/paragraphs/0

Artificial intelligence (AI) systems help this programme derive equations, write software, challenge arguments and draft explanations. They can also produce convincing mistakes. Clear writing matters because a reader must be able to see what was assumed, what was checked and where the argument could fail.

/topics/introduction/versions/general/7/paragraphs/1

Theory journeys follow a proposal toward a prediction and a test. The Research atlas lets you inspect the 576 questions and their evidence. Papers provide the detailed, dated arguments. You do not need to read the entire matrix first: choose a question, understand what would count as an answer, then inspect the supporting steps.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/0/heading

From equations to physical obligations

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/0/paragraphs/0

The programme combines two questions: whether pure Weyl gravity and related fourth-order models survive their classical, quantum and empirical tests, and which assumptions each test actually uses. The unit of progress is an obligation—such as state existence, causal propagation or a calibrated prediction—rather than a theory name.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/0/paragraphs/1

The two introductory papers motivate the same decomposition. Physical postulates, mathematical realizations and logical existence principles are distinct inputs. A result about one realization must not acquire a stronger interpretation merely because the notation resembles that of another. This introduction presents that programme, with later corrections incorporated below.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/1/paragraphs/0

Separate physical postulates, the carrier used to realize them, logic and existence assumptions, operational identifications, and empirical protocols. Locality or conformal symmetry is a physical premise; a Hilbert completion or an operator domain is mathematical structure; a detector functional and a noise model add different operational information.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/1/paragraphs/1

The distinction matters at familiar transitions: from finite truncations to continuum fields, from Euclidean constructions to Lorentzian propagation, and from an indefinite gauge description to physical probabilities. Each transition needs a theorem or an explicitly stated premise. A successful calculation on one side does not supply that bridge by itself.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/2/paragraphs/0

The six foundational regimes, six carrier families and sixteen obligations form a common coordinate system. A finite algebraic construction, a Hilbert-space argument and a local field-theoretic construction can contribute evidence to different cells without being identified.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/2/paragraphs/1

Theory profiles show coverage, whereas theory journeys follow assumptions, states, evolution, observables, predictions and empirical tests along one route. A profile may collect strong results from incompatible realizations. The journey asks whether the necessary interfaces have actually been supplied for the same model.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/3/paragraphs/0

The fourth-order and pure-Weyl studies distinguish local modes, reduction by gauge symmetry and constraints, nonlinear continuation, and quantum interpretation. An additional classical direction is not automatically a negative-probability particle; a positive free block does not automatically extend to an interacting theory.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/3/paragraphs/1

The empirical studies distinguish unweighted residuals from uncertainty-weighted gates in a bounded galaxy protocol. The Bateman–Turok example targets a proposed universal Euclidean stability estimate rather than the whole reconstruction programme. Each is a case study in specifying which implication succeeds or fails, with the detailed assumptions retained in its paper and evidence record.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/4/paragraphs/0

The wave benchmark fixes chiral data and one smeared detector. Supplying a Cauchy rate permits the declared reconstruction in the weak base; removing it yields a stronger existence statement. The lower bound already occurs at a fixed time and profile, so the additional logical cost is amplitude completion rather than propagation.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/4/paragraphs/1

The reduced tensor-mode example distinguishes a positive correction at each finite cutoff from one smooth correction compatible with all modes and the stated commutator. Its boundary is reduced-model positivity. Neither calculation supplies the full gravity state construction or decides a research programme’s empirical viability.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/5/paragraphs/0

The serialized full complex fails its differential consistency prerequisite. The trace/ghost candidate repairs two exposed blocks and selected contraction identities, but full nilpotency, compatible maps and pairing for the repaired object remain unverified. Older claims of an accepted full BRST–Hadamard transfer must be read with this correction.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/5/paragraphs/1

This failure does not erase every classical or reduced-mode calculation. It blocks conclusions that depend on this full export. A physical positive state, full Lorentzian renormalization and an interacting quantum theory still need their own constructions; neither formal causal structure nor a local quantum calculation can substitute for them.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/6/paragraphs/0

An exact algebraic identity, a numerical benchmark, a written infinite-dimensional proof and a proof-assistant derivation answer different questions. An independent checker is useful only if it does not silently inherit the producer’s decisive assumption. A skip or timeout contributes no passing evidence.

/topics/introduction/versions/physics/6/paragraphs/1

The unusual AI-assisted authorship increases the importance of explicit sources and reproducible arguments. The website is a current explanatory account, while papers preserve dated arguments. Neither fluent prose nor a machine-readable certificate supplies missing physical premises or substitutes for independent scientific review.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/0/paragraphs/0

A physical model is more than a differential equation. It needs a space of admissible data, evolution, quantities extracted from solutions, and a bridge to observations. Existence, uniqueness, continuity and probability can depend on different structures and hypotheses.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/0/paragraphs/1

The project studies that dependency explicitly. Reverse foundations of physics is broader than reverse mathematics: it compares physical premises and mathematical representations as well as logical strength. Its origin in pure Weyl gravity provides concrete problems; the general question is which assumptions suffice for each specified task and which can be proved necessary.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/1/paragraphs/0

A carrier includes the relevant topology, pairing and operator domains, not merely an underlying set or vector space. A change of topology can change which operations are continuous; a change of pairing can change positivity. A representation of input data can include or omit a convergence rate.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/1/paragraphs/1

Logical assumptions form another layer. A proof may assert that an infinite set or limit exists without supplying an effective construction. Reverse mathematics studies the strength of such assertions over a fixed base. The wider programme also asks whether another carrier preserves the intended observable while avoiding a particular assumption.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/2/paragraphs/0

Each cell combines a foundational regime, a carrier family and a physical obligation. The geometry of this table is organizational: neighboring coordinates need not be connected by an implication. A populated cell does not quantify over every object in its family.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/2/paragraphs/1

The atlas therefore keeps evidence records and interface claims separate. Compare hypotheses and constructions before composing results. A theorem about a finite exact complex can be useful without constituting a theorem about its completion; an existence proof on one carrier need not transport to another.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/3/paragraphs/0

A local solution may fail a global compatibility condition. A nontrivial cohomology class may have no demonstrated positive state interpretation. A family of finite constructions may lack a uniform estimate needed to pass to a limit. These are different obstructions and should have different conclusions.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/3/paragraphs/1

The introductory papers illustrate this with fourth-order gravity, a Euclidean stability counterfamily, and bounded empirical comparisons. The counterexample to a sufficient estimate does not negate every possible construction; a comparison of fitted residuals does not identify a model without its statistical and physical assumptions.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/4/paragraphs/0

Over RCA₀, the supplied-rate reconstruction WRμ is provable, whereas the rate-free assertion WR is equivalent to ACA₀. RCA means Recursive Comprehension Axiom; ACA means Arithmetical Comprehension Axiom. They name mathematical axiom systems used to calibrate the two assertions, not levels of physical accuracy.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/4/paragraphs/1

The distinction is in the input representation and the requested fast name for the observable. The scalar completeness reversal is standard; the project realizes it within the fixed wave-detector construction. The cutoff example separately illustrates the logical difference between a correction for each finite stage and one correction with the required uniform regularity.

A candidate fixes the diagnosed blocks, but those repairs do not constitute a proof of all identities for the whole object.

Finite exact arithmetic can verify a matrix identity without proving an infinite convergence theorem. Replaying a generator demonstrates reproduction; a distinct derivation tests a different failure mode.

/topics/introduction/versions/mathematics/6/paragraphs/1

Audience editions are also editorial work. Sharing a status record prevents one kind of inconsistency but cannot mechanically prove that every explanation preserves meaning.

/topics/wave/versions/general/0/heading

A wave and a detector

/topics/wave/versions/general/0/paragraphs/0

Imagine a wave travelling around a circular track. Our mathematical detector returns a weighted average of the wave over space, rather than its height at one point. The example asks whether increasingly accurate descriptions of the wave determine a well-controlled detector reading.

/topics/wave/versions/general/2/paragraphs/0

With the error schedule supplied, the model lets us construct an approximation to the detector reading with controlled error. Without it, the assertion that an appropriate completed reading always exists uses a stronger set of mathematical assumptions in the formulation studied here.

/topics/wave/versions/general/2/paragraphs/1

The surprise is where the extra difficulty sits: it is already present in finding a limiting amplitude before the wave moves. Complicated dynamics are not responsible.

/topics/wave/versions/general/3/paragraphs/1

It does not show that a physical detector needs stronger logic, nor that every prediction from a wave equation is difficult. It is a comparison of two carefully defined mathematical tasks.

/topics/wave/versions/physics/0/heading

The observable

/topics/wave/versions/physics/0/paragraphs/0

Take mean-zero left- and right-moving scalar profiles on the unit circle. A fixed periodic tent function h defines Oₚ(t)=∫h(x)[a(x−t)+b(x+t)]dx. Translation supplies the dynamics; spatial smearing supplies a bounded observable.

/topics/wave/versions/physics/1/paragraphs/0

Both formulations give bounded rational step-pair approximants satisfying a Cauchy promise in the energy metric. One also supplies a function choosing the index needed for each tolerance. The rate-free formulation supplies no completed state or convergence oracle.

/topics/wave/versions/physics/1/paragraphs/1

The output is a continuous observable represented by polygonal approximations with a prescribed error rate. A completed input state would already provide missing information and would change the question.

/topics/wave/versions/physics/2/paragraphs/0

With a supplied rate, observable reconstruction is provable over RCA₀. The rate-free existence principle is equivalent over that base to ACA₀, arithmetic comprehension. This is a logical classification of the represented reconstruction problem.

/topics/wave/versions/physics/2/paragraphs/1

The reversal needs only a fixed spatial profile and the detector at t=0. It therefore does not attribute the stronger requirement to propagation, infinitely many excited modes or chaotic dynamics.

/topics/wave/versions/physics/3/paragraphs/0

The useful lesson is to distinguish finite numerical data, an error-controlled state description, and a bare existence claim. The theorem classifies the last-to-output step for one input contract.

/topics/wave/versions/physics/3/paragraphs/1

No calibration, stochastic noise model, empirical comparison or full gravitational-field reconstruction is established. The finite detector-recovery benchmark addresses a different question: prediction intervals from specified measurements and an energy bound.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/0/paragraphs/0

Let D consist of rational periodic step pairs p=(a,b), each component of mean zero. Use the squared distance d(p,p′)²=∫[(a−a′)²+(b−b′)²]. A fixed tent function defines a bounded linear observable of translated profiles.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/0/paragraphs/1

The input is a coded sequence in the unit ball of D satisfying the ordinary Cauchy condition. The output is a fast uniform Cauchy sequence of rational periodic polygons representing the limiting observable.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/1/paragraphs/0

The Cauchy promise has the form ∀k ∃N ∀m,n≥N: d(pₘ,pₙ)²≤2⁻²ᵏ. A modulus supplies a function μ(k) witnessing the existential indices. It is not part of the rate-free input.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/1/paragraphs/1

RCA₀ supports the coded rate-supplied construction. ACA₀ adds arithmetic comprehension: existence of sets specified by arithmetic formulas. The question is what is needed to obtain the output when only the promise is given.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/2/paragraphs/0

Writing WR for the specified rate-free principle, the comparison is RCA₀ ⊢ WRμ and RCA₀ ⊢ (WR ↔ ACA₀). The quantifier is “for every input there exists an output”; no higher-type uniform selector is asserted.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/2/paragraphs/1

The upper bound extracts suitable indices with arithmetic comprehension. For the reverse implication, a fixed profile transfers scalar limiting-amplitude information to one detector value at time zero. The familiar completeness phenomenon is realized in an explicit wave-observable representation.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/3/paragraphs/0

Finite rational inequalities and the reduction witnesses have independent computational checks. The infinite reverse-mathematical argument is a human proof, not a proof-assistant-checked theorem.

/topics/wave/versions/mathematics/3/paragraphs/1

Neither RCA₀ as a representation-independent weakest base for wave physics nor a general requirement for ACA₀ in physical measurement follows. The exact coding is part of the statement.

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/0/paragraphs/0

Over RCA₀, WR ↔ ACA₀ for the rate-free observable reconstruction principle on unit-bounded ordinary-Cauchy sequences of rational mean-zero chiral step pairs. The supplied-modulus version WRμ is provable in RCA₀.

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/0/paragraphs/1

Prerequisites: second-order arithmetic and represented analysis. The scalar completeness reversal is standard; the contribution is its realization in this fixed observable together with the matched rate-supplied formulation.

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/1/paragraphs/0

Inputs provide the ordinary Cauchy promise in the squared L² pair metric, without a modulus, completed energy-state name or oracle. Outputs are polygonal names satisfying ‖Aₗ−Aₖ‖∞≤2⁻ᵏ for l≥k, whose limit is the ordinary uniform limit of the input observables.

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/1/paragraphs/1

WR is a second-order ∀input ∃output assertion. Rational-time evaluation and finite polygon comparisons express the required conditions without assuming a function space as a set of all points or a higher-type choice functional.

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/2/paragraphs/0

Uniform boundedness of the observable controls passage from data to output. Arithmetic comprehension supplies modulus information for the upper bound. The reverse reduction is already supported on one fixed spatial profile and a fixed tent detector at t=0.

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/2/paragraphs/1

Thus the lower bound is an amplitude-completion effect, not a propagation obstruction. Read the linked proof for the exact coding and both implications; finite witness checks do not formalize the infinite theorem.

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/3/paragraphs/0

/topics/wave/versions/specialist/3/paragraphs/1
