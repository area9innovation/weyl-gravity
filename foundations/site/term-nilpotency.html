Nilpotency

Mathematical background and its role in the reconstruction problem.

General

Nilpotency means that repeating an operation enough times gives zero. For the differential used here, the required condition is stronger and specific: applying it twice gives zero.

Example and distinction

This is like checking that every two-step route in a bookkeeping system cancels exactly. A nonzero remainder signals an inconsistency in the object being checked, even if most individual components look correct.

Physics

For the BRST differential Q—named after Becchi, Rouet, Stora and Tyutin—the relevant condition is Q²=0. It is a gauge-complex consistency condition, distinct from solving the wave equation.

Use in the model

The project’s current full export fails this prerequisite. A repair must establish the identity for the entire required object before dependent cohomology and transfer claims can be used; selected successful blocks are insufficient.

Mathematics

An operator is nilpotent if some finite positive power vanishes. A differential is specifically square-zero, ensuring that its image lies inside its kernel.

Conditions and consequences

The distinction matters: generic nilpotency of higher order does not define the usual cohomology quotient with the same map. Exact arithmetic can test a finite encoded identity, but only for the supplied operator and domain.

Specialist

Square-zero certification is an import gate, not a conclusion inferred from expected BRST structure. Nonzero Q² terms in the serialized export block the advertised full transfer.

Project role and claim boundary

Record the operator, domain and complete checked scope. The partial trace/ghost correction does not certify the repaired full complex, and a skipped check is not evidence of nilpotency.
