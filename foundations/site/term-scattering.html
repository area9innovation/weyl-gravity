Scattering

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

Scattering studies how an interaction changes incoming disturbances or particles into outgoing ones. It provides one route from a theory to quantities that can be compared with experiments.

Example and distinction

That route assumes the model has meaningful incoming and outgoing states. A formal interaction term alone does not show that such states exist or that their predicted probabilities are sensible.

Physics

A scattering construction compares suitable asymptotic dynamics and states, often through an S-matrix. Its existence, normalization and unitarity depend on the model and physical setting.

Use in the model

Not every spacetime or interacting theory admits the same asymptotic description. A local vertex or free propagator does not establish a complete scattering theory, and comparison with data adds operational assumptions.

Mathematics

Wave operators or other asymptotic constructions relate interacting dynamics to a reference evolution under stated convergence conditions. The resulting scattering map requires appropriate domains and state spaces.

Conditions and consequences

Formal perturbative amplitudes are distinct from an operator theorem about asymptotic completeness or unitary scattering. The relevant limiting argument cannot be inferred from a finite list of coefficients.

Specialist

A residual vertex class classifies an interaction direction; it is not automatically an S-matrix element or a one-particle state. The physical representation and causal interacting construction remain separate obligations.

Project role and claim boundary

No full metric Lorentzian scattering certificate follows from the current local or reduced results. Retain that boundary when placing an amplitude calculation in a theory journey.
