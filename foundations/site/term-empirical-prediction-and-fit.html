Empirical prediction and fit

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

A prediction states what a model expects a specified observation to show. A fit chooses model parameters using data. A good fit and a successful new prediction provide different kinds of evidence.

Example and distinction

A flexible curve can match known points without correctly describing the mechanism behind them. The dataset, parameter freedom, uncertainty model and comparison rule are part of judging what the agreement means.

Physics

Empirical comparison connects a mathematical observable to calibrated measurements and an error model. Fitting estimates parameters under that protocol; predictive validation tests the resulting model on the stated task or data.

Use in the model

Unweighted and uncertainty-weighted scores can rank models differently. A bounded comparison does not establish universal empirical superiority or validate every theoretical component of the model.

Mathematics

A fit solves a specified estimation or optimization problem, with loss function, parameter domain and data treatment fixed. Identifiability and uncertainty require additional analysis.

Conditions and consequences

A small residual norm is a property of that objective on that dataset. It does not itself prove uniqueness of parameters, statistical adequacy or equivalence of the fitted models’ mechanisms.

Specialist

Keep empirical score, acceptance gate and physical interpretation distinct. Record dataset version, weights, nuisance parameters and allowed flexibility before claiming comparative success.

Project role and claim boundary

The galaxy case studies address bounded protocols, not blanket validation of Weyl gravity or another programme. Exact mathematical certificates do not substitute for the observational assumptions entering those tests.
