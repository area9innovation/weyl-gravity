Physical obligation

How to read the programme’s claims and evidence.

General

A physical obligation is a concrete job a model must perform before a claim can be trusted. Examples include defining states, evolving them, assigning probabilities and connecting an observable to data.

Example and distinction

An equation can help with some jobs while leaving others open. The project uses this checklist of mathematical and physical tasks to make progress visible without presenting a partial construction as a finished theory.

Physics

An obligation specifies a target such as state existence, causal propagation, renormalized products or empirical acceptance. Its carrier, assumptions and success criterion must be explicit.

Use in the model

Different calculations discharge different obligations. A local quantum coefficient does not substitute for a positive state, and a fit to a bounded dataset does not establish a complete causal quantum model.

Mathematics

An obligation is an asserted property or construction required of the declared objects. It may concern existence, uniqueness, compatibility, interpretation or an operational map.

Conditions and consequences

The atlas organizes sixteen such task families. A certificate for one encoded witness does not universally quantify over its carrier family unless the theorem explicitly does so; the statement and evidence must retain that distinction.

Specialist

Obligation-based reporting separates classification, coefficients, restoration, transfer and causal certification. These are not interchangeable progress labels for a single undifferentiated quantum result.

Project role and claim boundary

A compatible end-to-end route needs both discharged obligations and verified interfaces. Failures and missing joins should remain explicit even when other cells contain strong results.
