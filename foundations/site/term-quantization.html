Quantization

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

Quantization is the attempt to build a quantum description from classical starting data. It adds rules for observables, states and their probabilities rather than merely making numbers more precise.

Example and distinction

There can be choices and obstructions along the way. A formal recipe may produce useful equations without establishing that all its probabilities are nonnegative or that its full dynamics is well defined.

Physics

Quantization constructs quantum observables and states from a classical model under specified conventions. Gauge symmetry, operator ordering, representations and interactions can introduce additional requirements.

Use in the model

Canonical commutators or a path-integral expression are not alone a complete existence theorem. The programme separates local quantum identities, reduced sectors and full Lorentzian construction rather than treating any one as completion of quantization.

Mathematics

Quantization can refer to several constructions, such as an operator representation, deformation of an observable algebra or a perturbative assignment. The chosen target category and correspondence conditions must be stated.

Conditions and consequences

Formal algebraic existence differs from a positive representation on a Hilbert space or a measure-theoretic construction. A bridge between those objects needs its own proof and may require stronger assumptions.

Specialist

For this programme, certified classical data enter through the import gate before local quantum work is transferred. Classification, coefficient computation, QME restoration and residual transfer are separate lifecycle states.

Project role and claim boundary

None supplies LORENTZIAN_CERTIFIED implicitly. A reduced-mode quantization is evidence for its declared sector, not for the full off-shell propagator, compatible Hadamard state or renormalized Lorentzian products.
