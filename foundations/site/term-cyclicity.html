Cyclicity

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

Cyclicity is a compatibility rule saying that certain rearrangements inside a pairing give the same result, with the signs required by the kinds of objects involved.

Example and distinction

It helps ensure that a reduction preserves more than its equations. A map may respect a differential while failing to respect the pairing that carries the model’s variational or interaction structure.

Physics

Cyclicity expresses compatibility of differentials or operations with the declared graded pairing. It is an additional condition in the classical reduction and quantum transfer programme.

Use in the model

It is not the same as time periodicity, a positive inner product or a trace identity quoted without grading conventions. Pairing and degree data must accompany the formula.

Mathematics

For a differential graded space, cyclic compatibility relates moving the differential or multilinear operations between paired arguments, with signs determined by degrees and pairing conventions.

Conditions and consequences

The exact identity depends on those conventions. A chain map preserves differentials but need not preserve the cyclic structure, so the two checks are logically distinct.

Specialist

Cyclicity is a required classical import check, independent of nilpotency, contraction and chain-map verification. Keep the pairing, suspension conventions and imported degrees explicit.

Project role and claim boundary

The current partial trace/ghost repair does not supply a complete cyclic transfer certificate. No residual quantum interpretation should inherit cyclicity merely because the relevant maps satisfy some unpaired identities.
