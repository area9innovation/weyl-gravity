Positivity of a state

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

Positivity of a quantum state is the condition that quantities representing squared measurement amplitudes receive nonnegative values. It is part of making probabilities sensible.

Example and distinction

This is different from positive energy. It is also a condition on all the relevant observables, not just a few finite tests. The cutoff example shows why passing every separately repaired finite model can still leave the required full construction impossible under its assumptions.

Physics

On a unital star algebra, a state is normalized and positive: its value on A* A is nonnegative for every admissible A. In represented settings this supports the usual nonnegative probability interpretation.

Use in the model

A candidate two-point function must satisfy the relevant positivity and compatibility conditions. Preserving a commutator or satisfying a field equation is not sufficient. Gauge descriptions may require a physical reduction before this interpretation is available.

Mathematics

Positivity of a linear functional ω means ω(A* A)≥0; normalization is ω(1)=1 in the unital setting. The star operation, algebra and domain are part of the definition.

Conditions and consequences

The matrix restrictions induced by finite observable families are necessary checks. A family of independently chosen positive restrictions is not automatically the restriction of one compatible full state with the required regularity.

Specialist

The reduced tensor cutoff result separates finite positive repairs from a single smooth repair preserving the stated commutator across the full declared mode family. Its conclusion is scoped to that reduced reference.

Project role and claim boundary

It does not decide full strict-386 physical positivity or construct a compatible metric BV state. Record the reference, regularity and compatibility conditions before transporting either a positive example or an obstruction.
