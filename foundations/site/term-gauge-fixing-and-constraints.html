Gauge fixing and constraints

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

Gauge fixing chooses representatives among descriptions intended to represent the same physical situation. Constraints restrict which data or configurations are admissible. The two operations are related but not identical.

Example and distinction

Removing a variable because it is inconvenient is not enough. One must show that the choice preserves the intended physical possibilities and that the remaining data still satisfy the relevant restrictions.

Physics

Gauge conditions address redundant descriptions; constraints encode relations that admissible data or solutions must satisfy. Their compatibility, propagation and possible residual gauge freedom depend on the formulation.

Use in the model

A gauge-fixed operator may be easier to invert, but that inverse must still fit the gauge complex. A discarded classical direction is not proven unphysical merely because one convenient gauge omits it.

Mathematics

Gauge fixing can be formulated as a slice, a choice of additional equations or an algebraic procedure on an extended complex. Constraints define a subspace or submanifold only under suitable assumptions.

Conditions and consequences

Existence and uniqueness of representatives can fail globally. Quotienting, imposing equations and choosing coordinates are different mathematical operations and need explicit comparison maps.

Specialist

Retain gauge conditions, constraint class and allowed field content in propagation certificates. Cyclicity or principal-symbol success in a selected ansatz is not certification of every gauge condition.

Project role and claim boundary

The current full-export failure concerns actual differential identities; it cannot be excused by invoking gauge redundancy without a corrected, fully checked construction. Nonlinear and physical-state obligations remain distinct.
