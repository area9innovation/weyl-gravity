Particle and one-particle state

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

A particle is a physical interpretation within a theory, not simply any mathematical solution. A one-particle state represents a single excitation in a setting where that distinction is meaningful. A graviton is a proposed quantum excitation of gravity.

Example and distinction

A wave pattern or a surviving algebraic class may be a candidate ingredient, but more structure is needed to assign probabilities and identify the excitation. The project’s residual deformation classes must not be renamed gravitons on that basis alone.

Physics

A one-particle sector is defined relative to suitable dynamics, state and representation data. On general curved backgrounds there need not be a unique preferred particle interpretation.

Use in the model

Gauge reduction and linear mode counting do not themselves establish positive one-particle norms or an interacting spectrum. The programme treats existence, redundancy, nonlinear continuation and probability as different tests.

Mathematics

A particle interpretation often uses a Hilbert representation and a decomposition of excitations relative to a reference state or symmetry. It is not an intrinsic meaning of an arbitrary cohomology group.

Conditions and consequences

A class can instead parameterize a deformation or local interaction. Before identifying cohomology with states, specify the relevant degree, carrier, pairing and map into the physical representation.

Specialist

The certified residual W₊² and W₋² classes are centered deformation/vertex classes, not one-particle graviton states. Their nontriviality does not establish a physical spectrum.

Project role and claim boundary

The absence of a certified full positive Lorentzian state blocks stronger interpretations that depend on it. Scoped no-go results for propagator witnesses also do not, by themselves, prove the absence of all particle sectors.
