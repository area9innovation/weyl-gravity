Self-adjointness

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

A self-adjoint operator is a suitably well-defined operator that equals its adjoint, the partner determined by the inner product. In infinite-dimensional spaces, this requires agreement of allowed inputs as well as formulas.

Example and distinction

Self-adjoint operators play an important role in quantum observables and evolution. Their eigenvalues need not all be positive, so this condition must not be confused with positive energy.

Physics

Self-adjointness is A=A* with equality of domains in the relevant Hilbert space. For a generator, it supports construction of unitary time evolution under the standard hypotheses.

Use in the model

A symmetric differential expression or a finite Hermitian matrix approximation is insufficient to establish a chosen infinite operator’s self-adjointness. Boundary conditions and extensions must be specified, and a positive physical Hilbert realization is another requirement.

Mathematics

A densely defined symmetric operator is self-adjoint when its domain equals that of its adjoint and their actions coincide. Essential self-adjointness means its closure is self-adjoint.

Conditions and consequences

Symmetry alone may permit multiple extensions or no self-adjoint extension of the desired kind. Spectral conclusions attach to the actual closed operator, not merely to a formal differential expression.

Specialist

Treat self-adjointness as a domain-sensitive analytic claim. A finite exact pairing calculation does not certify it for a completed carrier or a gauge-reduced physical space.

Project role and claim boundary

When interpreting Euclidean spectral data or Lorentzian dynamics, retain the chosen inner product and extension. Self-adjointness does not discharge state positivity, bounded-below energy and interacting causal consistency as one combined gate.
