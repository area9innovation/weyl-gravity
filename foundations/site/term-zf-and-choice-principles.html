ZF and choice principles

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

Stands for: Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory

General

ZF means Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory, a standard rulebook for reasoning about sets. ZFC adds the Axiom of Choice, a further rule allowing simultaneous selections from arbitrary families of nonempty sets.

Example and distinction

Choosing from one finite list and choosing from an unrestricted infinite family are different permissions. The project asks which selections a construction actually uses rather than automatically giving every proof all available choice rules.

Physics

Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory (ZF) specifies a foundational environment; adding Choice gives ZFC. Some state or carrier constructions can use weaker choice principles, or none, depending on their details.

Use in the model

A “choice-free” claim is relative to a declared formulation. It does not mean that no physical parameters were selected, and finite choices in an experiment should not be confused with a set-existence axiom.

Mathematics

ZF stands for Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory. ZF is a set theory without the Axiom of Choice as an axiom; ZFC includes it. Countable choice, dependent choice and unrestricted choice are distinct principles.

Conditions and consequences

An explicit selection function can avoid an appeal to choice for a particular family. The precise domain and hypotheses must be retained when claiming that a construction works in ZF or requires an additional selection principle.

Specialist

ZF stands for Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory. Zermelo–Fraenkel (ZF) and choice-sensitive claims belong to the foundational dependency record. Do not infer their exact strength from a broad atlas regime label.

Project role and claim boundary

Carrier replacements may change which selections are needed, but require maps preserving the intended observables. A result avoiding one choice principle does not thereby establish every completion, positive-state or causal obligation for that carrier.
