Fixed wave-detector benchmark

How to read the programme’s constructions, dependencies and current status.

General

The fixed wave-detector benchmark holds the wave setup and detector rule steady while changing the information supplied about approximations. This isolates the reason one reconstruction task is harder than another.

Example and distinction

An error schedule tells us when an approximation is accurate enough. A bare promise of eventual agreement does not. Since the apparatus stays fixed, the comparison is not explained by switching to a more complicated detector.

Physics

The benchmark uses declared periodic chiral data and a fixed smeared detector. The supplied-rate and rate-free inputs are compared with the same intended observable and output representation.

Use in the model

The necessity argument already works at a fixed time and profile. Its extra logical requirement is amplitude completion, not a change in propagation or an added interaction.

Mathematics

The input consists of coded approximants with stated bounds and Cauchy conditions; the output is a fast name for a fixed scalar functional. Adding a modulus changes the information available in that input.

Conditions and consequences

The physical profile and detector are not varied to manufacture the lower bound. The reduction encodes an arbitrary scalar completion problem within those fixed ingredients.

Specialist

The benchmark realizes a standard scalar completeness reversal within the project’s specified observable. Its mathematical dependency tags are LOCAL-ALGEBRAIC and REDUCED-MODE.

Project role and claim boundary

Do not infer a uniform higher-type solver, a full field algebra or a metric BV state. Finite witness certificates support the encoding, while the infinite equivalence over RCA₀ remains a written argument.
