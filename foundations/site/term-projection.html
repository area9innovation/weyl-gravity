Projection

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

A projection selects a specified part of an object in a consistent way. Applying an ordinary projection twice has the same effect as applying it once.

Example and distinction

Which part is selected matters. Selecting coordinates can lose information needed for an equation, a pairing or a detector reading. A physically useful reduction must show that the relevant structures survive.

Physics

A projection can select a sector or participate in a reduction to residual data. Orthogonal, gauge and homological projections have different compatibility requirements.

Use in the model

A projection formula is not automatically gauge-compatible or support-preserving. In the observable extension audit, a simple metric block can preserve compact support while a different required chain-map relation still fails.

Mathematics

An endomorphism P is idempotent when P²=P. In a contraction, a map p:C→H is instead paired with an inclusion i:H→C satisfying pi=1; ip is the associated endomorphism.

Conditions and consequences

Orthogonality additionally refers to an inner product. Neither idempotence nor a right-inverse identity alone gives a chain map or preservation of cohomological and analytic structures.

Specialist

Reserve pi_cl for homological projection and P for kinetic or witness operators. Inspect the actual projection and inclusion coefficients before asserting transport compatibility.

Project role and claim boundary

The full metric projection’s audited defect is substantive even though some order-zero source-lift identities hold. Preserve those separate results rather than treating one successful projection property as certification of all others.
