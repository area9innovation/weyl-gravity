Vacuum

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

A vacuum is a chosen quantum state regarded as a reference with no excitations of the specified kind. It is not simply the absence of mathematical structure or all physical effects.

Example and distinction

What counts as an excitation depends on the theory and reference setting. Curved or time-dependent spacetime may not supply one preferred vacuum for every observer or construction.

Physics

A vacuum choice specifies a reference state, often selected by symmetries or a ground-state condition where available. Its correlations and particle interpretation depend on the representation.

Use in the model

A mode prescription called a vacuum must still satisfy positivity and the required field, gauge and regularity conditions. Those conditions cannot be inferred from its name.

Mathematics

A vacuum is extra state data, not a property of an algebra alone. A ground-state or symmetry-invariance criterion can select one in some settings but requires explicit dynamics and hypotheses.

Conditions and consequences

Hadamard short-distance behavior does not generally single out a unique state. The symmetric part of a two-point function can vary while preserving a fixed commutator and local singularity class.

Specialist

The reduced vacuum-cylinder reference is a specified model input with provenance and sign conventions. Its acceptance and possible repairs are scoped to that carrier.

Project role and claim boundary

The reconciled reduced-reference hash does not certify the entire transitive classical import or a full metric vacuum. The current full-export consistency failure remains a separate barrier to dependent state constructions.
