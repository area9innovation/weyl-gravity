Fourier mode

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

Fourier modes are simple oscillating patterns used as building blocks for more complicated signals or waves. Their coefficients tell us how much of each pattern is present.

Example and distinction

A sound can be described by several frequencies. An individual oscillation usually extends across the whole domain, so retaining only finitely many frequencies does not automatically produce a spatially confined disturbance.

Physics

A Fourier expansion resolves data into oscillatory modes adapted to the domain and boundary conventions. The normalization determines coefficients, derivative weights and energy formulas.

Use in the model

Finite mode calculations can be exact. An infinite expansion requires convergence in a declared topology, and its support or state interpretation cannot be inferred solely from the coefficient list.

Mathematics

On a periodic domain, complex exponentials form the familiar Fourier family. Differentiation multiplies coefficients by frequency factors, with constants fixed by the period convention.

Conditions and consequences

Formal coefficient manipulations require analytic justification for infinite sums. Different convergence notions—pointwise, norm, distributional or smooth—support different subsequent operations.

Specialist

The finite chiral fixtures exploit finite sums and integer frequency weights in the chosen normalization. That explains their exact arithmetic without granting a general infinite expansion theorem.

Project role and claim boundary

Galerkin nesting, completed energy spaces and causal localization remain additional obligations. Do not identify finite support in Fourier coefficients with compact support in spacetime.
