Galaxy rotation curve

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

A galaxy rotation curve describes how orbital speed varies with distance from a galaxy’s center. It is used to compare gravitational models with inferred motion in galaxies.

Example and distinction

The curve is not a direct reading of the gravity equation alone. Distances, viewing angle, visible matter and measurement errors enter its interpretation, so a model comparison needs those choices stated.

Physics

Rotation-curve modeling connects a gravitational potential and matter model to inferred circular velocities under the declared geometry and dynamical assumptions. Systematic and measurement uncertainties affect the test.

Use in the model

The project’s conformal-gravity comparisons use bounded datasets and protocols. A successful or failed fit there should not be generalized to every galaxy or every realization of the theory.

Mathematics

The comparison is an inverse or estimation problem: model parameters determine a velocity profile, and data constrain them through a chosen loss and uncertainty model.

Conditions and consequences

Parameter degeneracies, nuisance inputs and correlations can change what is identifiable. Minimizing one residual measure is not equivalent to passing every statistical acceptance criterion.

Specialist

Retain the dataset, radial selection, normalization, mass-model freedom and weighting in the empirical claim. The relevant evidence is protocol-specific rather than a universal theorem about galactic dynamics.

Project role and claim boundary

These tests neither certify nor refute the full quantum construction. A theory journey must connect the particular classical prediction to its operational and observational inputs without importing unrelated certificate strength.
