Representation of data

Mathematical background and its role in the reconstruction problem.

General

A representation is the form in which mathematical information is supplied. Two descriptions can refer to the same intended object while giving different help in finding an answer.

Example and distinction

A sequence of approximations accompanied by an error schedule tells you when to stop for a requested accuracy. A promise that the sequence eventually settles does not tell you where. The project compares precisely this difference.

Physics

A data representation specifies what an input code provides and what an output code must contain. It is distinct from the physical identity of the intended field or detector.

Use in the model

Supplying a convergence rate changes the reconstruction problem without changing the intended wave observable. Before comparing models or algorithms, check whether their interfaces deliver the same information, not just similarly named quantities.

Mathematics

A representation connects admissible codes or names with the objects they denote. Multiple names can denote one object, and representations can differ in the information their names supply.

Conditions and consequences

The relevant theorem includes both input promises and the requested output representation. Existence of an abstract limit does not automatically supply a fast Cauchy name in the chosen base theory.

Specialist

Representation is part of the WR/WRμ specification. The observable and physical benchmark remain fixed while the input’s convergence information changes.

Project role and claim boundary

A representation-invariance claim needs explicit translations preserving the intended observable and the theorem’s available resources. Identifying two abstract carriers is not itself a resource-preserving translation of their names.
