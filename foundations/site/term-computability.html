Computability

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

Computability asks whether a specified procedure can produce the requested answer from the information supplied. It is different from knowing that some answer exists.

Example and distinction

An error schedule can let a procedure choose an approximation at any requested accuracy. A promise that approximations eventually settle may not reveal when to stop. The input format therefore matters as much as the intended limiting object.

Physics

Computability concerns effective access to a represented output from represented input. It is distinct from computational cost, numerical stability and experimental feasibility.

Use in the model

A detector functional can be continuous while the input lacks information needed for its demanded output name. The wave comparison isolates this issue without changing the profile or propagation, but its logical equivalence is not itself a runtime bound.

Mathematics

A computability statement specifies an encoding, a class of algorithms and an input-output relation. For infinite objects, algorithms often produce names or successively refined approximations rather than one finite exact numeral.

Conditions and consequences

The available oracle or set parameters matter. A nonconstructive existence proof does not automatically compute the object, and a computable finite approximation need not come with a computable convergence modulus.

Specialist

Keep effectivity, logical strength and uniform realizability separate. WR/WRμ fixes the representation and requested fast output; its reversal is not a blanket classification of computable wave evolution.

Project role and claim boundary

A finite exact implementation checks declared witnesses. It does not certify a higher-type uniform algorithm or discharge the infinite argument by testing a large finite sample.
