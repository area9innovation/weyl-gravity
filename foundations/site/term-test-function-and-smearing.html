Test function and smearing

Mathematical background and its role in the reconstruction problem.

General

A test function describes how a detector weights different locations. Smearing means combining a field with that weighting to obtain an averaged quantity rather than demanding a value at one ideal point.

Example and distinction

Imagine a microphone with a finite region of sensitivity. Its reading depends on both the incoming wave and its response profile. The wave benchmark fixes such a profile so the comparison changes the input information, not the detector.

Physics

Smearing pairs a field or distribution with an admissible test function to define a detector functional. Test regularity, support and normalization specify what the measurement idealization assumes.

Use in the model

The resulting observable can have explicit continuity bounds even when pointwise field evaluation is unsuitable. These bounds control detector error but do not themselves reconstruct an amplitude from rate-free approximation data.

Mathematics

A distribution acts on a specified test-function space; smearing is this dual pairing. For an ordinary integrable field it may reduce to integrating its product with the test function.

Conditions and consequences

Continuity is relative to the test-space topology. Compact support and smoothness are common conditions, not interchangeable with finite support of a coefficient sequence. The domain and allowed test class belong to the statement.

Specialist

The fixed detector is part of the WR benchmark and its reversal. Its profile, estimates and output representation must be held fixed when identifying the logical cost.

Project role and claim boundary

A smeared scalar observable does not supply a full local observable algebra, causal products or a state on the metric BV complex. Preserve that boundary when using the example to motivate the wider programme.
