Continuum limit

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

A continuum limit is a proposed passage from discrete or truncated descriptions to a model with continuously varying space, time or unrestricted field data. It requires a rule saying how the approximations approach the intended object.

Example and distinction

Making a grid finer or adding more modes is not itself proof that predictions settle. The quantities being compared, their error controls and the properties required of the limit all have to be specified.

Physics

A continuum limit removes a discretization or regulator in a declared sense, often while adjusting parameters or controlling observables. Mode completion and lattice-spacing limits are related examples with different details.

Use in the model

Convergence of one observable does not establish locality, all correlations or a positive full state. The required topology and compatibility conditions depend on the claimed physical construction.

Mathematics

A limiting construction requires objects, comparison maps and a convergence notion or universal property. Completing a normed space and taking a regulator-removal limit are not identical operations.

Conditions and consequences

Existence at each finite stage is weaker than existence of compatible limiting data. Uniform estimates, compactness or explicit construction can supply bridges, but each introduces assumptions that must be recorded.

Specialist

The atlas separates finite carriers from continuum and local field-theoretic carriers. A finite witness or detector reconstruction does not cover every interface needed to reach the latter.

Project role and claim boundary

Retain exact versus numerical result types and the declared observable scope. The reduced positivity obstruction demonstrates a specific failure of full regularity despite finite repairability; it is not a universal continuum no-go theorem.
