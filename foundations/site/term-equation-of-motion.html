Equation of motion

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

An equation of motion states which configurations or histories obey a model’s dynamics. It can describe a stationary field as well as literal motion.

Example and distinction

An action is one way to derive such equations. Finding an equation is only the start: initial data, boundaries and an allowed solution space are needed before asking whether it has a useful solution.

Physics

Equations of motion are dynamical conditions, often obtained as Euler–Lagrange equations. Gauge identities are relations among those equations, not additional independent motions.

Use in the model

A configuration can solve the equations while failing global or boundary conditions. A linearized solution also need not extend to the interacting equations or define a positive quantum state.

Mathematics

An equation of motion is a differential or variational relation on the chosen configuration space. Its order, domain and weak formulation determine what counts as a solution.

Conditions and consequences

Identities among equations can make the system constrained or redundant. A zero residual for a finite ansatz proves satisfaction in that declared setting, not existence or uniqueness for arbitrary data.

Specialist

Keep equations, gauge generators and Noether identities distinct in the encoded complex. Their compatibility must be checked on the actual coefficients and grading.

Project role and claim boundary

A classical equation residual is not a BV quantum master-equation test. Neither exact finite solutions nor local quantum identities supply the full Lorentzian state and renormalization constructions.
