Mode and amplitude

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

A mode is a chosen building-block pattern; its amplitude says how much of that pattern is present. Combining modes with different amplitudes can describe a more complicated wave.

Example and distinction

A musical sound can be described by several tones and their strengths. Having only finitely many tones makes one model finite, but a limit involving infinitely many still needs a convergence rule. The amplitudes contain information, not just decoration on the equation.

Physics

A mode decomposition expresses a field in a chosen family of profiles with scalar or vector coefficients. The coefficients are amplitudes; the basis and normalization determine their meaning.

Use in the model

A finite truncation keeps selected coefficients. In the wave reversal, a single amplitude carries the limiting information while the profile and detector remain fixed. The result therefore does not depend on complicated mode mixing.

Mathematics

Modes are elements of a specified spanning or spectral family, and amplitudes are their coefficients. Infinite expansions require a topology and convergence statement beyond formal linear combination.

Conditions and consequences

Different notions of basis have different reconstruction properties. The finite fixtures avoid those issues by declaring a labelled finite family; completion and representation questions enter only at additional stages.

Specialist

Keep the labelled finite mode set, normalization and coefficient domain in the certificate. A reduced-mode result has a different scope from the full field complex.

Project role and claim boundary

The amplitude encoding used by WR is a logical reduction within a fixed physical profile. It is neither a particle-state construction nor a claim that arbitrary full-field data have a certified mode expansion.
