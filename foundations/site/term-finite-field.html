Finite field

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

A finite field is an algebraic number system with finitely many elements in which addition, multiplication and division by nonzero elements obey field rules. It is not a physical field with finitely many wave modes.

Example and distinction

Arithmetic modulo a prime gives a simple example. This dictionary separates the two meanings because “finite field” and “finite-mode field model” describe different mathematical choices.

Physics

A finite field is a coefficient-domain choice, not a mode cutoff or a finite spatial lattice. It changes arithmetic, including its characteristic.

Use in the model

A physical model using finitely many rational-complex coefficients still uses an infinite scalar field. Results obtained after reduction modulo a prime need a justified bridge before being used as claims about the original rational or real model.

Mathematics

A finite field has order pⁿ for a prime p and positive integer n. In characteristic p, adding one to itself p times gives zero.

Conditions and consequences

Reduction modulo a prime can alter ranks or erase nonzero coefficients. The coefficient domain must remain part of any exact certificate; finite cardinality and finite-dimensionality are independent properties.

Specialist

The atlas’s finite-field and finite-mode constructions are not equivalent merely because both admit finite computation. Preserve the scalar domain, carrier and obligation in comparisons.

Project role and claim boundary

An identity verified in a finite field is evidence in that field. It does not automatically prove the characteristic-zero identity or supply the continuum interpretation used by a wave or gravity theorem.
