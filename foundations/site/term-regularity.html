Regularity

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

Regularity describes how smooth or controlled a mathematical object is: for example, whether it has derivatives and whether those derivatives remain bounded in the required sense.

Example and distinction

A separate finite approximation can always be smooth while the attempted infinite limit becomes singular. The required amount of smoothness is therefore an actual condition on the full construction, not a cosmetic preference.

Physics

Regularity specifies the differentiability or integrability class needed for fields, kernels or observables. Different norms and derivative bounds provide different controls.

Use in the model

In the reduced positivity obstruction, the allowed correction must be one smooth kernel preserving the reference constraints. Positive repairs at each finite cutoff do not satisfy that full requirement merely because each finite repair is smooth.

Mathematics

Regularity may be expressed through Cᵏ, Sobolev, distributional or microlocal conditions. Each identifies a different class; greater differentiability and control of singular directions are not interchangeable statements.

Conditions and consequences

Convergence in a weak topology does not imply convergence of every derivative. A limiting argument must state which seminorms are controlled uniformly and which regularity properties survive the passage to the limit.

Specialist

The reduced obstruction quantifies over a declared smooth correction class, not all conceivable changes of carrier, involution or commutator. Preserve those hypotheses when reusing its conclusion.

Project role and claim boundary

For the full metric complex, regularity is only one of several requirements. A Hadamard-type singularity statement cannot substitute for positive type, nilpotency or a compatible gauge descent.
