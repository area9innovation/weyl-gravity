TT sector

How to read the programme’s constructions, dependencies and current status.

Stands for: transverse–traceless

General

TT stands for transverse–traceless. It describes tensor fields satisfying specified divergence-free and zero-trace conditions with respect to a chosen background. The TT sector selects fields of that kind.

Example and distinction

This can isolate a useful class of wave-like perturbations. It does not mean that every field satisfying those linear conditions has already been shown to be a physical graviton in the complete theory.

Physics

The transverse–traceless (TT) restriction is a background-dependent tensor decomposition used in the reduced mode analysis. Its gauge interpretation and completeness require the relevant constraints and boundary conditions.

Use in the model

A TT calculation can still involve infinitely many modes. Finite-cutoff positivity and positivity on the complete selected sector remain different tests, and neither is automatically full metric-state positivity.

Mathematics

TT stands for transverse–traceless. Transverse–traceless (TT) conditions set an appropriate divergence and metric contraction to zero. The background connection, metric and functional domain specify those equations.

Conditions and consequences

Projection onto that sector can be nonlocal or require additional hypotheses. A formal component restriction is not automatically a support-preserving map or a homological equivalence of the full complex.

Specialist

The transverse–traceless (TT) carrier used by the reduced reference has its own normalization, pairing and representatives. It is not identified with the centered W₊²/W₋² deformation classes.

Project role and claim boundary

The positivity obstruction remains REDUCED-MODE. The compact-source lift and unresolved full all-energy commutator comparison must be retained before drawing conclusions about the complete metric complex.
