Hyperbolicity

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

Hyperbolicity describes mathematical features of an equation that support wave-like evolution and finite-speed influence under suitable conditions. Different kinds of hyperbolicity impose different requirements.

Example and distinction

The term is not a guarantee that every proposed field theory has a complete causal construction. Starting data, constraints, boundaries and the whole set of coupled variables still matter.

Physics

A normally hyperbolic second-order operator has wave-type principal part, proportional to the inverse metric times the identity on field components. Strong hyperbolicity concerns well-controlled characteristic structure for evolution systems.

Use in the model

For standard first-order formulations, real characteristic speeds alone are insufficient without appropriate uniform control. The exact formulation and coefficient regularity must accompany any well-posedness conclusion.

Mathematics

Hyperbolicity is a property of a specified principal symbol or evolution formulation. It does not identify the full lower-order operator, its domain or the global spacetime assumptions.

Conditions and consequences

Normally hyperbolic scalar-like systems and strongly hyperbolic first-order systems are different classes. Converting between formulations requires checking the allowed variables, constraints and preservation of the intended solution problem.

Specialist

The two programme no-go results exclude the 24-field scalar normally-hyperbolic witness and the fixed-temporal cyclic 46-parameter first-order family. Their scopes must remain explicit.

Project role and claim boundary

They do not refute the classical BRST complex, residual cohomology or every possible physical spectrum or causal formulation. A new architecture needs a new certificate rather than an inference from either excluded family.
