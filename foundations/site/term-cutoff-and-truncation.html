Cutoff and truncation

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

A cutoff limits which parts of a model are included. A truncation keeps a finite or otherwise restricted part, such as only the first several wave modes.

Example and distinction

This can make a calculation manageable. But an answer that works for each cutoff separately need not produce one answer that remains compatible as the cutoff is removed. The project uses that difference as a central example.

Physics

A cutoff restricts momenta, modes, volume or another declared feature. Its removal requires appropriate convergence, compatibility and sometimes renormalization conditions.

Use in the model

For the reduced positivity example, each finite mode set can admit a repair, while no single smooth repair satisfies the full stated constraints. That is a scoped full-family obstruction, not a claim that every finite approximation is useless.

Mathematics

Truncation produces a restricted family of problems indexed by a bound. Passing from solutions at every finite index to one full solution exchanges quantifiers and requires a compatibility or compactness argument.

Conditions and consequences

A nested sequence of finite spaces supplies organization, not automatically convergence of solutions or preservation of positivity. The topology and uniform estimates determine which limiting conclusions are justified.

Specialist

Keep the cutoff parameter, restricted sector, reference pairing and correction class explicit. The reduced result rules out a smooth full repair under its commutator constraint, not every possible full theory.

Project role and claim boundary

Finite exact verification establishes the stated fixture properties. It cannot be promoted to completion, causal support or an infinite state theorem merely because all sampled cutoffs pass.
