Reduced positivity obstruction

How to read the programme’s constructions, dependencies and current status.

General

The reduced positivity obstruction shows, for a specified tensor model, that repairing every finite cutoff does not provide one acceptable repair of the whole selected mode family.

Example and distinction

The repairs can become increasingly uncontrolled as more modes are included. The conclusion depends on keeping the model’s commutator and smoothness requirements fixed; changing those conditions changes the problem.

Physics

On the stated reduced spectral reference, each finite spatial tensor cutoff admits a positive covariance with the same truncated commutator. No single smooth correction supplies positivity over the full declared family under the theorem’s hypotheses.

Use in the model

The argument allows more than stationary or mode-diagonal repairs. It remains a REDUCED-MODE result and does not decide the full metric physical-state problem without the required observable crosswalk.

Mathematics

The statement separates finite solvability from existence in a prescribed smooth full-kernel class. The singularity and antisymmetric parts are fixed, while positivity is tested on the relevant smeared observables.

Conditions and consequences

Uniform regularity fails as the cutoff increases. The analytic proof is supported by exact finite witnesses, but those finite checks alone are not a proof of the infinite quantified assertion.

Specialist

The original reduced reference had a stale dependency; the observable-extension work reconciles it on a separate copy and passes direct checks. This does not certify every transitive full-classical premise.

Project role and claim boundary

The full projection/commutator interface remains unresolved and the full export fails consistency. Preserve the REDUCED-MODE tag, fixed involution, branch content and smooth-repair assumptions; no full Lorentzian lifecycle state is promoted.
