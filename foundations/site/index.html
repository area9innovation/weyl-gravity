What would make an equation a theory?

An equation can describe how something changes without telling us everything we need to know. What can we prepare? What can we measure? Does the prediction agree with an experiment? Those questions need answers before an attractive equation becomes a useful physical theory.

This project asks which assumptions make each answer possible. It began with Weyl gravity, a proposed description of gravity, and grew into an investigation of the mathematics that connects equations to predictions.

From a field equation to a physical prediction

The programme separates equations of motion from state selection, gauge reduction, probability, causal response and comparison with data. Pure-Weyl gravity supplies a demanding case study: a local or mode-level construction does not by itself establish a viable interacting quantum theory.

The additional question is mathematical: which assumptions about the state space and the representation of its elements are actually used to construct an observable?

Which assumptions does a prediction use?

A physical theorem combines assumptions about a model, a class of mathematical objects, and a system in which existence is asserted. The programme separates these choices before asking what is sufficient for a specified conclusion.

For the mathematical reader, the central example is a represented wave observable: rational approximations describe inputs and outputs, and their convergence information is part of the definition of the problem.

Scope and contribution

The programme audits implications from physical assumptions and represented mathematical carriers to explicit obligations. Its reverse-mathematical component should be distinguished from characterization of a gravitational action by physical postulates.

The introductory entry points are a represented wave reconstruction comparison and a conditional reduced-tensor positivity separation. Neither is offered as a new general completeness theorem or a newly discovered ghost mechanism.

What is being worked out?

There are two connected questions. Which physical requirements distinguish possible theories of gravity? And which mathematical assumptions are needed to turn a description into a prediction?

For example, suppose a computer produces increasingly detailed pictures of a wave. Knowing that these pictures eventually agree is different from knowing how detailed a picture must be to meet a chosen accuracy. The latter gives a usable error guarantee.

The role of reverse mathematics

Reverse mathematics asks which axioms are sufficient for a theorem and, where possible, which are necessary. Here the input contract matters: a convergent sequence of wave data supplied with an error schedule is a different mathematical input from a sequence merely promised to converge.

This distinction concerns what the model supplies to the prediction procedure. It is not evidence that a laboratory instrument must implement a particular formal system.

Existence with and without a rate

A Cauchy condition says that for each tolerance there is an index beyond which all approximants agree within that tolerance. A supplied modulus gives a function selecting suitable indices. In weak foundations, extracting such information is an additional mathematical task.

Reverse mathematics studies equivalences over a specified base theory. RCA₀ is the weak base used here for coded constructions; ACA₀ additionally allows sets defined by arithmetic predicates. These are systems of axioms, not accuracy levels.

What have we learned?

In a particular wave model, an explicit error guarantee lets us construct a detector prediction using relatively modest mathematical assumptions. Removing that guarantee changes the mathematical existence problem, even though the wave and detector have not changed.

A separate model shows why checking positivity at every finite cutoff need not produce one acceptable state for the full continuum. Positivity here means that the mathematical rule for probabilities cannot assign negative values to the relevant tests. Both examples have precise restrictions; neither establishes a complete theory of gravity.

Terms used in this account

ACA₀

General ACA₀ extends the starting rulebook RCA₀ with arithmetic comprehension: permission to collect numbers satisfying an arithmetic condition into a set, even when no general decision procedure is supplied.

Physics ACA₀ adds arithmetic comprehension to the base theory. In this wave problem it supplies missing tail-selection information needed to turn a rate-free Cauchy input into the required named limit.

Mathematics The extension of RCA₀ by comprehension for arithmetical formulas: number quantifiers are allowed, with set parameters, but no bound set variables. The wave statement WR is equivalent to this extension over RCA₀.

Specialist The exact strength of WR over RCA₀ for the declared rate-free representation. The upper bound selects Cauchy-tail witnesses; the lower bound recovers range existence through one scalar amplitude at t=0.

Carrier

General The kind of mathematical structure in which a model is built. A finite collection of matrices, a space of waves, and an algebra of quantum quantities are different carriers, with different questions that must be checked.

Physics The specified mathematical realization carrying the model’s variables, operations and states: for example finite matrices, a completed function space or a local algebraic construction. Changing carrier may change which analytic obligations are meaningful.

Mathematics The chosen structured ambient object, including any topology, pairing, operator domains and completion data needed for a result. The matrix uses carrier as an organizational dimension rather than as one universally defined category.

Specialist The typed support of a construction in the atlas. Cross-carrier transfer requires a declared interface preserving the relevant operations and hypotheses; matching labels or finite fixtures do not establish equivalence.

Convergence rate (modulus)

General An error schedule: for each requested accuracy, it tells you how far along an approximation sequence you must go so that all later approximations agree to that accuracy.

Physics A tolerance-to-index function controlling all later differences between input approximants in a specified norm. Supplying this function is stronger input information than promising that the sequence eventually settles.

Mathematics A function μ such that m,n≥μ(k) implies d(pₘ,pₙ)≤2⁻ᵏ. The Cauchy statement asserts existence of a suitable index for each k; a supplied modulus gives those indices as one input function.

Specialist An input function witnessing the ordinary-Cauchy promise in the exact second-order representation. WR omits it; WRμ includes it. This changes the completion obligation while preserving the detector and dynamics.

Observable

General A quantity a model connects to a possible measurement, such as position, energy, or an average wave amplitude. The observable tells us what we are asking about; the state supplies the information used to predict it.

Physics A specified quantity evaluated on a classical state, or represented by an appropriate operator or algebra element in quantum theory. Its mathematical definition and domain must be fixed before discussing predictions.

Mathematics A map extracting a quantity from a state, with the required structure depending on the model. In the wave example it is a bounded linear functional; quantum observables instead belong to an operator or algebraic framework.

Specialist The observable notion is carrier-dependent. The WR benchmark fixes a bounded linear detector functional on chiral energy data; it makes no identification with the full gauge-invariant Weyl observable algebra.

RCA₀

General RCA₀ is the modest mathematical rulebook used as the starting point for this comparison. Its name comes from Recursive Comprehension Axiom. It lets the project distinguish what can already be proved from what needs an additional existence assumption.

Physics RCA₀ is a weak base theory for reasoning about coded numbers, sequences and countable mathematical objects. It is the bookkeeping framework for the wave comparison, not an extra law governing the wave.

Mathematics A subsystem of second-order arithmetic with elementary arithmetic, Σ⁰₁ induction and Δ⁰₁ comprehension, allowing set parameters. It serves as the base over which the stated reconstruction equivalence is proved.

Specialist The base for WR↔ACA₀ and WRμ. The input/output coding and all reductions must be available over RCA₀; importing an already completed input would invalidate the stated comparison.

Reverse mathematics

General A way of asking which mathematical assumptions a theorem really needs. First show that certain assumptions prove the theorem; then reason backwards from the theorem to see whether it recovers those assumptions.

Physics An analysis of the axiom strength of a mathematical assertion after its inputs, outputs and representations have been fixed. It can separate the cost of taking limits from the physical evolution being modeled.

Mathematics The study of implications and reversals over a specified base theory. An equivalence B⊢(T↔S) calibrates T against the additional principles S, with B held fixed.

Specialist An equivalence or implication analysis whose base, representation and quantifier form are part of the theorem. Here the focal comparison is RCA₀⊢WRμ versus RCA₀⊢(WR↔ACA₀).

State

General The mathematical information a model uses to make predictions about a system. What counts as a state depends on the model: a moving particle may need position and velocity, while a quantum model uses a different kind of description.

Physics A classical phase-space point or field initial datum, or a quantum density operator or normalized positive functional on an observable algebra. The carrier and positivity requirements must be stated; a cohomology class is not automatically a physical state.

Mathematics A model-dependent object on which predictions depend. Examples include a phase-space point and a normalized positive linear functional ω on a unital *-algebra, satisfying ω(A* A)≥0 and ω(1)=1.

Specialist Keep classical data, algebraic positive states, BRST state cohomology, and residual deformation classes separate. The existence or computation of one does not establish the others or an accepted full Lorentzian state construction.
