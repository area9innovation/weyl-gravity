Norm

Mathematical background and its role in the reconstruction problem.

General

A norm assigns a size to an object, generalizing the length of an arrow. It lets us express how large an error is and whether approximations are getting close.

Example and distinction

For a wave, one norm may emphasize total energy while another detects sharp local changes. Saying an error is small without naming the norm can therefore hide a meaningful difference.

Physics

A norm measures field or state size and sets the error notion used by estimates. Different norms control different observables or derivatives.

Use in the model

A useful reconstruction bound should connect the input norm to detector error. An indefinite quadratic form is not a norm merely because it resembles an energy expression; a norm must be nonnegative and separate zero from nonzero vectors.

Mathematics

A norm is a nonnegative function with definiteness, absolute homogeneity and the triangle inequality. It induces a metric by the norm of a difference.

Conditions and consequences

An inner product induces a norm, but not every norm comes from an inner product. Completeness in the induced metric defines a Banach space; completeness with a positive inner product gives a Hilbert space.

Specialist

Norm choice fixes the meaning of the Cauchy and continuity hypotheses used at an interface. Norm estimates are analytic data beyond finite exact algebraic identities.

Project role and claim boundary

The reduced positivity obstruction concerns a specified full regularity requirement, not arbitrary norm convergence. Retain the actual carrier and quantitative bounds when transporting a result or comparing finite approximations.
