Galerkin approximation

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

A Galerkin approximation searches for a solution inside a chosen finite space, often spanned by a few modes, and tests the equation against a corresponding finite set of probes.

Example and distinction

Adding more modes gives larger approximation spaces. If the spaces are nested, earlier possibilities remain available, but the solutions still need not converge or preserve every property of the full problem.

Physics

A Galerkin method projects or weakly tests an equation on a finite-dimensional trial space. Trial spaces, test spaces and projection choices determine the method.

Use in the model

Nesting is a structural property of the sequence of spaces. Stability, convergence, constraint compatibility and local support require separate arguments, especially when a mode truncation is used in a gauge theory.

Mathematics

The finite problem enforces a residual condition against the selected test space. Convergence depends on consistency, approximation properties and estimates appropriate to the equation and topology.

Conditions and consequences

A sequence of nested subspaces need not come with compatible selected solutions or a controlled limit. Exact vanishing for particular fixtures establishes their identities, not a general Galerkin convergence theorem.

Specialist

The ladder certifies Galerkin nesting within its labelled finite chiral construction. Its stated exclusions include completion, spatial localization and causal support.

Project role and claim boundary

Preserve that finite scope. A theorem supplying uniform estimates or an extension to a completed carrier is an additional result, not an interpretation already contained in the nesting certificate.
