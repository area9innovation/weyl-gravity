Symmetry

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

A symmetry is a transformation that preserves some specified feature of a model, such as its equations or predictions. The feature being preserved must be named.

Example and distinction

Turning an experiment without changing its outcome can express rotational symmetry. A change of mathematical description that represents the same physical situation is a gauge redundancy, a more specific idea explained separately.

Physics

A symmetry acts on fields or states while preserving the declared dynamics or observables. Global symmetries, spacetime symmetries and gauge redundancies need different treatment.

Use in the model

A symmetry of a classical action may face a separate quantum consistency question. Its preservation in one truncation or sector does not establish preservation by the full interacting construction.

Mathematics

A symmetry can be described by a group action or other transformation structure preserving specified data. Preservation of a set, equation, pairing or measure are distinct conditions.

Conditions and consequences

An isomorphism of underlying spaces need not preserve every structure of the model. State which operations and equivalences are retained before interpreting the transformation as a physical symmetry.

Specialist

Symmetry assumptions belong among the physical premises, while their encoded differential identities are mathematical obligations. Keep classical invariance, local anomaly classification and quantum identity restoration separate.

Project role and claim boundary

The presence of a BRST-style formula does not certify nilpotency of its serialized implementation. Nor does a surviving cohomology class automatically establish a symmetry-preserving physical state.
