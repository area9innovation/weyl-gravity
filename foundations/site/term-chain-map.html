Chain map

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

A chain map connects two differential complexes while respecting their consistency operations. Applying the map and then the differential must agree with doing those steps in the reverse order.

Example and distinction

This is one precise meaning of two descriptions fitting together. It does not automatically say that lengths, probabilities or causal locations are preserved; those require additional checks.

Physics

A chain map intertwines the differentials of two gauge or homological descriptions. It can support a reduction or transport of classes when the complexes are valid.

Use in the model

Gauge compatibility, pairing preservation and causal support are distinct conditions. The current projection audit shows why a formal transport claim must be tested on the actual exported maps rather than assumed from their construction labels.

Mathematics

A degree-zero map f between cochain complexes is a chain map when d_D f=f d_C. It then induces a map on cohomology.

Conditions and consequences

It need not induce an isomorphism, preserve a pairing or have a continuous inverse. Those are additional assertions. If either advertised differential fails square-zero, the usual induced-cohomology interpretation is unavailable.

Specialist

The full-export chain-map identity must be checked directly for the supplied differential and projection or inclusion. Formal transport metadata cannot substitute for the coefficient identity.

Project role and claim boundary

The trace/ghost candidate supplies selected repaired identities, but full nilpotency, chain-map and cyclic compatibility remain separate gates. A partial repair is not a certified full transfer.
