Supplied-rate and rate-free wave reconstruction

How to read the programme’s claims and evidence.

Stands for: wave-reconstruction assertion

General

WR names the wave-reconstruction assertion with a rate-free Cauchy input. WRμ names its supplied-rate version; μ denotes an error schedule. Both ask for a reliably approximated reading of the same fixed wave detector.

Example and distinction

With a schedule, the input says when later approximations are accurate enough. Without one, it only promises that they eventually agree. The project proves that this difference changes the mathematical existence principles needed for the declared output.

Physics

The benchmark fixes the wave profile and detector while comparing rate-free Cauchy data with data carrying a modulus. WRμ is provable in RCA₀; WR is equivalent to ACA₀ over that base.

Use in the model

The lower bound already occurs at fixed time and profile. It identifies amplitude completion as the additional requirement, rather than propagation, detector complexity or an empirical precision limit.

Mathematics

The assertions use specified input codings and require a fast output name for the detector observable. Supplying μ allows explicit reconstruction; omitting it permits a reduction from range existence over RCA₀.

Conditions and consequences

The scalar completeness reversal is standard background. The project embeds it in the fixed wave benchmark and supplies finite witnesses, while the infinite equivalence remains a written argument rather than a completed proof-assistant formalization.

Specialist

Preserve both directions and the representation boundary: RCA₀ proves WRμ, and over RCA₀ the declared WR is equivalent to ACA₀. No higher-type uniform reconstruction theorem is claimed.

Project role and claim boundary

The benchmark does not supply a full Lorentzian metric propagator, positive quantum state or causal interacting construction. Its contribution is a controlled comparison locating the logical cost with the physical profile and detector unchanged.
