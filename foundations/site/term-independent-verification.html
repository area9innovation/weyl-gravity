Independent verification

How to read the programme’s claims and evidence.

General

Independent verification checks a result by a route that does not simply repeat the same decisive assumption or calculation. Reproduction instead asks whether the original procedure gives the same answer again.

Example and distinction

Running the same program twice is useful for repeatability, but both runs can share one mistake. A separate derivation or checker can catch different errors. Neither approach eliminates the need to ask whether the right question was encoded.

Physics

An independent check targets failure modes not inherited from the producer, for example by using a distinct reduction or verifying witnesses against original operators. Reproducibility is a separate property.

Use in the model

Agreement between two wrappers around the same hard-coded basis is weak evidence for that basis. A useful audit exposes which mathematical data and implementation assumptions the checking routes still share.

Mathematics

Independence is relative to the claim and possible error, not merely to program names. A verifier should check supplied witnesses or identities without assuming the conclusion it is meant to establish.

Conditions and consequences

Finite independent checks do not convert a written infinite argument into a machine-checked proof. Conversely, a formal proof still depends on the correctness and relevance of its formal statement and trusted foundation.

Specialist

The evidence rail must distinguish reproduction, independent verification and semantic review. Shared content-addressed inputs are acceptable when their role and required checks are explicit.

Project role and claim boundary

Do not claim independent verification by rerunning the producer or importing its expected answer. A timeout, skip or incomplete domain check leaves the relevant obligation unpassed.
