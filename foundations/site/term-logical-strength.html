Logical strength

Mathematical background and its role in the reconstruction problem.

General

Logical strength measures which mathematical assumptions are needed to prove a specified assertion. It does not measure how accurate a physical prediction is or how impressive an equation looks.

Example and distinction

Two tasks can use the same wave but provide different information about its approximations. The task with less information can require stronger existence rules to guarantee the requested answer for every permitted input.

Physics

Logical strength compares existence requirements for a fixed mathematical task. It is distinct from computational complexity, numerical conditioning and empirical adequacy.

Use in the model

The wave benchmark locates a strength difference in amplitude completion, with propagation and detector fixed. A stronger foundational requirement does not imply that a corresponding laboratory procedure is impossible or that the model fits data less well.

Mathematics

Relative strength is established through implications over a stated base. An upper bound supplies sufficient axioms; a reversal supplies a corresponding necessary principle.

Conditions and consequences

Equivalence over a base is stronger evidence than merely proving a theorem in a familiar system. Coding and quantifier choices belong to the assertion being calibrated, so changes of representation can change the result.

Specialist

Strength attaches to a formalized assertion or declared coding, not to a theory name, atlas color or carrier in isolation. The project’s reversal is scoped to WR over RCA₀.

Project role and claim boundary

Do not promote a nonuniform existence result to a uniform operator theorem. Likewise, an analytic estimate and a logical upper bound constrain different objects even when both are described as bounds.
