Spectrum

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

An operator’s spectrum generalizes its possible eigenvalues. In physics it can describe frequencies or energy values associated with a specified operator and state space.

Example and distinction

For a finite matrix, finding eigenvalues may tell the whole spectral story. Infinite systems can also have continuous spectrum. A statement that the spectrum is real does not, by itself, say that probabilities or energy are positive.

Physics

The spectrum consists of values where the operator minus that value times the identity lacks the required inverse. Eigenfrequencies, Hamiltonian spectra and fluctuation-operator spectra are different uses.

Use in the model

Specify the carrier and domain before applying a spectral interpretation. A spectral calculation in a Euclidean or reduced setting does not automatically identify the full Lorentzian physical particle content.

Mathematics

For a closed operator, the resolvent set contains parameters for which the shifted operator has the appropriate bounded inverse; its complement is the spectrum. Point spectrum is only one part.

Conditions and consequences

Unbounded operators require explicit domain conventions. The spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators uses a positive Hilbert structure; it cannot be applied to an arbitrary indefinite pairing by analogy.

Specialist

Spectral reality, residue signs, lower energy bounds and state positivity are separate diagnostics. Retain which operator and realization a calculation addresses.

Project role and claim boundary

The certified centered residual deformation classes do not constitute a one-particle spectrum. A EUCLIDEAN-SPECTRAL or REDUCED-MODE result must not be used to certify the missing full Lorentzian state construction.
