Two-point function

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

A two-point function describes a correlation between two field measurements or insertions. It can reveal how a state relates observations at different places and times.

Example and distinction

For ideal quantum fields it may be a distribution rather than an ordinary function at two points, so one uses smeared measurements. An arbitrary correlation formula does not automatically define a valid state.

Physics

A two-point distribution encodes the expectation of two field insertions in a specified state or candidate state. Its antisymmetric part is tied to the commutator, while its symmetric part carries additional state information.

Use in the model

Positivity, field equations, regularity and gauge compatibility impose separate constraints. For Gaussian or quasifree constructions, two-point data can determine higher correlations under additional conventions; this is not true for every state.

Mathematics

The object acts on pairs of test functions or on an appropriate product test space. A state construction requires positive-type conditions, the field relations and suitable continuity.

Conditions and consequences

Having the right antisymmetric part is insufficient. Products, restrictions and pullbacks of distributional kernels need their own analytic hypotheses; formal pointwise manipulations can fail.

Specialist

The reduced obstruction considers candidates with a fixed commutator and stated Hadamard wavefront control, and tests whether positivity can be repaired smoothly. It does not supply the full metric two-point construction.

Project role and claim boundary

The compact-source lift and failed projection identity remain separate findings. A support-preserving block does not resolve the full action-normalized all-energy commutator comparison.
