Support and compact support

Mathematical background and its role in the reconstruction problem.

General

The support of a function is the region where it can be nonzero, including limiting boundary points. Compact support means this region lies in a compact part of its domain.

Example and distinction

A detector profile can vanish outside a bounded region of ordinary space. A finite list of Fourier modes need not be localized that way: each mode may extend across the whole spatial domain. Two uses of “finite” are therefore different.

Physics

Support controls where data, tests or responses can occur. Compact support of a source and causal support of its response impose distinct geometric restrictions.

Use in the model

Finite mode truncation does not imply spatial localization. On a compact spatial domain, compactness alone also does not express confinement to a small region. The actual domain and support inclusion must be stated.

Mathematics

For a function, support is the closure of its nonzero set; for a distribution it is defined through where it vanishes on tests. Compact support means this closed support is compact.

Conditions and consequences

Support is a topological notion, whereas finite support of a sequence concerns its nonzero indices. Passing between coefficient and spacetime descriptions requires an argument; their supports cannot simply be identified.

Specialist

Support preservation is a separate interface obligation in the ladder and causal construction. Exact finite residuals and nested Galerkin spaces do not establish it.

Project role and claim boundary

A Green operator needs the appropriate advanced or retarded support property in the declared geometry. Do not infer that property from a formal inverse, a mode expansion or an algebraic chain map.
