Observable

General concept with distinct classical, quantum and wave-example realizations.

General

A quantity a model connects to a possible measurement, such as position, energy, or an average wave amplitude. The observable tells us what we are asking about; the state supplies the information used to predict it.

How to think about it

A description of a wave contains much more information than one detector reports. To predict that report, the model needs a rule that turns the wave description into a number. That rule is the observable. Choosing a different rule asks a different measurement question.

Example here

The wave example averages the wave over space, giving some locations more weight than others. Small errors in the wave data then produce controlled errors in this average. The project asks what information is needed to obtain a reliable limiting prediction.

What it does not tell you

Predicting this average does not reconstruct the entire wave, and the mathematical averaging rule is not itself a calibrated laboratory instrument.

From a number to a measurement claim

A number extracted from a formula becomes a proposed observable through a stated connection to a measurement. A detector profile is one part of that connection; calibration and noise assumptions are further parts.

Physics

A specified quantity evaluated on a classical state, or represented by an appropriate operator or algebra element in quantum theory. Its mathematical definition and domain must be fixed before discussing predictions.

Classical and quantum uses

Classically an observable is often a function on phase space. Quantum mechanically a state supplies expectation values for observables; self-adjoint operators model real-valued sharp quantities, with domain conditions for unbounded operators. A specified expectation value is not an individual measurement outcome.

The wave calculation

For chiral data p=(a,b), the fixed detector evaluates Oₚ(t)=∫₀¹h(x)[a(x−t)+b(x+t)]dx. The tent weight h is fixed rather than optimized with the input. The energy-norm bound controls the propagated error uniformly in time.

Why the distinction matters

The reconstruction theorem concerns this functional and its coding. It does not by itself supply detector calibration, statistical noise modeling, or observables for the full metric BV theory.

From a number to a measurement claim

The fixed smeared detector defines the wave benchmark’s output. This does not provide a complete operational identification for every observable in the full gravity theory.

Mathematics

A map extracting a quantity from a state, with the required structure depending on the model. In the wave example it is a bounded linear functional; quantum observables instead belong to an operator or algebraic framework.

Mathematical structure

At each fixed time, Oₚ(t) is linear in the pair p. Its continuity is quantified by |Oₚ(t)−Oₚ′(t)|≤d(p,p′), where d is the stated L² product metric. This estimate lets finite input approximations control the output.

What must be represented

WR requests a fast sequence of rational polygonal functions representing the time-dependent observable. Existence of an abstract value and existence of an output in this representation are different statements over a weak base.

Scope

A bounded linear functional is the construction used here, not the definition of every observable in mathematics or physics. No full state reconstruction follows from this single functional.

From a number to a measurement claim

An observable may be a scalar functional in a classical example or an element of a quantum algebra. Its representation, domain and continuity requirements depend on which object is meant.

Specialist

The observable notion is carrier-dependent. The WR benchmark fixes a bounded linear detector functional on chiral energy data; it makes no identification with the full gauge-invariant Weyl observable algebra.

Exact role in WR

The detector and translated chiral data are fixed by the reconstruction source. The uniform operator bound factors error control through the input metric; interpolation produces the prescribed fast polygonal output.

Lower-bound mechanism

On the fixed profile p(q)=(qg,0), evaluation at time zero recovers a nonzero rational multiple of q. Thus the reversal can encode scalar completion without using propagation or an unbounded family of detector modes.

Claim boundary

Do not infer gauge invariance, a BRST observable algebra, a measurement model, or the accepted full-complex transfer from this reduced functional. Each requires its own construction.

From a number to a measurement claim

The WR comparison preserves one observable across the two input representations. It does not certify a local algebra, a positive state on that algebra or renormalized products of its observables.
