Perturbation and linearization

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

A perturbation is a change from a chosen reference situation. Linearization keeps only the part proportional to a small change, giving a simpler first approximation.

Example and distinction

A straight line can approximate a curved graph near one point, but it need not describe the whole curve. Similarly, a solution of the linearized equations may fail to extend to a solution of the full nonlinear equations.

Physics

Perturbation theory expands about a background, while linearization retains first-order field equations or variations. The expansion parameter and admissible background are part of the setup.

Use in the model

Linear modes help identify candidate directions and propagators. Their gauge status, nonlinear continuation and quantum positivity remain separate questions; a positive free block does not decide the interacting theory.

Mathematics

Linearization is the derivative of an equation or map at a chosen background, on specified domain and target spaces. A kernel vector gives a formal infinitesimal solution direction.

Conditions and consequences

Integrability of that direction into a nearby family of exact solutions needs additional conditions. Higher-order obstructions, regularity and constraints cannot be inferred from the linear kernel alone.

Specialist

The project distinguishes local directions, gauge reduction and nonlinear continuation before physical interpretation. Keep the expansion order and background in the certificate’s scope.

Project role and claim boundary

A surviving linear or residual direction is not automatically a one-particle graviton. Finite exact perturbative checks also do not certify the full repaired complex or renormalized Lorentzian interactions.
