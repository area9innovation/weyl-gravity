Inner product and pairing

Mathematical background and its role in the reconstruction problem.

General

A pairing turns two mathematical objects into a number. An inner product is a special pairing with positivity properties that support a notion of length and angle.

Example and distinction

The familiar dot product of arrows is an example. Other pairings can return negative values for an object paired with itself, or pair different types of objects. Those may be useful, but cannot automatically be read as probabilities.

Physics

A pairing can couple fields, dual variables or graded objects. A positive inner product provides the Hilbert-space geometry used for ordinary quantum probabilities.

Use in the model

The full gauge description can carry an indefinite or graded pairing while a physical-state construction requires its own positivity test. Preservation of a differential does not guarantee preservation of that pairing.

Mathematics

An inner product is a positive-definite symmetric bilinear form over the reals, or Hermitian sesquilinear form over the complexes. A general pairing may be degenerate, indefinite or between distinct spaces.

Conditions and consequences

The convention for which complex argument is linear should be fixed. Graded cyclic pairings introduce further signs and degree conditions; these do not follow from the ungraded inner-product axioms.

Specialist

Pairing compatibility is an independent import and transfer obligation. Nilpotency and chain-map identities alone do not certify cyclicity, nondegeneracy or positive state interpretation.

Project role and claim boundary

Keep the classical graded pairing distinct from a physical positive inner product. A residual vertex class and its pairing data are not thereby one-particle states with probabilistic norms.
