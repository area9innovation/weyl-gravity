A wave and a detector

Imagine a wave travelling around a circular track. Our mathematical detector returns a weighted average of the wave over space, rather than its height at one point. The example asks whether increasingly accurate descriptions of the wave determine a well-controlled detector reading.

This is an idealized scalar wave model, not a calibrated instrument or a model of gravitational-wave detection.

PhysicsUniversity physics; no mathematical logic assumed.

The observable

Take mean-zero left- and right-moving scalar profiles on the unit circle. A fixed periodic tent function h defines Oₚ(t)=∫h(x)[a(x−t)+b(x+t)]dx. Translation supplies the dynamics; spatial smearing supplies a bounded observable.

The model is deliberately simple enough that source representation can be isolated from dynamical complexity.

Objects and representations

Let D consist of rational periodic step pairs p=(a,b), each component of mean zero. Use the squared distance d(p,p′)²=∫[(a−a′)²+(b−b′)²]. A fixed tent function defines a bounded linear observable of translated profiles.

The input is a coded sequence in the unit ball of D satisfying the ordinary Cauchy condition. The output is a fast uniform Cauchy sequence of rational periodic polygons representing the limiting observable.

What information are we given?

Compare two descriptions. The first says that the approximations will eventually settle down. The second also gives an error schedule: for any requested accuracy, it tells us how far to go.

The second description contains extra information. Watching a finite number of approximations agree cannot, by itself, provide that guarantee about every later approximation.

The output is a continuous observable represented by polygonal approximations with a prescribed error rate. A completed input state would already provide missing information and would change the question.

Locality and spatial localization

General Locality concerns how a law relates nearby places or events. Spatial localization concerns where a particular object is concentrated or can be nonzero. They are related questions, but not the same property.

Physics Locality may refer to local differential dynamics or, in a quantum setting, specified compatibility at spacelike separation. Localization refers to support or concentration of data, fields or states.

Mathematics Spatial support is a property of a function or distribution. Locality of an operator typically restricts dependence on its input near a point; quantum locality is formulated through algebraic relations and causal regions.

Specialist Keep local BRST cohomology, localized configurations and Lorentzian causal products distinct. The adjective local does not identify their result types.

Initial-value problem

General An initial-value problem asks how a system evolves from specified starting information. The equation supplies a rule of change, while the initial data select the situation being evolved.

Physics An initial-value problem combines evolution equations, a starting hypersurface and admissible data, with any constraints imposed by the model. Different equation orders require different data structures.

Mathematics An initial-value formulation specifies an operator, data traces and a solution space. A well-posedness statement usually includes existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence in declared topologies.

Specialist The programme’s hyperbolicity no-go results concern specified witness architectures and parameter families. They do not rule out every possible formulation of the classical initial-value problem.

Lorentzian and Euclidean signature

General Lorentzian geometry distinguishes time directions from space directions and supports light cones. Euclidean geometry uses a positive metric with no corresponding distinction. Calculations in the two settings answer different questions.

Physics Lorentzian signature is indefinite with one time direction in the usual spacetime setting; Euclidean signature is positive definite. Their evolution, spectral and state constructions use different analytic structures.

Mathematics Signature records the numbers of positive and negative directions of a nondegenerate symmetric form. Lorentzian and positive-definite metrics induce different operator and geometric problems.

Specialist EUCLIDEAN-SPECTRAL and LORENTZIAN-CAUSAL mark different dependency classes. Neither label is a confidence score, and the former cannot certify the latter’s obligations.

Symmetry

General A symmetry is a transformation that preserves some specified feature of a model, such as its equations or predictions. The feature being preserved must be named.

Physics A symmetry acts on fields or states while preserving the declared dynamics or observables. Global symmetries, spacetime symmetries and gauge redundancies need different treatment.

Mathematics A symmetry can be described by a group action or other transformation structure preserving specified data. Preservation of a set, equation, pairing or measure are distinct conditions.

Specialist Symmetry assumptions belong among the physical premises, while their encoded differential identities are mathematical obligations. Keep classical invariance, local anomaly classification and quantum identity restoration separate.

Gauge symmetry

General Gauge symmetry describes different mathematical descriptions of the same physical situation. It tells us that some changes in the variables should not count as changes in what is physically observed.

Physics Gauge symmetry organizes redundancies of field descriptions. Gauge fixing, constraints and BRST methods address that redundancy, but their precise admissible spaces matter.

Mathematics Gauge reduction identifies configurations under a specified equivalence, often encoded infinitesimally by a complex. The quotient and any cohomological replacement depend on the actual maps and domains.

Specialist The programme separates classical directions, gauge reduction, nonlinear continuation and positive quantum interpretation. None of these stages may implicitly stand for the others.

Action and Lagrangian

General An action assigns a number to a possible history of a system. A Lagrangian supplies the local ingredients used to build that number. Requiring the action not to change to first order under allowed variations produces equations of motion.

Physics An action functional is usually an integral of a Lagrangian density, together with any boundary terms. Its stationary variations produce Euler–Lagrange equations under stated boundary conditions.

Mathematics A Lagrangian density depends on fields and specified derivatives; integration gives a functional whose first variation defines the equations and boundary contributions. The function space and variation class matter.

Specialist The pure-Weyl action is the starting point of a multi-stage construction, not its completion. Keep local variational identities, the encoded classical complex and quantum counterterm classification in their own result kinds.

Weyl gravity

General Weyl gravity is a proposed theory of gravity built from a part of spacetime curvature called the Weyl tensor. Its four-dimensional pure form has a local rescaling symmetry and equations involving higher derivatives.

Physics Four-dimensional pure Weyl gravity uses a Weyl-curvature-squared action, with conformal gauge symmetry and fourth-order metric equations. It is a specific realization within the wider discussion of conformal gravity.

Mathematics The model is a metric variational theory based on a curvature-squared functional. Its gauge and differential structures require specifying the dimension, allowed metrics, boundary conditions and treatment of conformal transformations.

Specialist Pure-Weyl is the programme’s originating model; it is not an umbrella certificate for its classical, local quantum, reduced-mode and empirical results. Those dependencies remain separately tagged.

Perturbation and linearization

General A perturbation is a change from a chosen reference situation. Linearization keeps only the part proportional to a small change, giving a simpler first approximation.

Physics Perturbation theory expands about a background, while linearization retains first-order field equations or variations. The expansion parameter and admissible background are part of the setup.

Mathematics Linearization is the derivative of an equation or map at a chosen background, on specified domain and target spaces. A kernel vector gives a formal infinitesimal solution direction.

Specialist The project distinguishes local directions, gauge reduction and nonlinear continuation before physical interpretation. Keep the expansion order and background in the certificate’s scope.

Free and interacting theory

General A free theory describes systems without the interactions under study; an interacting theory includes couplings that let components affect one another. Solving the free problem is often a starting point.

Physics A free theory is typically governed by a quadratic action, while interactions introduce higher-order couplings. The split depends on the chosen background and perturbative setup.

Mathematics The free/interacting distinction separates a linear or quadratic reference problem from nonlinear terms. Formal power-series solutions and convergent solutions are different mathematical claims.

Specialist Classify local counterterms and anomalies before computing coefficients, restore the local quantum master equation before residual transfer, and keep the Lorentzian gate independent.

Quantum field theory

General Quantum field theory, abbreviated QFT, combines fields with quantum rules for states, observables and probabilities. It aims to describe what measurements of fields can yield, not just a classical wave evolving in time.

Physics Quantum field theory specifies field observables, states and quantum dynamics, with further locality and covariance requirements depending on the framework. A Lagrangian is an input rather than a complete construction.

Mathematics A quantum field theory needs mathematical objects representing observables and states together with dynamics and the intended spacetime structure. Hilbert-space, algebraic and perturbative formulations organize those objects differently.

Specialist The programme has local quantum and reduced calculations, not a certified complete Lorentzian quantum theory of the full metric complex. Dependency tags constrain the use of each result.

Quantization

General Quantization is the attempt to build a quantum description from classical starting data. It adds rules for observables, states and their probabilities rather than merely making numbers more precise.

Physics Quantization constructs quantum observables and states from a classical model under specified conventions. Gauge symmetry, operator ordering, representations and interactions can introduce additional requirements.

Mathematics Quantization can refer to several constructions, such as an operator representation, deformation of an observable algebra or a perturbative assignment. The chosen target category and correspondence conditions must be stated.

Particle and one-particle state

General A particle is a physical interpretation within a theory, not simply any mathematical solution. A one-particle state represents a single excitation in a setting where that distinction is meaningful. A graviton is a proposed quantum excitation of gravity.

Physics A one-particle sector is defined relative to suitable dynamics, state and representation data. On general curved backgrounds there need not be a unique preferred particle interpretation.

Mathematics A particle interpretation often uses a Hilbert representation and a decomposition of excitations relative to a reference state or symmetry. It is not an intrinsic meaning of an arbitrary cohomology group.

Ghost

General Ghost is an overloaded physics word. It can mean an auxiliary field used to account for redundant descriptions, or a problematic mode associated with signs that obstruct a physical interpretation.

Physics BRST ghosts—named within the Becchi–Rouet–Stora–Tyutin formalism—encode gauge redundancy and are not ordinarily interpreted as physical external particles. Higher-derivative ghost terminology can instead concern energy or norm signs.

Mathematics Ghost variables belong to a graded gauge construction with specified degree and statistics. This algebraic role is distinct from an indefinite bilinear form or an unstable direction of an energy functional.

Specialist Use the term with an explicit sense and result type. The trace/ghost repair concerns diagnosed components of a serialized complex, not a proof that all problematic physical modes have disappeared.

Positivity of a state

General Positivity of a quantum state is the condition that quantities representing squared measurement amplitudes receive nonnegative values. It is part of making probabilities sensible.

Physics On a unital star algebra, a state is normalized and positive: its value on A* A is nonnegative for every admissible A. In represented settings this supports the usual nonnegative probability interpretation.

Mathematics Positivity of a linear functional ω means ω(A* A)≥0; normalization is ω(1)=1 in the unital setting. The star operation, algebra and domain are part of the definition.

Born rule

General The Born rule is the quantum rule connecting a state to probabilities of measurement outcomes. In a simple state-vector description, probabilities come from squared amplitudes.

Physics For a normalized state and a projective measurement, the Born rule assigns an outcome probability from the corresponding projection’s expectation value. More general measurements use positive effects summing to the identity.

Mathematics In a Hilbert representation, a normalized vector ψ assigns a projection E the value ⟨ψ,Eψ⟩. A density operator ρ gives Tr(ρE); algebraic states evaluate admissible measurement effects.

Specialist The probability obligation is distinct from spectral reality, gauge-complex consistency and local quantum identity restoration. A residual class does not already define a Born-rule state.

Unitarity

General Unitary evolution preserves the inner-product structure used to compare quantum state vectors. In the usual positive Hilbert setting, this preserves their normalization and the total probability.

Physics A unitary operator U on a Hilbert space satisfies U*U=UU*=1. Unitary dynamics or a unitary scattering operator must be established on the declared physical space.

Mathematics Unitarity is an inner-product-preserving surjective linear isomorphism in the complex Hilbert setting. For continuous time evolution, generator and domain conditions enter the construction of a unitary group.

Specialist The programme treats unitarity as a physical obligation independent of local BRST identities, finite positive blocks and Euclidean spectral calculations. Specify whether the claim concerns evolution or scattering.

Cutoff and truncation

General A cutoff limits which parts of a model are included. A truncation keeps a finite or otherwise restricted part, such as only the first several wave modes.

Physics A cutoff restricts momenta, modes, volume or another declared feature. Its removal requires appropriate convergence, compatibility and sometimes renormalization conditions.

Continuum limit

General A continuum limit is a proposed passage from discrete or truncated descriptions to a model with continuously varying space, time or unrestricted field data. It requires a rule saying how the approximations approach the intended object.

Physics A continuum limit removes a discretization or regulator in a declared sense, often while adjusting parameters or controlling observables. Mode completion and lattice-spacing limits are related examples with different details.

Mathematics A limiting construction requires objects, comparison maps and a convergence notion or universal property. Completing a normed space and taking a regulator-removal limit are not identical operations.
