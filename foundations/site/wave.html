A wave and a detector

Imagine a wave travelling around a circular track. Our mathematical detector returns a weighted average of the wave over space, rather than its height at one point. The example asks whether increasingly accurate descriptions of the wave determine a well-controlled detector reading.

This is an idealized scalar wave model, not a calibrated instrument or a model of gravitational-wave detection.

PhysicsUniversity physics; no mathematical logic assumed.

The observable

Take mean-zero left- and right-moving scalar profiles on the unit circle. A fixed periodic tent function h defines Oₚ(t)=∫h(x)[a(x−t)+b(x+t)]dx. Translation supplies the dynamics; spatial smearing supplies a bounded observable.

The model is deliberately simple enough that source representation can be isolated from dynamical complexity.

Objects and representations

Let D consist of rational periodic step pairs p=(a,b), each component of mean zero. Use the squared distance d(p,p′)²=∫[(a−a′)²+(b−b′)²]. A fixed tent function defines a bounded linear observable of translated profiles.

The input is a coded sequence in the unit ball of D satisfying the ordinary Cauchy condition. The output is a fast uniform Cauchy sequence of rational periodic polygons representing the limiting observable.

Prerequisites: second-order arithmetic and represented analysis. The scalar completeness reversal is standard; the contribution is its realization in this fixed observable together with the matched rate-supplied formulation.

What information are we given?

Compare two descriptions. The first says that the approximations will eventually settle down. The second also gives an error schedule: for any requested accuracy, it tells us how far to go.

The second description contains extra information. Watching a finite number of approximations agree cannot, by itself, provide that guarantee about every later approximation.

Two source contracts

Both formulations give bounded rational step-pair approximants satisfying a Cauchy promise in the energy metric. One also supplies a function choosing the index needed for each tolerance. The rate-free formulation supplies no completed state or convergence oracle.

The output is a continuous observable represented by polygonal approximations with a prescribed error rate. A completed input state would already provide missing information and would change the question.

The missing function

The Cauchy promise has the form ∀k ∃N ∀m,n≥N: d(pₘ,pₙ)²≤2⁻²ᵏ. A modulus supplies a function μ(k) witnessing the existential indices. It is not part of the rate-free input.

RCA₀ supports the coded rate-supplied construction. ACA₀ adds arithmetic comprehension: existence of sets specified by arithmetic formulas. The question is what is needed to obtain the output when only the promise is given.

Input/output contract

Inputs provide the ordinary Cauchy promise in the squared L² pair metric, without a modulus, completed energy-state name or oracle. Outputs are polygonal names satisfying ‖Aₗ−Aₖ‖∞≤2⁻ᵏ for l≥k, whose limit is the ordinary uniform limit of the input observables.

WR is a second-order ∀input ∃output assertion. Rational-time evaluation and finite polygon comparisons express the required conditions without assuming a function space as a set of all points or a higher-type choice functional.

What follows?

With the error schedule supplied, the model lets us construct an approximation to the detector reading with controlled error. Without it, the assertion that an appropriate completed reading always exists uses a stronger set of mathematical assumptions in the formulation studied here.

The surprise is where the extra difficulty sits: it is already present in finding a limiting amplitude before the wave moves. Complicated dynamics are not responsible.

The comparison

With a supplied rate, observable reconstruction is provable over RCA₀. The rate-free existence principle is equivalent over that base to ACA₀, arithmetic comprehension. This is a logical classification of the represented reconstruction problem.

The reversal needs only a fixed spatial profile and the detector at t=0. It therefore does not attribute the stronger requirement to propagation, infinitely many excited modes or chaotic dynamics.

The theorem and its mechanism

Writing WR for the specified rate-free principle, the comparison is RCA₀ ⊢ WRμ and RCA₀ ⊢ (WR ↔ ACA₀). The quantifier is “for every input there exists an output”; no higher-type uniform selector is asserted.

The upper bound extracts suitable indices with arithmetic comprehension. For the reverse implication, a fixed profile transfers scalar limiting-amplitude information to one detector value at time zero. The familiar completeness phenomenon is realized in an explicit wave-observable representation.

Proof mechanism

Uniform boundedness of the observable controls passage from data to output. Arithmetic comprehension supplies modulus information for the upper bound. The reverse reduction is already supported on one fixed spatial profile and a fixed tent detector at t=0.

Thus the lower bound is an amplitude-completion effect, not a propagation obstruction. Read the linked proof for the exact coding and both implications; finite witness checks do not formalize the infinite theorem.

Why this matters—and its limits

A prediction depends on how its inputs are specified, not only on its equation. This example makes one hidden assumption visible: whether convergence information is part of the data.

It does not show that a physical detector needs stronger logic, nor that every prediction from a wave equation is difficult. It is a comparison of two carefully defined mathematical tasks.

Physical interpretation

The useful lesson is to distinguish finite numerical data, an error-controlled state description, and a bare existence claim. The theorem classifies the last-to-output step for one input contract.

No calibration, stochastic noise model, empirical comparison or full gravitational-field reconstruction is established. The finite detector-recovery benchmark addresses a different question: prediction intervals from specified measurements and an energy bound.

Scope of verification

Finite rational inequalities and the reduction witnesses have independent computational checks. The infinite reverse-mathematical argument is a human proof, not a proof-assistant-checked theorem.

Neither RCA₀ as a representation-independent weakest base for wave physics nor a general requirement for ACA₀ in physical measurement follows. The exact coding is part of the statement.

Terms used in this account

ACA₀

General ACA₀ extends the starting rulebook RCA₀ with arithmetic comprehension: permission to collect numbers satisfying an arithmetic condition into a set, even when no general decision procedure is supplied.

Physics ACA₀ adds arithmetic comprehension to the base theory. In this wave problem it supplies missing tail-selection information needed to turn a rate-free Cauchy input into the required named limit.

Mathematics The extension of RCA₀ by comprehension for arithmetical formulas: number quantifiers are allowed, with set parameters, but no bound set variables. The wave statement WR is equivalent to this extension over RCA₀.

Specialist The exact strength of WR over RCA₀ for the declared rate-free representation. The upper bound selects Cauchy-tail witnesses; the lower bound recovers range existence through one scalar amplitude at t=0.

Carrier

General The kind of mathematical structure in which a model is built. A finite collection of matrices, a space of waves, and an algebra of quantum quantities are different carriers, with different questions that must be checked.

Physics The specified mathematical realization carrying the model’s variables, operations and states: for example finite matrices, a completed function space or a local algebraic construction. Changing carrier may change which analytic obligations are meaningful.

Mathematics The chosen structured ambient object, including any topology, pairing, operator domains and completion data needed for a result. The matrix uses carrier as an organizational dimension rather than as one universally defined category.

Specialist The typed support of a construction in the atlas. Cross-carrier transfer requires a declared interface preserving the relevant operations and hypotheses; matching labels or finite fixtures do not establish equivalence.

Convergence rate (modulus)

General An error schedule: for each requested accuracy, it tells you how far along an approximation sequence you must go so that all later approximations agree to that accuracy.

Physics A tolerance-to-index function controlling all later differences between input approximants in a specified norm. Supplying this function is stronger input information than promising that the sequence eventually settles.

Mathematics A function μ such that m,n≥μ(k) implies d(pₘ,pₙ)≤2⁻ᵏ. The Cauchy statement asserts existence of a suitable index for each k; a supplied modulus gives those indices as one input function.

Specialist An input function witnessing the ordinary-Cauchy promise in the exact second-order representation. WR omits it; WRμ includes it. This changes the completion obligation while preserving the detector and dynamics.

Observable

General A quantity a model connects to a possible measurement, such as position, energy, or an average wave amplitude. The observable tells us what we are asking about; the state supplies the information used to predict it.

Physics A specified quantity evaluated on a classical state, or represented by an appropriate operator or algebra element in quantum theory. Its mathematical definition and domain must be fixed before discussing predictions.

Mathematics A map extracting a quantity from a state, with the required structure depending on the model. In the wave example it is a bounded linear functional; quantum observables instead belong to an operator or algebraic framework.

Specialist The observable notion is carrier-dependent. The WR benchmark fixes a bounded linear detector functional on chiral energy data; it makes no identification with the full gauge-invariant Weyl observable algebra.

RCA₀

General RCA₀ is the modest mathematical rulebook used as the starting point for this comparison. Its name comes from Recursive Comprehension Axiom. It lets the project distinguish what can already be proved from what needs an additional existence assumption.

Physics RCA₀ is a weak base theory for reasoning about coded numbers, sequences and countable mathematical objects. It is the bookkeeping framework for the wave comparison, not an extra law governing the wave.

Mathematics A subsystem of second-order arithmetic with elementary arithmetic, Σ⁰₁ induction and Δ⁰₁ comprehension, allowing set parameters. It serves as the base over which the stated reconstruction equivalence is proved.

Specialist The base for WR↔ACA₀ and WRμ. The input/output coding and all reductions must be available over RCA₀; importing an already completed input would invalidate the stated comparison.

Reverse mathematics

General A way of asking which mathematical assumptions a theorem really needs. First show that certain assumptions prove the theorem; then reason backwards from the theorem to see whether it recovers those assumptions.

Physics An analysis of the axiom strength of a mathematical assertion after its inputs, outputs and representations have been fixed. It can separate the cost of taking limits from the physical evolution being modeled.

Mathematics The study of implications and reversals over a specified base theory. An equivalence B⊢(T↔S) calibrates T against the additional principles S, with B held fixed.

Specialist An equivalence or implication analysis whose base, representation and quantifier form are part of the theorem. Here the focal comparison is RCA₀⊢WRμ versus RCA₀⊢(WR↔ACA₀).

State

General The mathematical information a model uses to make predictions about a system. What counts as a state depends on the model: a moving particle may need position and velocity, while a quantum model uses a different kind of description.

Physics A classical phase-space point or field initial datum, or a quantum density operator or normalized positive functional on an observable algebra. The carrier and positivity requirements must be stated; a cohomology class is not automatically a physical state.

Mathematics A model-dependent object on which predictions depend. Examples include a phase-space point and a normalized positive linear functional ω on a unital *-algebra, satisfying ω(A* A)≥0 and ω(1)=1.

Specialist Keep classical data, algebraic positive states, BRST state cohomology, and residual deformation classes separate. The existence or computation of one does not establish the others or an accepted full Lorentzian state construction.
