No-go theorem and obstruction

How to read the programme’s claims and evidence.

General

A no-go theorem proves that a specified goal cannot be achieved under stated conditions. An obstruction identifies something preventing a proposed construction from working.

Example and distinction

If one bridge design cannot span a river, that does not prove no bridge can. Likewise, a failed method can teach us which assumption or architecture must change without disproving every version of a physical theory.

Physics

A scoped no-go result excludes a defined class of constructions. Its architecture, parameter range, background and target requirements determine how widely the exclusion applies.

Use in the model

The programme’s hyperbolicity witnesses exclude tested families, while the reduced positivity obstruction excludes a particular smooth full repair. Neither is a universal rejection of the classical gauge complex or all physical-state constructions.

Mathematics

An obstruction may be a nonvanishing class, incompatible identity or counterexample to a required estimate. A no-go conclusion requires showing that every candidate in the stated class encounters the obstruction.

Conditions and consequences

Failure of one necessary condition for one encoded object differs from a theorem quantifying over all allowed objects. Negative conclusions must preserve those quantifiers as carefully as positive existence theorems.

Specialist

Retain exact architecture and dependency tags. The 24-field scalar normally-hyperbolic witness and fixed-temporal cyclic 46-parameter first-order family are scoped exclusions.

Project role and claim boundary

The Euclidean stability counterfamily targets a proposed bound, and the current export audit targets a concrete serialized object. Do not merge those failures into an unsupported general claim about spectrum, unitarity or every Lorentzian construction.
