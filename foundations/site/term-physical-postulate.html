Physical postulate

How to read the programme’s claims and evidence.

General

A physical postulate is an assumption about how the modeled world behaves. It can concern interactions, symmetries or what counts as an observable. It is different from a mathematical rule used to reason about that model.

Example and distinction

For example, assuming that influences obey a causal restriction is a physical choice. A theorem may show what follows from that choice, but the theorem alone does not show that the choice matches nature.

Physics

Physical postulates specify modeled properties such as locality, covariance or conformal symmetry. Their encoding in a carrier is another input, alongside the logical principles used in proofs.

Use in the model

The same verbal postulate can admit inequivalent mathematical realizations. State which realization and operational meaning are intended before comparing results or importing a theorem from another carrier.

Mathematics

A physical premise becomes usable in mathematics through an explicit encoding as conditions on objects and maps. The encoding is not supplied by the proposition’s informal physical name.

Conditions and consequences

Proof establishes consequences of those conditions. It does not establish that they capture the intended empirical situation, nor that a different encoding preserves the same observables or existence requirements.

Specialist

Keep physical premises separate from logical axioms, carrier structure and empirical protocols in every obligation claim. Their encoded form and normalization are part of the provenance.

Project role and claim boundary

A formal causal-looking term or symmetry label is insufficient to promote the result’s dependency tag. The corresponding construction and preservation properties must actually be certified at the scope being claimed.
