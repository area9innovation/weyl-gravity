Distribution

Mathematical structures, hypotheses and their role in the atlas.

General

In analysis, a distribution is a generalized function described by how it responds to test functions. It can represent idealized sharply concentrated sources that are not ordinary functions.

Example and distinction

A point source can assign a test function its value at one point. That is different from a probability distribution describing uncertain outcomes. This dictionary uses the analytical meaning here and keeps automatic annotation of the bare word disabled.

Physics

Distributions let fields, sources and correlation functions be smeared against admissible tests even when pointwise values are not defined. The test space and continuity requirement specify the construction.

Use in the model

A two-point distribution is not automatically a positive state, and products of singular distributions may not be defined. Regularity and wavefront conditions are important precisely because formal pointwise manipulations can fail.

Mathematics

A distribution is a continuous linear functional on a chosen test-function space, such as smooth compactly supported functions. Derivatives are defined by duality with the appropriate signs.

Conditions and consequences

Multiplication by a smooth function is well defined, whereas multiplication of two arbitrary distributions is not. Support and wavefront information refine what operations are allowed and which singularities can occur.

Specialist

Distributional carriers introduce analytic obligations beyond finite mode identities: continuity on tests, support, singularity control and compatible operations. A formal series must be shown to define the declared distribution.

Project role and claim boundary

The ladder’s distributional stage and the full metric two-point construction are distinct targets. Neither a scalar weak solution nor a reduced reference alone certifies the latter’s positivity and gauge compatibility.
