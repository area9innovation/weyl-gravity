Time-ordered product

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

A time-ordered product organizes field insertions according to their temporal order. It is used in perturbative descriptions of interactions and responses.

Example and distinction

For ideal fields, bringing insertion points together can create singularities. Defining the product there is part of the mathematical construction; an informal ordering symbol does not supply the missing definition.

Physics

Time-ordered products extend the products used in perturbative interactions while obeying causal factorization and other required normalization conditions. Gauge compatibility adds further identities.

Use in the model

In a Lorentzian theory, their construction is distinct from a Euclidean determinant or a free two-point function. A candidate propagator is an ingredient, not a certificate for all renormalized products.

Mathematics

Singular distributional products need controlled extensions at coincident configurations. The extensions must form a compatible family satisfying locality, symmetry and causal requirements in the chosen framework.

Conditions and consequences

Different allowed extensions encode renormalization freedom. Their existence as formal perturbative data is separate from convergence of an interacting theory or existence of a positive full state.

Specialist

Renormalized Lorentzian time-ordered products for the full metric BV complex remain unestablished absent an explicit certificate. They belong to a LORENTZIAN-CAUSAL construction.

Project role and claim boundary

Local anomaly classification or QME restoration does not by itself supply these products. Nor does the reduced Hadamard reference repair the full-export consistency prerequisite on which the advertised transfer depended.
