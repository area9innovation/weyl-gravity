Wave equation

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

A wave equation relates changes in time to changes across space, describing how a disturbance evolves. Familiar examples model vibrations of strings or the travel of sound.

Example and distinction

The equation alone does not select one wave: starting data and boundary conditions matter. The project uses a deliberately fixed wave and detector to ask how much information is needed to reconstruct a reading from approximations.

Physics

The wave equation is a hyperbolic evolution equation whose precise form depends on the background and field type. Initial data and the chosen solution class determine the problem.

Use in the model

The benchmark fixes the physical evolution while changing whether approximation data include a convergence rate. Exact satisfaction by finite modes does not establish localization, a completed energy carrier or a full gauge-field propagator.

Mathematics

A standard scalar form is ∂²_t u−c²Δu=0. More general geometries use the appropriate wave operator, and weak or distributional solutions require a declared testing convention.

Conditions and consequences

Existence, uniqueness and regularity depend on domains and data spaces. The reverse-mathematical statement concerns a particular coded solution and detector task; it is not a classification of every wave-equation theorem.

Specialist

The finite chiral fixtures give exact wave residuals within their declared arithmetic and mode family. WR and WRμ add a specific observable-reconstruction obligation.

Project role and claim boundary

The reversal already operates at fixed time and profile, locating the additional logical cost in amplitude completion. No full Lorentzian metric BV propagator or causal quantum construction follows from this scalar benchmark.
