Ghost

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

General

Ghost is an overloaded physics word. It can mean an auxiliary field used to account for redundant descriptions, or a problematic mode associated with signs that obstruct a physical interpretation.

Example and distinction

These are not the same diagnosis. Before deciding that a ghost is harmless or fatal, ask which object is meant: a bookkeeping variable, an extra classical solution, an energy-sign problem or a negative-probability concern.

Physics

BRST ghosts—named within the Becchi–Rouet–Stora–Tyutin formalism—encode gauge redundancy and are not ordinarily interpreted as physical external particles. Higher-derivative ghost terminology can instead concern energy or norm signs.

Use in the model

An extra mode must be tested separately for gauge triviality, nonlinear continuation and positive quantum interpretation. A classical extra direction does not automatically prove a negative-probability state.

Mathematics

Ghost variables belong to a graded gauge construction with specified degree and statistics. This algebraic role is distinct from an indefinite bilinear form or an unstable direction of an energy functional.

Conditions and consequences

Cohomological reduction can remove exact directions only after the differential is valid. The physical meaning of surviving classes requires additional structure; the word ghost alone identifies neither the complex nor its interpretation.

Specialist

Use the term with an explicit sense and result type. The trace/ghost repair concerns diagnosed components of a serialized complex, not a proof that all problematic physical modes have disappeared.

Project role and claim boundary

The programme’s four tests—classical direction, gauge reduction, nonlinear continuation and state positivity—must not be collapsed into a single ghost verdict. Full repair and physical-spectrum claims remain separately gated.
