Residual and uncertainty weighting

Physical meaning, analytic conditions and limits of interpretation.

General

An observational residual is the difference between a predicted value and a measured value, using a stated sign convention. Uncertainty weighting changes how strongly each discrepancy contributes to a comparison.

Example and distinction

A small error on a very precise measurement can matter more than a larger error on a noisy one. This use of residual has nothing to do with the residual complex in the project’s homological calculations.

Physics

Weighted residual analysis accounts for a declared noise model. Independent Gaussian errors motivate a sum of squared discrepancies divided by variances; correlated errors require the covariance structure.

Use in the model

The weights must be justified and parameter fitting accounted for. A model can improve an unweighted score while failing an uncertainty-weighted gate, without either score alone identifying the correct physical mechanism.

Mathematics

For residual vector r and a suitable nonsingular covariance matrix C, the quadratic statistic rᵀC⁻¹r measures discrepancies in the covariance geometry. Its distributional interpretation needs statistical assumptions.

Conditions and consequences

Changing C changes the objective and its geometry. This is not cohomological reduction, and a positive quadratic statistic does not imply positivity of a quantum state.

Specialist

Keep data provenance, sign convention, covariance, fitted parameters and acceptance threshold with the empirical receipt. Do not substitute an unweighted curve comparison for the stated weighted protocol.

Project role and claim boundary

The homological residual sector is a different result kind. Automatic annotation of bare residual is avoided so that an empirical discrepancy cannot accidentally inherit the meaning of a reduced cohomology class.
