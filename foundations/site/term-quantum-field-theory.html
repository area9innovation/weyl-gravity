Quantum field theory

Physical meaning, mathematical requirements and limits of interpretation.

Stands for: Quantum field theory

General

Quantum field theory, abbreviated QFT, combines fields with quantum rules for states, observables and probabilities. It aims to describe what measurements of fields can yield, not just a classical wave evolving in time.

Example and distinction

Writing a classical field equation is therefore only one ingredient. A quantum model still needs acceptable states, a probability rule and compatible dynamics, including the treatment of interactions when they are claimed.

Physics

Quantum field theory specifies field observables, states and quantum dynamics, with further locality and covariance requirements depending on the framework. A Lagrangian is an input rather than a complete construction.

Use in the model

Finite mode models can illuminate particular obstructions, but they do not automatically define the continuum theory. Gauge redundancy, state positivity and renormalized interactions may each require their own acceptance tests.

Mathematics

A quantum field theory needs mathematical objects representing observables and states together with dynamics and the intended spacetime structure. Hilbert-space, algebraic and perturbative formulations organize those objects differently.

Conditions and consequences

Existence in one framework or restricted sector does not supply an equivalence with another. The atlas records carriers and obligations precisely to avoid identifying constructions solely because they use the same theory name.

Specialist

The programme has local quantum and reduced calculations, not a certified complete Lorentzian quantum theory of the full metric complex. Dependency tags constrain the use of each result.

Project role and claim boundary

A determinant, beta function, trace anomaly, local BV breaking and physical state are distinct objects. Neither a coefficient nor a residual vertex class can substitute for the missing causal and positive-state constructions.
