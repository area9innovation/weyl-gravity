## Primary-source reconstruction

### Island/QES input

[Almheiri--Mahajan--Maldacena--Zhao,
arXiv:1908.10996v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10996v2) introduces the island
prescription in section 4, equation (15): generalized entropy is extremized and
then minimized over gravitational islands.  The text and figure 12(b) note that
taking a whole closed universe costs no island-boundary area.  The paper also
explicitly separates the semiclassical density matrix used in the rule from an
exact nongravitating density matrix.  It does not itself prove a general
rank-one closed-universe encoding.  That rank-one step is an inference made
explicit in the later observer paper: its section 2.1, equation (2.1), applies
the whole-universe alternative to a reference entangled with a system in the
closed universe and obtains zero reference entropy.

### Path-integral/Gram criterion

[Usatyuk--Wang--Zhao,
arXiv:2402.00098v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00098v3) defines closed-universe
inner products by a Euclidean gravitational path integral in section 1,
equation (1.5).  Cross-connected manifolds give order-one corrections to
apparently orthogonal states, equation (1.6).  Their replica rank expression,
equation (1.7), combined with permutation symmetry
\(\operatorname{Tr}(M^n)=(\operatorname{Tr}M)^n\), gives dimension one in
equation (1.8).  In the exact topological-model distribution, section 3.2,
equation (3.16), a fixed alpha sector has \(M_{ij}=A_iA_j\), manifestly rank
one.  The paper distinguishes this nonperturbative result from the many
semiclassical JT wave functionals, including those with a massive-particle
observer.  The higher-dimensional extension is formal and depends on replica
analytic continuation; it is not a theorem about this four-dimensional BV
complex.

### Fixed-theory factorization

[Usatyuk--Zhao,
arXiv:2403.13047v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13047v2) gives the cleanest
fixed-theory chain.  Section 2.1, equation (2.4), makes every
path-integral-prepared wave function proportional to the Hartle--Hawking wave
function under factorization.  Section 2.2, equations (2.5)-(2.6), assumes the
stronger factorization of finite-slice inner products; the Gram matrix is then
an outer product and has rank one.  Section 2.3 explains why ensemble averaging
breaks this conclusion.  The paper's section 4 explicitly marks
finite-boundary factorization as less clear in general dimensions, although the
deformed-JT realization has an independent boundary definition.  This is a
factorization theorem conditional on quantum/holographic input, not a classical
charge quotient.
