# Closed-universe observer first comparison

## Result

The first exact result is asymmetric.

1. The external fixed-
   \(O\) code fixture exactly supports a rank-one global Gram carrier and a
   rank-two observer-effective carrier.  Global collapse and observer recovery
   are therefore algebraically compatible in that declared architecture.
2. The Berger branch supports a partial classical observer map containing a
   clock slice, an averaged relational Maxwell redshift, and a nonzero retarded
   Maxwell response while raw \(D\) is presymplectically null on the tested
   fixed-coupling tangent sector.
3. The two results do not yet define one common reduction principle.  The
   external fixture acts on quantum code/Hilbert carriers through a
   pointer-basis clone.  The Berger map acts on classical invariant
   functionals through clock slicing and a retarded Green operator.  No typed
   map currently preserves product, pairing, and probability between those
   carriers.

The lifecycle is therefore `EXTERNAL_FIXTURE_REPRODUCED`, not
`CLASSICAL_OBSERVER_MAP_CERTIFIED`.  The overall verdict is
`QUANTUM_COMPARISON_NOT_YET_DEFINED`.  The recommendation is to construct two
localized clock-labelled detector records and then wait for the quantum state,
inner-product, and QME gates.  There is not yet a bridge theorem or a scoped
no-go suitable for a new paper.

Dependency boundary: the fixture is `LOCAL-ALGEBRAIC` and
`EUCLIDEAN-SPECTRAL`; the imported classical response also uses
`REDUCED-MODE` and `LORENTZIAN-CAUSAL`.  None of this establishes a Lorentzian
quantum observer theory.

## Strongest case against conflation

The parallel fails before coefficients or dynamics are compared:

| Feature | External closed-universe collapse | Selected pure-Weyl/ Berger facts |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier | exact/fundamental or effective quantum Hilbert/code carrier | classical one-particle complex, residual deformation cohomology, or covariant observables |
| Reduction | QES minimization, path-integral Gram rank, fixed-theory factorization, or a one-row encoding | Taub-zero restriction, residual quotient, or presymplectic nullity |
| Pairing | quantum inner product or code Gram form | classical residual or symplectic pairing |
| Observer mechanism | pointer-basis decoherence and cloned reference \(Ob'\) | relational phase clock and causal Maxwell evaluation |
| Boundaries | source-dependent: compact slice, Euclidean path integral, or auxiliary nongravitating reference | Lorentzian cylinder with compact \(S^3\) Cauchy slices and no spatial boundary |

Thus factorization is not a Taub-zero restriction, and a one-dimensional
fundamental Hilbert space is not a vanishing one-particle cohomology group.  The
surviving degree-four classes \([W_+^2]\) and \([W_-^2]\) are centered
deformation/vertex classes with pairing \(I_2\), not one-particle states and not
a quantum norm.

The narrow surviving parallel is only this: a small reduced global carrier can
coexist with a richer observer or relational carrier.  That statement is not a
common theorem until the carriers and preserved structures are typed in one
category.

## Primary-source reconstruction

### Island/QES input

[Almheiri--Mahajan--Maldacena--Zhao,
arXiv:1908.10996v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10996v2) introduces the island
prescription in section 4, equation (15): generalized entropy is extremized and
then minimized over gravitational islands.  The text and figure 12(b) note that
taking a whole closed universe costs no island-boundary area.  The paper also
explicitly separates the semiclassical density matrix used in the rule from an
exact nongravitating density matrix.  It does not itself prove a general
rank-one closed-universe encoding.  That rank-one step is an inference made
explicit in the later observer paper: its section 2.1, equation (2.1), applies
the whole-universe alternative to a reference entangled with a system in the
closed universe and obtains zero reference entropy.

### Path-integral/Gram criterion

[Usatyuk--Wang--Zhao,
arXiv:2402.00098v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00098v3) defines closed-universe
inner products by a Euclidean gravitational path integral in section 1,
equation (1.5).  Cross-connected manifolds give order-one corrections to
apparently orthogonal states, equation (1.6).  Their replica rank expression,
equation (1.7), combined with permutation symmetry
\(\operatorname{Tr}(M^n)=(\operatorname{Tr}M)^n\), gives dimension one in
equation (1.8).  In the exact topological-model distribution, section 3.2,
equation (3.16), a fixed alpha sector has \(M_{ij}=A_iA_j\), manifestly rank
one.  The paper distinguishes this nonperturbative result from the many
semiclassical JT wave functionals, including those with a massive-particle
observer.  The higher-dimensional extension is formal and depends on replica
analytic continuation; it is not a theorem about this four-dimensional BV
complex.

### Fixed-theory factorization

[Usatyuk--Zhao,
arXiv:2403.13047v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13047v2) gives the cleanest
fixed-theory chain.  Section 2.1, equation (2.4), makes every
path-integral-prepared wave function proportional to the Hartle--Hawking wave
function under factorization.  Section 2.2, equations (2.5)-(2.6), assumes the
stronger factorization of finite-slice inner products; the Gram matrix is then
an outer product and has rank one.  Section 2.3 explains why ensemble averaging
breaks this conclusion.  The paper's section 4 explicitly marks
finite-boundary factorization as less clear in general dimensions, although the
deformed-JT realization has an independent boundary definition.  This is a
factorization theorem conditional on quantum/holographic input, not a classical
charge quotient.

### Euclidean observer and clock

[Maldacena,
arXiv:2412.14014v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14014v3) studies the
\(D\)-dimensional Euclidean de Sitter sphere, not a Lorentzian compact Cauchy
slice.  Equation (1) records the one-loop phase \(i^{D+2}\).  Adding a massive
particle observer and clock in equations (22)-(26) cancels the
dimension-dependent part.  Equation (25) supplies the clock density, and
equation (27) imposes the observer-local Hamiltonian constraint.  The version-3
main derivation, equations (33)-(37), still gives a negative proposed count; a
note after equation (38) reports that a later order-of-integration prescription
removes it.  Section 5 says that no explicit Hilbert-space realization of the
count is supplied.  This source therefore motivates observer-relative quantum
objects but supplies neither a closed-universe rank fixture nor our relational
clock map.

### Observer/cloned-observer recovery

[Harlow--Usatyuk--Zhao,
arXiv:2501.02359v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02359v2) distinguishes the
one-dimensional fundamental carrier from observer-effective quantum mechanics.
Section 2.1 gives QES, Gram/path-integral, and fixed-CFT arguments.  Equation
(1.1) defines a fixed orthogonal encoding from the effective carrier to a
one-dimensional output; equations (1.2)-(1.3) show that Haar averaging preserves
the mean inner product but a fixed projection cannot preserve every input.
Section 3 assumes an entropic observer, a stable pointer basis, and decoherence;
equation (3.7) clones only that basis to \(Ob'\).  The map can then be read as
\(\widehat V:\mathcal H_M\to\mathcal H_{Ob'}\).  Equations (4.1)-(4.3) suppress
inner-product fluctuations by observer Renyi entropy, and equations (4.4)-(4.6)
bound the observer-effective entanglement capacity.  The external clone is a
nongravitating auxiliary carrier, even though it is interpreted as a copy of
the internal observer's pointer record.  The Berger programme has no quantum
counterpart of this carrier yet.
